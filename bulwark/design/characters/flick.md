# Flick

- **id:** `flick`
- **Class:** Sorcerer (Elemental bloodline)
- **Ancestry:** Goblin
- **Pronouns:** she/her
- **Kind:** recruitable PC
- **Associated building:** None
- **Deity:** None. Flick has never sat still long enough to consider the divine. If a god
  wanted her attention they would need to be on fire, and even then she might just try to
  figure out what kind of fire.

## Vibe

Flick picks things up. This is the core problem. She picks up rocks and they spark. She
picks up sticks and they ignite. She picks up a handful of rusty nails and lightning arcs
between them. She picks up literally anything and elemental force channels through it in
ways that are unpredictable, uncontrolled, and spectacular. She thinks this is amazing.
Everyone around her thinks this is a crisis.

She is not afraid of her power. She is delighted by it. Every misfire is a data point.
Every explosion is an experiment. She grabs something, points it at something else, and
whatever happens next is interesting regardless of whether it was intended. The scorch
marks on her hands and the singed tips of her ears are not warnings to her. They are
evidence of progress.

She does not understand why the tall folk panic when things explode. Goblins work with
dangerous materials constantly. Things blow up. You check if anyone died, you patch what
needs patching, and you try again. The fact that other ancestries treat a small detonation
as an emergency is genuinely funny to her. She laughs about it. She is not being cruel.
She sincerely does not see the problem.

This is the thing about Flick: the recklessness is a misread. She is fearless, and the gap
between her risk tolerance and everyone else's is enormous. What a human considers
catastrophic, a goblin considers Tuesday. Her bloodline did not create the fearlessness; it
gave that fearlessness consequences beyond the goblin norm.

## The Junk Magic

Flick's elemental sorcery channels through physical objects. She does not cast with
gestures and incantations like a trained wizard. She grabs something and the element moves
through it. The object determines the shape of the spell in ways she is still figuring out:
metal conducts lightning, stone erupts with force, wood catches fire, water freezes or
boils. The interaction between material and element is the system she is trying to map,
one explosion at a time.

This means she is always collecting things. Pockets full of junk. Rocks, nails, broken
glass, scraps of cloth, bits of chain, interesting-looking sticks. None of it looks like
spell components. All of it is ammunition. She does not see a difference between a
battlefield and a scrap heap, and she is equally comfortable in both.

The junk magic is primal in tradition. The elemental force flowing through her is raw and
ancient, the same power that drives storms and volcanic eruptions and the grinding of
tectonic plates. It is flowing through a four-foot goblin holding a bent spoon, and it
does not care about the absurdity of that arrangement.

## Role in the town

Flick has no building assignment. She is a combat recruit whose value is raw elemental
damage output. In a party she is a blaster: high damage, wide area, collateral risk. She
is the kind of caster you point at the enemy and hope the cone is aimed the right way.

At the outpost she is a managed hazard. She needs space to practice, and that space needs
to be away from anything flammable, structural, or alive. An open area the outpost
designates as her testing ground could become a recurring visual gag as it accumulates
scorch marks, craters, and inexplicable frozen patches.

As her control improves through her character arc, her combat reliability increases and
her outpost hazard level decreases. Early-game Flick might set the wrong thing on fire
during a fight. Late-game Flick puts the fire exactly where it needs to go and nowhere
else.

## Background

Flick is a goblin from a clan operating in reclaimer tradition, scavenging and repurposing
the refuse of larger civilizations. She was unremarkable among her people until the
bloodline manifested. Elemental sorcery is innate, not learned, and it surfaced in Flick
without warning or precedent. One day she was sorting scrap. The next day the scrap
started exploding.

Her clan is accustomed to dangerous work. Goblin reclaimers handle volatile materials,
unstable structures, and toxic substances as a matter of course. But Flick's magic
escalated beyond what even goblin risk tolerance could absorb. She was not exiled with
malice. Her clan likes her. They told her, with genuine affection, that she needed to go
figure this out somewhere that was not next to their homes. She understood, and she left
without offense.

Left to her own devices in the wilderness, she has been experimenting freely and without
restraint. The results are visible from a distance: scorched clearings, shattered rock
formations, patches of inexplicable frost. She is a one-goblin natural disaster, and she
is having a wonderful time.

## Arrival

Recruitable. The party encounters Flick during an expedition when they stumble into the
aftermath of her experiments, or find her mid-fight where her magic is erupting in every
direction. She is winning, if you define winning as "the enemy is defeated and also
everything else in a fifty-foot radius."

The party brings her back to the outpost not because they want a sorcerer but because they
cannot leave her out there. She is an environmental hazard. Uncontained, she will cause
fires, destabilize terrain, and trigger the kind of elemental chain reactions that
reshape geography. Bringing her to the outpost is an act of containment dressed up as
hospitality.

Flick thinks she has been invited. She is pleased. Free meals, new materials to test,
and people who make funny faces when things explode. Everyone else at the outpost
understands the real reason she is here. Nobody corrects her.

## Heart Level Progression

- **Hearts 0-2:** Uncontained chaos. She experiments freely, causes minor incidents,
  laughs at the reactions. The outpost is managing her more than befriending her. She is
  cheerful and oblivious to the stress she causes.
- **Hearts 3-4:** The player starts showing her that her actions have consequences beyond
  the immediate blast. Not through scolding but through showing her what she almost
  destroyed, who she almost hurt. She does not become afraid. She becomes thoughtful,
  which for Flick is new.
- **Hearts 5-6:** She begins experimenting with intent rather than impulse. Still grabbing
  junk, still channeling wildly, but now she is trying to control the direction and scale.
  The failures are smaller. The successes are more precise. She is frustrated by the
  slowness of progress but committed to it.
- **Hearts 7-8:** Control. Not mastery, but reliable aim. She can point the fire where it
  needs to go and keep it there. She still loves the explosions. She has just learned to
  choose when they happen. This is when she becomes genuinely dangerous in the useful
  sense.
- **Hearts 9-10:** Flick understands what she is. Not just a goblin who picked up the
  wrong rock one day, but someone carrying elemental force that could reshape the land.
  She has chosen to be precise with it. The recklessness is gone but the delight is not.
  She still grins when things catch fire. She just makes sure nothing catches fire that
  should not.
