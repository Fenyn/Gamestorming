# Grub

- **id:** `grub`
- **Class:** Druid
- **Ancestry:** Goblin
- **Pronouns:** he/him
- **Kind:** recruitable PC (mid-late game)
- **Associated building:** Farmhouse / Fields
- **Deity:** None. Grub has a personal, unstructured relationship with the natural world that
  does not map to any recognized tradition. If asked what he worships, he would look confused
  and gesture vaguely at everything around him.

## Vibe

Grub is cheerful, chatty, and completely unaware that anything about the way he lives is
unusual. He sleeps in a nest of moss and roots. He eats things he pulled out of the ground
thirty seconds ago without washing them. His living space is indistinguishable from a
greenhouse that has been abandoned for a decade, and if you point this out he will look around,
fail to identify the problem, and offer you a radish.

He is not performing eccentricity. He is not making a statement. This is simply how goblins
are, as far as he is concerned, and the fact that other people organize their lives differently
is a curiosity he finds mildly interesting but not worth adopting. When the party tries to
explain that most people plant crops in rows, he listens politely, nods, and then continues
doing what he was doing. It is not defiance. The information just does not stick, because it
does not make sense to him, and things that do not make sense slide off Grub like water.

He is social in a way that catches people off guard. He has been living alone for a while but
not because he wanted to. The good soil was here, so he was here. Now that people have shown
up, he is delighted. He has opinions about everything and shares them without prompting. He
asks questions constantly. He wants to know what Fenwick is cooking and whether he can smell
it, and whether Arkus has tried eating the clay near the river because it is excellent clay,
and why Aldric wears all that metal when it must be so hot. None of this is meant to annoy.
Grub is genuinely, boundlessly interested in the people around him, and he has no filter
between curiosity and speech.

## Role in the town

Grub runs the Farmhouse and Fields. His arrival automates and expands the outpost's
agricultural operations. The player has been managing crops manually up to this point, and
Grub takes over with methods that look chaotic and produce results that are undeniably
superior.

His approach to farming does not resemble farming. He does not plant in rows. He does not
weed. He does not follow seasons in any way the party recognizes. He puts things where they
want to go, which means tubers next to legumes next to wildflowers next to something nobody
can identify, and the whole mess produces more food per acre than the player's careful
planning ever did. He cannot explain why it works. "Things grow where they are happy" is the
closest he gets to a philosophy, and he says it the way someone might say "water is wet."

As the Farmhouse upgrades, Grub's managed chaos expands: more crop variety, higher yields,
and eventually greenhouse and specialty cultivation. His building will become overgrown with
plants and ivy to the point where it is difficult to tell where the structure ends and the
garden begins. He claims this is part of the natural order. The walls agree with him, apparently.

## Background

Grub has been living in the wilderness near the outpost for longer than the outpost has been
occupied. He found a patch of land where things grew well and he helped them grow better. Not
through magic, or not only through magic. Through attention. He watches what the soil wants,
where the water goes, which plants grow stronger next to which other plants, and he arranges
things accordingly. He has been doing this long enough that his patch of wilderness is
suspiciously productive: wild-looking but heavy with fruit, dense with edible plants, alive in
a way the surrounding land is not.

He is a goblin, which means most civilizations have never noticed him and would not know what
to make of him if they did. The hegemony does not track goblins. The elves do not concern
themselves with them. Grub has existed in the margins for his entire life, and it has not
occurred to him to be bothered by this. The margins are where the good soil is.

Goblin creation myths say the first goblins sprang from the refuse of civilization. Grub does
not think about this much, but he lives it. He reclaims. He takes what has been discarded or
neglected and makes it produce. The patch of land he tends was overlooked by every power that
has ever claimed this territory. Grub looked at it and saw potential. That is what goblins do.

## Arrival

Recruitable, mid-late game. The party discovers Grub's patch during a territory expansion.
The land is unmistakably tended, but not in any way that looks deliberate to human eyes. As
the party investigates, they find Grub, who is neither hiding nor presenting himself. He is
just there, the way he has always been there, doing what he has always been doing.

He does not need to be convinced to join the outpost. The outpost came to him. He is perfectly
happy to keep doing what he was doing, just with more people around and a building to not
maintain. Specific trigger TBD (territory expansion milestone, day threshold, or farm level).

## Notes (deferred / later)

- Build: Druid + TBD FA line. Wis 18. Likely wild order or untamed order if we define one.
  Primal spellcasting focused on growth, terrain, and plant-related effects.
- Druid is not yet on the planned class list. Needs adding to the README. Primal spellcasting,
  wild shape (if relevant), and order abilities will need implementation.
- Grub is the first goblin in the roster. His presence should feel genuinely exotic to the
  other characters. Most of them have never met a goblin. Some of them were not entirely sure
  goblins were real.
- His automation of the farm should be a meaningful gameplay transition. Before Grub, farming
  is manual and requires daily player attention. After Grub, the farm runs itself with
  occasional player input for expansion decisions and specialty crops. This is the reward for
  reaching mid-late game.
- His relationship with Spore could be interesting. Both work with growing things, both are
  outsiders from rare ancestries, but their approaches are completely different. Spore brews
  and transforms. Grub grows and reclaims. Grandmother Mulch's philosophy of "nothing is
  wasted" aligns perfectly with goblin reclaimer culture, though neither Grub nor Spore would
  articulate that connection.
- His relationship with Fenwick is the most natural pairing: the grower and the cook. Fenwick
  would be fascinated by ingredients he has never seen before, and Grub would be fascinated
  that someone turns raw plants into something unrecognizable and calls it better.
