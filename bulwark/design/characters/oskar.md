# Oskar

- **id:** `oskar`
- **Class:** Oracle
- **Ancestry:** Dwarf
- **Pronouns:** he/him
- **Kind:** recruitable PC
- **Associated building:** Chapel / Shrine
- **Deity:** None chosen. Oskar's divine power was not granted through faith. It was forced
  into him by exposure to a weaponized divine detonation during the wars. His relationship
  with the divine is involuntary and complicated.

## Vibe

Oskar is the oldest person at the outpost and he carries that age gently. He is patient the
way someone is patient when they have accepted that they do not have unlimited time and have
decided not to waste any of it on frustration. He listens more than he speaks. When he does
speak, it matters, not because he is dramatic about it but because he has spent decades
learning which words are worth saying.

He is warm. Genuinely, quietly warm. He remembers names. He asks about people's days and
listens to the answers. He saves a seat by the fire for whoever looks like they need it most.
He is building a chapel not because he was told to but because he has looked at the people
around him and decided that they need a place to rest that is not their bed and not their
workplace. Somewhere to just be.

He does not talk about the curse unless asked, and even then he is matter-of-fact about it.
He has had a long time to grieve for himself and he has finished. What is left is purpose. The
outpost will need a chapel after he is gone. The people in it will need the habits of community
he is building now. Every blessing he gives, every ward he places, every small ritual he
establishes is a deposit into something that will outlast him. Society grows great when old men
plant trees under which they will never sit. Oskar is planting trees.

## Role in the town

Oskar runs the Chapel. He provides divine blessings, maintains spiritual wards, and serves as
the outpost's anchor for matters of faith and meaning. He is not a preacher and does not
deliver sermons or push doctrine. He offers a space and a presence, and people come to him
because they want to, not because they are told to.

As the Chapel upgrades, the strength and variety of blessings he can provide expand. The Chapel
also serves as the outpost's communal gathering point for events that are not about commerce or
combat — funerals, celebrations, quiet moments of collective rest.

In combat, Oskar is a divine caster whose power comes at a cost. His oracle curse intensifies
when he channels divine energy, manifesting as moments of confusion, lost focus, or briefly
forgetting where he is. He is effective but the toll is visible to anyone paying attention.

## Background

Oskar is a dwarf who has lived a long life, even by dwarven standards. He served the hegemony
during the human-elf wars in the way most dwarves served: as infrastructure. Engineers,
builders, fortifiers. Dwarves built the siege works and the supply lines and the walls, and
when the walls came down they were standing behind them.

Oskar was present at a battle where the elven forces deployed what amounted to a divine weapon
of mass destruction. Not arcane. Divine. They channeled something sacred, a force meant for
miracles, and turned it into a weapon. He does not describe the weapon itself in detail. He
describes the light, and then the silence afterward. The blast killed most of the people around
him instantly. Those who survived were changed. Oskar is considered one of the lucky ones.

The detonation flooded him with divine energy he was never meant to hold. Oracle power took
root in him, unbidden and unasked for, the residue of a god's power passing through him on its
way to killing everyone around him. He can channel divine energy. He can heal, bless, and ward.
The cost is that the same signal that grants him these abilities is slowly burning out his mind.
The curse is not a spell that was cast on him. It is damage. A mortal mind was never meant to
receive this much divine signal for this long, and his is being overwritten by something too
large for it.

This also means the elves committed an act of desecration to win a battle. They took something
holy and made it kill. The cold peace between the hegemony and the elven nations is not just
mutual exhaustion. It is mutual shame. Neither side can claim moral high ground.

The decline is mental. It began with small things: a name forgotten, a thought lost mid-
sentence, a moment of confusion about where he was. It has been progressing slowly for years.
Oskar has had time to watch it come, to understand what it means, and to decide what to do with
what remains. He chose to build something. The outpost needed a chapel. He needed a purpose. The
math was simple.

## Arrival

Recruitable. Oskar arrives at the outpost the way old soldiers arrive at quiet places: slowly,
deliberately, with no intention of leaving. He heard about the settlement through the same
networks that carry frontier news, and he recognized it as the kind of place where the last
years of a life could mean something. He does not announce his condition. The party discovers
it over time, through dialogue that gradually reveals the decline — a repeated story, a
forgotten name, a blessing that falters mid-casting. The trigger is the Chapel's construction
itself. There is nothing for Oskar to tend without it, so he only arrives once the outpost has
raised the building he means to spend his last years inside.

## The Curse — Design Notes

- The curse manifests exclusively through dialogue and narrative, not through visual appearance
  changes. No new sprites needed. The player notices through repeated lines, lost thoughts,
  moments of confusion, and increasingly urgent conversations about making sure the chapel
  will function without him.
- The decline follows a timeline. If the player does not intervene, Oskar dies in a late-game
  story event. This is the first and possibly only permanent character death in the game. It
  should carry weight.
- If Oskar dies, the chapel does not close. The systems he built, the rituals, the wards,
  persist. The building functions mechanically but the NPC is gone. The empty chair by the
  fire is the kind of detail that should make the player feel the loss.

## The Ritual — Hidden Quest

The death is preventable through a hidden multi-character quest. The player is never told
directly who can help or how. They must discover it through exploration, friendship, and
intuition.

**Quest trigger:** Reaching 6/10 hearts with Oskar triggers the possibility of encountering
the Psychic character in the world. Meeting the Psychic starts the quest proper. The Psychic
recognizes what is happening to Oskar's mind and suggests that a ritual might help, but that
it would require specialists with specific knowledge of the mind, the body, and the
supernatural.

**Quest flow:** The player is prompted to find specialists who might help but is NOT told who
they are. The player must think about which party members or recruitable characters have
relevant expertise and bring them into the effort. This is a puzzle the player solves through
understanding their own roster.

**The ritual team (0-3 helpers):**
- **Psychic** (auto-included, quest giver) — stabilizes Oskar's mind, holds his consciousness
  together during the extraction
- **Thaumaturge** (discoverable helper) — identifies the divine curse signature and unweaves
  it. Introduced from elsewhere in the world (origin TBD). The player must intuit that the
  thaumaturge's expertise with supernatural phenomena is relevant
- **Josen** (discoverable helper, requires high friendship) — uses Quiet Hand knowledge of
  the body's energy pathways to keep Oskar physically stable during the ritual. The player
  must intuit that a master of the body's internal systems could help guide divine energy
  safely through a living form

**Resolution:** The ritual uses a PF2e-style dice roll. The number of helpers determines the
odds:
- 0 additional helpers (Psychic alone): near-impossible, almost certain failure
- 1 additional helper: difficult but possible
- 2 additional helpers: good odds
- 3 helpers (Psychic + Thaumaturge + Josen): guaranteed success

**Success:** The curse is not fully removed. The team stabilizes Oskar, untangling the divine
echo from his own mind enough that the decline stops. He is diminished but present. He stays
at the chapel. He remembers everyone's name again.

**Failure:** Oskar dies. Same as the untreated outcome but the player carries the additional
weight of having tried and failed.

**Timeline pressure:** The death event has a fixed trigger (day count or story milestone).
The player must find and befriend the helpers before it fires. There is no warning beyond
Oskar's dialogue getting progressively worse.

  (Bastion, Marshal, or even Fighter dedication) makes thematic sense — the old soldier's
  training layered under the divine power that was forced onto him. Cha 18 or Wis 18. Divine
  spellcasting. Oracle mystery and curse mechanic will need implementation.
- Oracle is not on the planned class list. Needs adding to the README.
- Oskar's relationship with Tharr is the natural pairing — two old dwarves, both integrated
  into the hegemony, both veterans of its wars in different ways. Tharr builds things that
  last. Oskar is building things that will outlast him specifically. They understand each other
  in a way the younger characters cannot.
- Oskar's relationship with Aldric: both are veterans of the same wars, same faction. Aldric
  has heard of the battle where the elven weapon was deployed but was not present. Oskar's
  condition is a concrete reminder of what the wars cost, not in guilt but in consequence.
  Aldric's guilt is about what he did. Oskar's curse is about what was done to him. They carry
  different sides of the same weight.

  before the death event. The Psychic's encounter is gated behind 6/10 hearts with Oskar.
  Both characters' stories should stand on their own and not exist solely to service Oskar's
