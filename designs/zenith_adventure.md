# Eidolarch: Adventure Mode

Single-player run mode for `zenith`. Drafted 2026-09-20. Design, plus the parts already built:
hand-authored starters in `data/adventure/starters/`, opponent tiers generated by
`tools/scale_deck.py`, `DeckValidator` accepting both in adventure mode, and the select screen
listing the starters.

A run starts with a stripped version of one of the thirteen precons and rebuilds it. You begin at
40 cards, one Mastery, two Aspects, no Relic, no Grounds, and a thin deck that barely
functions. You duel your way up a ladder of opponents who are further along the same road, taking
one card from three after each win. A finished run is a deck `DeckValidator` would pass in
multiplayer. Legality is the win screen.

**Rules baseline:** unchanged from `zenith.md`. Adventure mode adds no new card rules. Every
reward is an existing system: cards, Aspects, Relics, Reserve slots, Seals, Grounds.

---

## 1. Why the Life Deck carries the mode

The Life Deck is the health total. `DeckValidator` bands it at 50 to 85 cards, 90 for Root.

That one fact supplies the whole roguelite tension without inventing anything, and it runs
opposite to Slay the Spire:

- Adding a card raises effective health and dilutes draws.
- Removing a card sharpens draws and lowers health.

In Slay the Spire removal is almost always correct, which is a known flatness in its economy.
Here it is a real trade every time. The run is built around this axis and no other.

A run's deck climbs from 40 toward roughly 70. More endurance and more dilution on the same
slider. No separate health bar, no healing item, no new engine concept.

## 2. Reference games

| Game | Structure | What was taken |
|---|---|---|
| DBZ CCG (GBA, 2002) | Rank ladder, 12 opponents, random card drops | Difficulty as deck size and Mastery access rather than stats. Refightable opponents. Finish with one character to unlock another |
| Pokémon TCG (GBC) | 8 club leaders, then Grand Masters | Collection grows, deck size does not. Each opponent teaches one counter-strategy |
| Slay the Spire 1 | 3 acts, branching map, relics, card removal | Node-type variety on one map. Relics as the real power curve |
| Slay the Spire 2 | Two act choices per stage, Enchantments | Persistent per-run card modifiers, including ones the opponent inflicts |
| Monster Train | Clan pairing, Covenant ladder | Difficulty ladder after first clear |
| Griftlands | Persistent NPCs | Opponents who return stronger within a run |
| Roguebook | Wound cards jam the deck | Damage that enters the deck as junk |

What the GBA game got right and is worth copying: the first opponent runs a 52-card deck with no
Mastery, the late villains run 74 with one. Difficulty was deck size and Mastery access. The final
boss gets a stated rule exemption printed on screen rather than a stat bump.

What it got wrong and is not worth copying: batches of ten random cards after every win, of which
three or four are useful. Pick one from three instead.

## 3. The starter

### 3.1 How a starter is made

Adventure starters are **separate authored files**. Nothing reads across from `data/decks/` at
runtime, so the precons can be rebalanced for multiplayer without moving a starter.

```
data/decks/                  unchanged, the 13 full precons, multiplayer only
data/adventure/
  starters/<deck_id>.json    40-card starter (Root 45), same DeckList schema
  pools/<deck_id>.json       reward pool and build order
  unlocks.json               earned collection, save data
```

Starters use the existing schema so `DeckList` and `CardLibrary` load them unchanged, plus one
field `source_deck` for provenance.

Starters are **hand-authored**, deck by deck, since 2026-09-20. Each keeps 40 cards (45 for Root),
one or two bombs, stops for both Strikes and Arts, board or hand interaction, and the cards its
own plan needs. The reasoning per deck is in `zenith/docs/adventure_starters.md`. The generated
starters culled by a power score and left several decks without their plan; `tools/scale_deck.py`
now writes only the opponent tiers (section 6).

## 4. Rewards

### 4.1 Pick 3

After every won duel, three cards, pick one.

**Superseded 2026-09-21 by 12.1, which is what is built.** The first slice offered single cards.
Its pool was:

- any card of the deck's school,
- a 17-card Freestyle basic core list,
- signature cards that name the run's own duelist, and no other character's.

Strikes, Arts, Combat, Non-Combat and Drills only. A card's `only` gate has to be met by the deck.
Every candidate is then test-added to the run deck and passed through `DeckValidator`, so nothing
illegal is ever offered. Three are drawn uniformly at random from what is left, seeded per stage,
and saved with the run so a reload shows the same three.

**Not built yet:** weights, stage gating, and any power rating or rarity. A lockout or a limit-1
card can be offered at stage 1. Section 5 is the starting point for that work. The weight order
below is the earlier intent and is kept for that pass:

1. Cards the scaled precon holds at a larger size than yours, including second and third copies
   of what you already run. This is the spine, and it is the same ranking section 6 uses.
2. Your school's wider pool at the Aspect tier you have reached.
3. Freestyle staples. Always available, always slightly worse than the on-school option.
4. Signature cards, only when the named character they link to is already in the deck, or offered
   paired with that character. `SIGNATURE_LIMIT` is 4 and the pool must track it.

Rules on top:

- Skipping is always allowed.
- The skip can become a **cut**: remove one card from the deck instead of adding one. With the
  deck as the health total this is a real trade, not the free win removal is elsewhere.
- Never offer a card the deck cannot legally hold. School mismatch and limit-per-deck are filtered
  before the offer.

### 4.2 Reward currencies

Seven, all existing rules:

| Reward | Notes |
|---|---|
| Cards | pick 3, the main line |
| Deck cuts | the pacing lever |
| Aspects | 1 or 2 up to 5 |
| Relics | the side-deck holder, Sensei parallel. Sets Reserve size |
| Reserve slots | the side deck itself. Swap between stages once the next opponent is known |
| Seals | Root's win condition, utility for everyone else |
| Grounds | block of 3, shared across all decks |

### 4.3 Aspects

Starters run 1 to 2 Aspects. Aspect awards come **early** in a run, because the MPPV house rule
makes Aspect count a matchup fact and a shallow duelist is exposed to it.

MPPV stays live at 1 to 2 Aspects. One guard: tier 1 and 2 opponents are capped at the player's
own Aspect count, so nobody loses to an opponent's first ascension before the run has started.

Consider a cost on the fifth Aspect. Attunement that deep should hurt.

### 4.4 Grounds

Six exist: `trampled_crossroads`, `ancient_grove`, `tollgate_yard`, `frostbound_moor`,
`weighted_hollow`, `the_high_watch`. All schoolless, limit 3, and **no card text anywhere
references Grounds**. `tollgate_yard` is in no precon and is already a spare.

Every precon runs 3 copies of exactly one Grounds. Starters ship with none.

- Grounds are completely optional cards. A run with no Grounds is a normal run.
- They unlock as a **block of 3**, never as singles. A single copy in a 40-card deck is nearly
  blank, and a stacking-toward-three mechanic is not worth the complexity.
- Because they are schoolless they are the one reward track shared by all 13 decks, which gives
  the meta-progression a spine the ally and card unlocks cannot provide.
- Their JSON carries no `text`; the effects live in `effects`, `modifiers` and `forbid`, rendered
  by `card_text.gd`. They need power ranking like everything else. A Grounds with a `forbid` entry
  is a bomb and belongs late.

## 5. Ranking card power

Needed for two jobs: deciding which cards are cut from starters, and weighting the reward pool.

### 5.1 The printed tell

`limit_per_deck` is the strongest signal already in the data. A card printed at limit 1 or 2 is a
card a designer already judged too strong at three copies. Combined with `remove_after_use`, which
marks high-impact one-shots, limit 1 plus remove-after-use is nearly a bomb detector with no
tuning at all.

### 5.2 Heuristic

Scored from `CardDef` fields, descending weight:

| Signal | Field |
|---|---|
| Lockouts and standing bans | `forbid` |
| Unstoppable or unpreventable attacks | `attack.unstoppable`, `attack.no_prevent` |
| Multi-use in one combat | `remain`, `remain_when` |
| Universal defense | `defense.stops == "any"`, `stop_focused` |
| Free resilience | `shield` on drills, `start_in_play` |
| Response capability | `counter == "combat"` |
| Output per cost | `attack.life`, `attack.stages` against `cost_stages`, `cost_life` |
| Broad static buffs | `modifiers` with no `when` |
| Tempo swings | `effects` ops touching fervor, aspect, energy, search, draw |
| Self-limiting | `remove_after_use`, `bottom_after_use`, as negative terms |

Four tiers. Tiers 1 and 2 are starter-eligible. Tier 3 is mid-run reward. Tier 4 is late reward
and meta-unlock only.

### 5.3 Validate against the sim

A field heuristic will misrank cards whose power is contextual, and those are the ones that
matter. `deck_outcomes.gd` and the 756-match baseline already exist. Ablation gives the real
answer: run a deck at baseline, swap one card for a filler basic, rerun, take the win-rate delta
as that card's measured power.

Full coverage is 13 decks by roughly 40 cards, which is a lot of matches. Use the heuristic to
bucket everything and spend sim time only on the top and bottom buckets, where a misplacement
breaks a starter.

The ablation numbers are reusable: they set the reward weights, they drive the section 6 ranking,
and they
will surface balance bugs the way the two Ally engine bugs did.

## 6. Scaling a precon to any size

Nothing is hand-authored. One function takes a multiplayer precon and a target size and returns a
coherent deck at that size, and the same function produces both the player's starter and every
opponent tier. `tools/scale_deck.py` implements it; a GDScript port is needed for runtime
opponent generation.

### 6.1 Method

1. **Expand into slots.** A card at count 3 contributes three slots. The deck becomes a flat list
   of copies rather than a list of cards.
2. **Drop what adventure decks never hold.** Grounds, and allies outside the deck's kept pair.
3. **Rank the slots**, by this key in order:
   - core first (Seals, kept allies)
   - copy 1 of everything before any copy 2
   - the precon's own workhorses, highest count first
   - weakest first, so bombs land last
4. **Fill to target with role quotas** taken from the precon's own ratios, across offense
   (Strikes, Arts), answers (Combat) and support (Non-Combat, Drills).

### 6.2 Why it is built this way

**Monotone by construction.** Every size is a prefix of the same ranked list, so a tier-2 opponent
is strictly a superset of a tier-1 one and the full precon is the end of the line. Difficulty is a
single number, and no tier can contain a card a later tier lacks.

**Weakest first is the right direction.** A low-size deck is basics with no bombs, which is what a
starter should be and what an early opponent should be. Bombs appear only as the size climbs,
which is also why they are reward content: the player meets them before they own them.

**Role quotas are what stops drift.** Without them, culling by score alone strips a beatdown deck
of its few answers and leaves a pile of Strikes. Holding the precon's own ratios means a scaled
deck is a smaller version of that deck rather than a generically reasonable one. `pyre_beatdown`
at 40 keeps 31 offense, 7 answers and 2 support, which is thin on defense because the precon is
thin on defense. That is honest scaling, not a bug to correct.

**No authored ordering to maintain.** A precon rebalance reflows every tier automatically. The
cost is that the ranking is only as good as the power heuristic in section 5, so the ablation work
feeds straight back into this.

### 6.4 No lockouts in a starter

A starter holds no card that turns off a whole card type. No "stops all attacks for the remainder
of Combat", no "may not use Strikes this Combat", no naming a card out of the game. A starter is
Strikes, Arts, and blocks that answer one attack, with the deck's own archetype cards on top.

`tools/scale_deck.py` filters them out by field rather than by a list of ids:

| Marker | What it is |
|---|---|
| `defense.stop_all` | stops every attack, or every attack of a kind, for the Combat |
| card-level `forbid` | a standing ban while the card is in play |
| op `stop_all` | the same as a rider on another card |
| op `forbid`, `choose_forbid_type` | "may not use X for the remainder of Combat" |
| op `choose_stop_all_kind` | choose Strikes or Arts, all of them stop |
| op `cannot_declare_combat`, `skip_next_attack_phase` | a lost turn or phase |
| op `name_card` | neither player may play the named card |

Seals are exempt. A Seal is a win condition rather than a combat trick, and Root's set has to stay
whole.

That cut 93 card copies across the 13 starters. The freed slots refill from the same ranking, so
every starter is still 40 cards, 45 for Root, and still legal.

**Lockouts are reward content.** They stay in the precons untouched, which means the player meets
them on the ladder before they can own one, and they are exactly the right shape for a late pick
or a meta unlock: a single card that changes a duel, rather than a small number that changes a
curve. Tier 3 and gate-boss opponents run them, so a run teaches what they do before it offers
one.

### 6.5 Measured: small decks favour aggression

First playtest, 2026-09-20, `pyre_beatdown` against `storm_volley`, scorer policy, 80 matches per
configuration. Starter rows are after the Aspect-1 Ally house rule, so both decks run 2 Aspects.

| Configuration | Pyre win% | Turns |
|---|---|---|
| Full precons, 78 and 80 cards | 62.5% | 6.5 |
| Both starters at 40 | 77.5% | 3.4 |
| Both at 40, lockouts removed | 78.8% | 2.9 |
| Both at 50 | 81.3% | 3.7 |
| Stratified cull instead of weakest-first, both at 40 | 76.3% | 3.1 |
| Pyre 40 against Storm 50 | 67.5% | 3.7 |

The matchup is Pyre-favoured at full size and stripping widens it by about 16 points. Two
candidate causes were tested and neither is it. Deck size alone does not explain it, since 50
against 50 is no better than 40 against 40. The culling method does not explain it either, since
sampling evenly across the power range instead of taking the weakest first moves the result by two
points.

What does explain it is duel length. Life Deck is health, so a smaller deck is a shorter duel, and
the stripped duels run at half the precon length. **Short duels favour archetypes whose plan works
on turn one and punish archetypes that need setup.** Pyre attacks immediately. Storm builds Drills
and charges Energy before its discounted Arts matter, and at 3 turns it never gets there.

This is structural, not a tuning error, and it shapes the mode:

- Starter size should vary by archetype, not be a single number. Giving Storm 50 against Pyre's 40
  brings the matchup back to 67.5%, near its 62.5% baseline. Root already runs 45 for a different
  reason, so the precedent exists.
- Setup archetypes need their engine pieces early, so the first rewards on those ladders should be
  weighted toward the pieces the plan needs rather than toward raw power.
- Ladder difficulty has to be tuned per duelist. Starters are not balanced against each other and
  do not need to be, since the player never faces another starter. What matters is each starter
  against its own tier 1.

| Tier | Size | Lockouts | Max copies | Aspects | Relic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| t1 | 32 | no | 2 | 2 | no |
| t2 | 38 | no | 2 | 2 | no |
| t3 | 46 | yes | 3 | 3 | no |

### 6.7 Measured: size is a weak dial, deck identity is a strong one

`pyre_beatdown_start` against the tiers of one strong deck, scorer policy, 48 matches each:

| Opponent | Player win% |
|---|---|
| steel_beatdown t1, 32 cards | 50.0% |
| steel_beatdown t2, 38 cards | 29.2% |
| steel_beatdown t3, 46 cards | 27.1% |
| steel_beatdown t5, 66 cards | 20.8% |

Against four historically weak decks at t1:

| Opponent | Player win% |
|---|---|
| steel_heir t1 | 97.9% |
| tide_companions t1 | 97.9% |
| shade_salvage t1 | 93.8% |
| pyre_ascent t1 | 91.7% |

Two conclusions.

**Shrinking an aggressive deck barely weakens it.** Deck size is health, so cutting a beatdown
deck to 32 cards makes it a glass cannon that races rather than a weak deck. It keeps its tempo
and loses only its endurance, and in a three-turn duel endurance is not what was winning. Size is
a strong dial against setup archetypes, which lose the time their plan needs, and a weak one
against aggression. `steel_beatdown` at 32 cards still holds a 40-card starter to even.

**Deck identity swings harder than any tier.** The same player deck goes from 50% to 98% purely by
changing which deck it faces at the same tier. The 2026-09-19 scorer standings (Steel Heir 19.2%,
Pyre Ascent 24.6%, Tide Companions 27.1%, Shade Salvage 27.1%, Storm Volley 49.2%, Storm Unbound
52.9%) are the right starting order for a ladder, with the caveat that they were uncontrolled; the
current baseline is `docs/deck_tournament_2026-09-20.md`.

**So the ladder is fitted, not computed.** Pick a target win rate per stage, something like 85% at
stage 1 easing to 30% at the gate, and choose the (deck, tier) pair that measures closest at each
step. A formula on tier alone will not produce a curve, because the two dials do not compose
evenly. This needs a sweep tool: for a given starter, measure candidate opponents and select the
ladder that fits the target curve.

Also worth noting from these runs: every duel at these sizes lasts 2.4 to 3.7 turns. That is few
enough decisions that the early ladder may read as coin flips whatever the win rates say.

## 7. The ladder

A stage ladder, not a dungeon crawl. Every duel starts at full strength with the whole Life Deck
shuffled. Nothing carries between duels except the deck you built.

The model is a fighting-game story mode. Discrete matches, a named opponent per stage, a short
narrative beat, and a reward screen between. Progression is entirely deck growth.

**No persistent wounds. No rest or heal node.** Cards lost in a duel come back for the next one.
The deck-as-health tension still operates in full inside each duel, which is where it belongs, and
the mode does not need an attrition layer on top of it.

Eight to twelve stages, mostly linear with occasional branches, ending at a gate that belongs to
the duelist's own Eidolon. Thirteen duelists means thirteen short stories.

### 7.1 Stages

Every stage is a duel. Rewards attach to the duel rather than to separate nodes, so there is no
overworld to walk and no node type that is not a fight.

| After stage | Reward |
|---|---|
| any win | pick 3, or skip and cut one card |
| set stages | an Aspect, front-loaded so the ladder is not fought at 2 |
| one mid stage | a Relic, which gives the run a side deck |
| one mid stage | a Grounds block of 3 |
| branch choice | pick which of two opponents to face, and therefore which reward pool |

The cut option lives on every reward screen. It is the only way the deck gets smaller, and with
the deck as the life total it is always a real trade.

### 7.2 Stage conditions

Fighting-game story modes give stages a twist rather than a stat bump. Cheap here, and it uses
rules that already exist:

- The opponent starts with a Drill already in play.
- The opponent opens at Fervor 3.
- You open one Aspect higher.
- A named Rival returns two stages later at a larger deck size.

Gate bosses get one stated rule exemption printed on screen, following the GBA game's final boss,
instead of inflated numbers.

### 7.3 Losing

**A loss ends the run.** No retries. The deck you built dies with it, and unlocks are still
banked because acquisition marks cards seen (section 8).

That sets the temperature, and it puts weight on two things. Tier 1 stages have to be forgiving,
because a first-stage loss with a 40-card starter is the worst version of this mode. And the run
needs a power curve the player can feel building, which is what section 7.4 is for.

### 7.4 Stacking bonuses

This layer has no equivalent in the card game and is new to adventure mode.

It is **not** the Relic. The Relic is the side-deck holder, the parallel to a Sensei card: one
slot, an activated power, and a `reserve_size` that sets how many cards the Reserve holds. It
answers "what do I bring against this opponent", which is a different question from "what has this
run made me". The two do not overlap and both exist.

So the run gets its own stacking layer, in the Slay the Spire relic sense: small permanent
modifiers that accumulate over a run and combine into something the player did not plan.

They are **Resonances**. A duelist who keeps winning on the leylines starts to resonate with
them, and each one stacks into a fuller harmonic. Approved 2026-09-20. Deliberately not
"Enchantment", which is what Slay the Spire 2 calls its own per-run modifier system.

**Implementation.** A Resonance is a hidden Drill with `start_in_play`, owned by the run rather than by
the Life Deck, and it cannot be discarded or removed. It carries `modifiers` and `effects` arrays
in exactly the schema `CardDef` already uses, so there is no new effect vocabulary, no new
resolution path, and `card_text.gd` renders it for the UI unchanged. Ascending discards Drills, so
Resonances need a flag exempting them from that, which is the only engine change the layer
requires.

**Economy.** Three to five per run. Offered at stage rewards and as the payoff for taking the
harder branch. Tiered the way Slay the Spire tiers relics, with the strongest reserved for gate
wins.

**Shape.** Every example below is an existing rule, not a new one:

| Tier | Example |
|---|---|
| Common | Open each duel at one Energy stage higher |
| Common | Draw one extra card on your first turn |
| Common | Your first Strike each duel does +2 stages |
| Uncommon | Gain +1 Fervor the first time you ascend |
| Uncommon | Your Seals cannot be captured the turn they are placed |
| Uncommon | The opponent opens with one fewer Drill |
| Gate | Your Reserve holds 3 more cards |
| Gate | Once per duel, ignore the first successful Art against you |

**Guard.** Resonances must not touch the Ascension or MPPV win directly. A Resonance that grants Fervor on a
schedule turns every duel into a race to the same ending, which is the failure mode the house rule
already flirts with.

## 8. Meta-progression

Unlocks are a single global collection keyed by card id in `unlocks.json`. School legality still
filters what any duelist can field, so one collection does not mean one deck.

- A card banks the moment you pick it, whether or not the run survives. See 8.2.
- Before a run, swap cards out of the authored starter for unlocked ones, inside whatever size
  cap is currently unlocked. Adventure legality still applies.
- Finishing with one duelist unlocks another, GBA-style, in a short chain.
- A Covenant-style difficulty ladder after the first clear.

`core` cards are locked from being swapped out. The alternative is a player discovering at stage 3
that their archetype does not function.

### 8.1 Start-of-run upgrades

Unlocks are not only cards. The whole shape of the starting deck is upgradable, and the end point
is a start that already looks like a tournament deck.

**Advanced duelists.** Decided 2026-09-20. Five starters are locked until the deck-cap track
reaches 50: Steel Heir, Shade Salvage, Tide Companions, Storm Unbound and Root Seals. They start at
50 cards (Root 55) with 3 Aspects and their upgrades already in, including the Allies the open
builds would ration. These are the decks whose plans need setup, and they came in lowest when
starters were playtested at 40 cards. Details in `zenith/docs/adventure_starters.md`; each file
carries `"unlock": {"track": "deck_cap", "step": 50}` for the runtime to read.

| Track | Steps | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Card pool | wider each run | The main line. Swap into the starter, do not add |
| Starting deck cap | 40 → 45 → 50 | 50 is `MIN_CARDS`, so a fully upgraded start is a legal multiplayer deck |
| Aspects at start | 2 → 3 → 4 | Also the fix for the Ascension-win problem at low Aspect counts |
| Allies at start | the rationed ones become startable | Tide's third and fourth companion, the rest of the Draiks |
| Relic at start | locked → available | A side deck from stage one, so scouting works immediately |
| Grounds at start | locked → one block of 3 | |
| Extraction slots | 1 → 2 → 3 | How many cards a run can pull into the collection at full count |
| Resonance at start | none → choose one | The strongest track, keep it last |

Reaching the top of the deck-size track closes the loop the mode opened. Section 1 says a finished
run is a deck `DeckValidator` would pass. A fully upgraded **start** is that same deck, so the
veteran begins where the first run ended and the ladder has to be what supplies the challenge.

**This is real power creep and it has to be paid for.** Every step above makes run 20 start
stronger than run 1, which inverts the difficulty curve unless something scales with it. Two ways
to pay, and they combine:

- Tie the tracks to the Covenant-style ladder, so a stronger start is only available on a harder
  ladder, or unlocks the harder ladder alongside it.
- Make them a budget rather than a stack. The player picks two or three setup upgrades per run out
  of everything unlocked, so a veteran configures a run instead of compounding every bonus.

The budget version is the one I would build first. It keeps every unlock meaningful instead of
making the last few irrelevant, and it turns pre-run setup into a decision rather than a menu that
is always set to maximum.

### 8.2 Losing still pays

A loss ends the run (7.3), but it does not erase it. Progress banks in two tiers.

**Per stage, kept on a loss.** The card you picked at a stage is unlocked the moment you pick it.
Die at stage 6 and you keep six cards in the collection. This is the small, steady line, and it
means a bad run still widens next run's options.

**Per run, finish only.** The setup tracks in 8.1 (deck cap step, Aspect step, Relic, Grounds,
Resonance slot) and the next duelist in the chain. These are the satisfying ones and they are what
finishing is for.

**Milestones, kept on a loss.** Furthest stage reached with each duelist, banked permanently.
Reaching stage 3 and stage 6 for the first time each pay a setup-track step. So a player who keeps
losing at stage 7 still climbs, slowly, and eventually starts strong enough to get past it. This
is the anti-wall valve, and it is why a run ending in a loss is allowed to be harsh.

Power creep is accepted here on purpose. Beating a run should feel like it moved something. The
guard is not to suppress the creep but to keep the ladder scaling with it, per 8.1.

**Farming guard.** A card banks the first time it is picked, so replaying stage 1 to harvest the
same cards gives nothing after the first pass. Early stages also offer a narrow pool by design.


### 8.3 Collection, Motes and forging

The collection is per card id with a copy count. **Banking a card gives you one copy.** Copies two
and three have to come from somewhere else, which is what this section is for. That matters
because starters cap at 2 copies (3.1) and a finished deck wants 3, so the copy economy has a real
sink from the first run onward.

**Motes** are the currency. Named 2026-09-20. They are what is left when a spell is dissolved
instead of cast.

**Extraction.** At the end of a run, win or lose, you see the deck you built and pull cards out of
it into the permanent collection at the count the run had them. This is the deliberate version of
banking: banking is automatic and gives one copy, extraction is a choice and gives the copies you
actually assembled. It is also the moment a run that died at stage 6 still hands you something you
chose.

**Start at one slot.** One extraction per run, so the choice is sharp. You pull the single card
the run was actually about. More slots come from two directions, and both should exist:

- A meta track in 8.1, permanent, one extra slot per step.
- A per-run reward, temporary, "+1 extraction this run". A useful non-card stage reward that is
  not a Resonance, and it makes the branch you took matter at the end rather than only during.

Slots are worth more late in a run than early, so an in-run slot offered at stage 3 is a real
gamble: it only pays if you survive to use it.

**Dissolving.** Duplicate copies beyond what the collection can hold, and any card you no longer
want, dissolve into Motes. Rates scale with the power tier from section 5, which is where rarity
comes from. Zenith has no printed rarity, so the tiers do that job and the open question about
where tiers are stored now matters more.

| Tier | Dissolve | Forge |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | 30 |
| 2 | 15 | 90 |
| 3 | 40 | 240 |
| 4 | 100 | 600 |

The 6x spread is deliberately wider than Hearthstone's 4x to 8x band at the low end. Forging is
meant to be the slow way to finish a deck, not the fast one.

**Forging.** Spend Motes to add a copy of a card to the collection.

- **You can only forge a card you have already unlocked at least one copy of.** Discovery still
  has to happen in a run. Whether unseen cards become forgeable later, at a premium or at all, is
  left open.
- Forging is deliberately expensive at the rates above. It is the answer to "I have seen this card
  three runs running and never got a second copy", not a deck builder.
- `limit_per_deck` still caps the collection. There is no reason to hold four of a card printed at
  three.

**Where Motes come from.**

| Source | Notes |
|---|---|
| Dissolving duplicates | The main line |
| Cards left in a run deck at the end | Everything not extracted dissolves automatically |
| Stage milestones | Small, and they pay out on a loss |
| Gate wins | The large payouts |

Because leftover run cards dissolve, a losing run at stage 6 still pays Motes proportional to how
far it got, which is the same principle as 8.2.

**The wildcard variant.** MTG Arena solves this with tier wildcards instead of a currency: a token
that becomes any card of its tier. Simpler for the player and impossible to hoard badly. Worth
keeping in mind if Motes turn out to be fiddly, but Motes are the more robust system and are what
was asked for.

## 10. Decided

- Mastery is never stripped. It is fundamental to the starting deck.
- MPPV stays live at 1 to 2 Aspects, handled by early Aspect awards rather than by gating the rule.
- Starters are separate authored files. No bleed-through from `data/decks/`.
- Starting allies are concrete and fixed per deck, not randomised.
- Grounds are optional cards, cut from every starter, unlocked as a block of 3.
- Starters are 40 cards, Root 45. No card appears more than twice in a starter.
- Bombs are cut objectively: any non-personality, non-seal card printed at `limit_per_deck` 1.
- Unlocked cards can be swapped into future starting decks.
- Cards bank per stage and survive a loss. Setup tracks need a finish. Stage milestones pay out
  even to a player who keeps losing. See 8.2.
- Start-of-run upgrades go beyond cards: deck size cap toward 50, Aspects at start, Relic,
  Grounds, Resonance, the rationed allies. See 8.1.
- Banking gives one copy of a card. Extra copies come from extraction at run end, or from
  forging with **Motes**, the currency duplicates dissolve into. Forging is limited to cards
  already unlocked, at a deliberately expensive rate. See 8.3.
- Runs start with one extraction slot. More come from a meta track and from per-run rewards.
- No persistent wounds and no heal node. Every duel starts at full strength.
- The cut option is on every reward screen.
- A lost stage ends the run. No retries.
- Starters hold no lockout cards. Those stay in the precons as ladder threats and reward
  content. See 6.4.
- Opponent tiers are generated by one scaling function from the precons (section 6). Starters are
  hand-authored with one or two bombs each; see `zenith/docs/adventure_starters.md`.
- Starters run 2 Aspects. Aspect-1 Allies are legal in any deck as of the 2026-09-20 house rule
  in `zenith.md`, so ally decks do not need a taller ladder to be legal. Fervor does not peak fast enough for the Ascension win to be a
  problem at that count.
- Stacking per-run modifiers are **Resonances**, implemented as undiscardable hidden Drills,
  3 to 5 a run. They are not Relics; the Relic is the Sensei parallel and holds the Reserve.

## 11. Open

- Per-archetype starter sizes: which decks get 40, 45 or 50.
- The fitted ladder: which (deck, tier) pair sits at each stage, per duelist. Needs a sweep
  tool. See 6.7.
- Whether Grounds move to a pre-duel site slot instead of the Life Deck. Plays better, but it is a
  rules change that would have to apply to multiplayer too.
- Power tiers: authored on the card JSON, or computed at load from the heuristic. They now double
  as rarity for the Mote economy, so this matters more than it did.
- Whether cards never seen in a run become forgeable later, at a premium or at all.
- Whether start-of-run upgrades stack freely or are spent from a per-run budget. See 8.1.
- Whether a Style swap is ever allowed mid-run. Every school check in `deck_validator.gd` assumes
  one Style per deck, so probably not.

## 12. Revisions after the first played slice, 2026-09-20

The first slice (section 4.1 as built) played well but one card per win is far too little. These
decisions supersede 4.1, 8.2 and 8.3 where they conflict. Those sections get folded in once the
bundle work below is settled.

### 12.1 Rewards are theme bundles

- The pick-3 window offers three **theme bundles**, not three cards. A bundle is 2 or 3 cards that
  share a theme, shown with their cards visible. Pick one bundle.
- Bundles come from an analysis of the full roster by school and card type:
  `zenith/docs/archetypes.md` (mechanic themes, school identities, archetype plans, proposed
  bundles). That document is wanted for its own sake too.
- Legality is unchanged: every card in an offered bundle has to pass `DeckValidator` in the run
  deck. Bundle names are working names until tone approval.
- Bundles may hold any card legal in the run deck: school cards, Freestyle cards, Allies and
  Grounds. Masteries and Relics stay out. The validator's rules still bind: Allies at one copy,
  the Ally Aspect rule, alignment gates.
- **Freestyle is a school like any other, only shared.** Any legal Freestyle card can be bundled.
  The old 17-card Freestyle core list is gone.
- **An Ally always arrives with two of its own named cards.** The bundle is the personality plus
  two cards that name it. An Ally's named cards are offered only in that bundle or once the Ally
  is in the run deck. An Ally with fewer than two named cards cannot be bundled yet.
- **No Seals through the bundle choice.** Seals are held for a different reward route.
- Working default: when any card in a bundle cannot legally be added, the whole bundle is left
  out of the offer.
- Open: whether lockouts and limit-1 cards can sit in bundles, whether bundle size grows with the
  stage, whether Grounds get their own reward slot.

**Built 2026-09-21** (`adventure/adventure_bundles.gd`, `adventure/adventure_rewards.gd`).

- 147 bundles in `data/adventure/bundles.json`, transcribed from `zenith/docs/archetypes.md`
  section 5: 12 Pyre, 15 Steel, 13 Tide, 15 Storm, 15 Root, 13 Shade, 29 Freestyle, 7 Grounds,
  12 Ally, 16 signature. Each carries `group`, `tier`, its cards with counts, and a `character`
  (Ally core and signature) or `requires_character` (Ally follow-up).
- Tier gates by stage, as a fraction of the ladder length: `early` from stage 1, `mid` from a
  quarter of the way up (stage 3 of 8), `late` from halfway (stage 5 of 8).
- Every offer holds one bundle of the run's own school group whenever one is eligible; for the
  Freestyle Style that is a Freestyle bundle. No offer holds more than two bundles of one group.
  A bundle already taken is never offered again.
- The hard gate is last and is the working default above: all of a bundle's cards are added
  together to a copy of the run deck and passed through `DeckValidator`, and one problem drops
  the whole bundle. Alignment-gated cards (`alignment_only`) are filtered in the reward layer,
  because `DeckValidator` reads that field on personalities only.
- **Aspects are a choice.** A stage with `"grant": "aspect"` now sets the run's status to
  `"aspect"` and lists every legal next-tier card of the Duelist's character. Taking one moves
  the run to `"reward"`, where the bundle offer is drawn from the deck as it stands afterwards.
  One option is still shown and confirmed. A Duelist at the top of its ladder, or at 5, records
  a skipped grant and goes straight to the bundles.

### 12.2 Pool expansion

The school pools are too thin for bundles (Storm 13 cards, Root 9). Decided 2026-09-20:

- Fill gaps first. `zenith/docs/archetypes.md` names what each school lacks by type and theme.
  Every school comes up to roughly 35 to 40 cards, thin schools first. About 80 to 100 new cards.
- Every new card parallels one printed card from the reference game's era, later sets included.
  The rule in `zenith/CLAUDE.md` is unchanged.
- Candidates go into `zenith/tools/source_candidates.tsv` with a status (candidate, approved,
  built), beside `source_cards.tsv` and `source_audit.tsv`. They cannot live in
  `docs/card_roster.csv`, because `tools/gen_roster.py` rebuilds that file from shipped data and
  would drop them. A built card gets its roster row the usual way.
- Names are drafted per school and approved before the cards are written.
- Order: gaps, then cards, then final bundles and the bundle reward screen.
- New cards join the whole game's pool, not only adventure rewards.

### 12.3 Motes are the meta currency

Motes are the one currency outside a run. Extraction slots are gone.

- **Keeping cards costs Motes.** Nothing banks for free. At the end of a run you see the cards
  the run added to the deck and pay Motes for the ones you want in the collection.
- **Winning gives a discount.** Beating the ladder opens a one-time discounted offer on any card
  in the run deck, available only before the run resets.
- **Card vendor.** Sells cards outright into the collection for Motes. Rotating stock, so there
  is a reason to check back and a reason to save.
- **Income:** a payout per stage cleared, so a lost run still pays for how far it got; a run
  completion bonus; dissolving collection duplicates.
- Prices need the rarity tiers from section 5, which are still not built.
- **A per-run currency is an option, not a decision.** Nothing is assigned to it yet. Candidate
  uses if it comes in: rerolling a bundle offer, buying a second bundle, paying for cuts, a shop
  stage on the ladder. Motes stay meta only.

Removed by this: free banking of picked cards (8.2), extraction slots and their meta track (8.1,
8.3), automatic dissolving of leftover run cards, and the "forge only what you have unlocked"
rule, which the vendor replaces.

**Built 2026-09-21.** Logic and data only; the settle, vendor and loadout screens are a separate
pass. `adventure/adventure_economy.gd` (prices and payouts), `adventure_wallet.gd` (Motes and a
ledger), `adventure_collection.gd` (the card library), `adventure_vendor.gd` (the shelf),
`adventure_settlement.gd` (the run-end screen's rules), `adventure_loadout.gd` (pre-run swaps),
with the numbers in `data/adventure/economy.json` and the plumbing in the adventure block of
`scripts/autoload/session.gd`.

- **Bands.** Prices are flat by a printed tell, in three bands, because the game prints no rarity
  and section 5's power tiers are still not built. `base` 80 Motes is anything a deck may run
  three of; `limited` 160 is a card printed at two, plus every personality and every Grounds;
  `restricted` 320 is a lockout (`is_lockout`, ported from `tools/scale_deck.py`), a card printed
  at one, and every Seal. A rarity system later changes `economy.json` and
  `AdventureEconomy.band()` and nothing else.
- **First numbers.** Stage payout 20 + 10 per stage index (20 through 90, 440 over eight stages),
  completion bonus 50, win discount 25%, dissolve 25% of price, vendor shelf 6 cards, reroll 40.
  A full win pays 490, which keeps about five or six of the roughly 22 cards a run adds, or buys
  one or two of the vendor's better cards. A run lost on the fifth stage pays 140, which keeps one
  or two.
- **The collection is a library, not a box.** A run copies cards out of it and never empties it,
  so a card swapped into a starter is still there next run. Copies per id cap at what a deck may
  legally run, read off `DeckValidator`'s own constants.
- **The run-end settlement** is a status of its own, `"settle"`, reached from `"won"` or
  `"lost"`. A loss puts the cards the run added on offer at full price. A win puts the whole run
  deck on offer, the starter's own cards included, at the discount, on that screen only. Closing
  it ends the run and rolls the vendor's shelf over.
- Files: `user://adventure/wallet.json` (Motes, ledger, the vendor's `stock_seed`) and
  `user://adventure/collection.json` (id to copy count). The run save is at version 4: a version 3
  run loads with a run id generated and its starting deck read back off the starter file, so a run
  in flight still settles for what it actually added.

**Three rules added 2026-09-21.**

- **Copy caps, and the overflow dissolves.** The collection holds at most **3** copies of a normal
  card and **4** of a card named for a character. Personalities and Seals are **1** apiece. A card
  that prints a tighter limit of its own keeps that lower number, so a card printed at two caps at
  two. Anything that lands past the cap dissolves into Motes on the spot at `dissolve_value`, with
  a ledger line and a report the screen shows once ("2 copies dissolved for 40 Motes"). The settle
  screen's Keep and the vendor's Buy stop at the cap instead of dissolving on purchase, so nobody
  pays full price for a copy that would come straight back at a quarter. The auto-dissolve is for
  migration and any other path that lands copies the collection cannot hold; `collection.json` is
  at version 2 and a version 1 file is trimmed on load, the overflow paid back.
- **A loadout takes no more copies than the collection holds.** The collection is a library shared
  by every starter, so the same three copies go into as many starters as you like and nothing is
  consumed. What is limited is one deck: a loadout may put in at most as many copies of a card as
  the collection holds. The starter's own printed cards are the starter's, not the library's, so
  they never count against it; `AdventureLoadout` measures what a deck took against the printed
  starter it was built from.
- **Deck slots and Aspect tiers are bought with Motes, per starter.** A starter begins at its own
  printed size (50 Life Deck cards for the shipped starters) and its own stack height. A bought
  slot raises that starter's loadout size cap by one, filled with any legal pick from the
  collection; an empty slot is a ceiling and not a requirement, so a starter may begin with it
  unfilled. An Aspect tier unlock lets the loadout add the next Aspect card of the starter's own
  character from the collection, one tier at a time and consecutive from Aspect 1; the run's own
  in-run grant then carries on from wherever the stack ends, offering tier 4 to a stack that
  reached tier 3 and skipping the grant once the stack is at the construction maximum.
  `user://adventure/upgrades.json` (version 1) holds `extra_slots` and `aspect_tiers` per starter.
- **Slot and tier numbers.** Slots cost 100, 150, 200, 275, 350, 450, 550, 700, 850, 1000, ten in
  all, with the last price repeating if the list is ever extended. Aspect tiers cost 200 for tier
  3, 400 for tier 4 and 800 for tier 5. A full win pays 490 and a run lost on the fifth stage pays
  140, so the first slot is inside one good run, the first three cost 450 and land inside two, and
  the fifth brings the total to 1075, about two and a quarter full wins. The whole ten-slot track
  is 4725, near ten wins, which is meant to outlast the cards themselves. Tier 3 at 200 sits
  alongside a couple of kept cards out of one win; tiers 4 and 5 double each time, so a five-Aspect
  stack costs 1400 on its own.
- Files: `adventure/adventure_upgrades.gd` with the numbers in `data/adventure/economy.json`
  (`slot_costs`, `slot_max`, `aspect_tier_costs`), the loadout API in
  `adventure/adventure_loadout.gd` (`size_cap`, `add_card`, `add_aspect`, `swappable_add`,
  `swappable_aspect`) and the wrappers in the adventure block of `scripts/autoload/session.gd`
  (`upgrades`, `buy_slot`, `buy_aspect_tier`, `take_dissolve_report`).

## 13. First to two, 2026-09-21

Adventure duels run first to two points (`Session.build_referee`, `DuelEngine.set_points_to_win`).
Every other mode is the printed game.

- **Survival point.** A Life Deck that runs out scores the rival a point. The discard pile shuffles
  into a new Life Deck; cards removed from the game stay out, so the second deck is the weaker
  one. The table is untouched. The hit that scored loses whatever damage it had left. An empty
  discard pile at that moment is the loss.
- **Ascension point.** Entering the top Aspect or standing above the rival's ladder (the MPPV
  house rule) scores once per duelist and resets nothing beyond the Fervor peak the printed rules
  already give a top Aspect.
- **Seal point.** A full Seal set scores once per duelist and the Seals stay in play
  (`set_points_options`). Decided 2026-09-21 after measuring: with the set as an outright win,
  Root won 72% of a 14-starter round robin; as one point it still won 72%, so the change costs
  Root nothing and removes the one route that ended a duel in a single stroke.
- **Rejected the same day:** returning "remove from the game after use" cards for the second
  Life Deck. Measured, it helped the strongest decks most (Steel Beatdown 81 to 83, Root 72 to
  78) and moved the Art decks not at all. The switch stays in the engine, off.
- Measured against best-of-one on the same seeds, first to two lengthens a duel from about 5
  turns to about 8 and favours the slow engines (Root, the Drill deck) over the Art and Ally decks,
  whose one-shot cards do not come back.
