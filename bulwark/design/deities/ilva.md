# Ilva, the Ward (dead)

Elven goddess of protection: shields, fords, gates, and the holding of lines. She died at
Ilvaren at the end of the war, when her own shrine became the channel through which she was
detonated. Who triggered it is disputed to this day. She is the only deity in the setting
known to be dead, and her death is the wound at the center of elven religion.

## Core identity (historical)

- **Title:** The Ward
- **Domains:** Protection, Duty
- **Divine font:** Heal
- **Sanctification:** Optional good
- **Divine skill:** Athletics
- **Favored weapon:** Spear (the line-holder's weapon)

No cleric has drawn on her since Ilvaren. The block above is archival: it describes a faith
that can no longer be practiced, and no character in the game will have her as a live deity.

## What she was

The elven pantheon is broad, and Ilva stood high in it, though not at its head. Where other
gods held the forest, the moon, the craft, or the dead, Ilva held the ground itself: any place
that needed keeping. Her shrines stood at fords, gates, passes, and city walls, wherever a
line could be drawn between what is kept and what is lost. A town that grew around one of her
shrines grew up defended, and Ilvaren, at its river ford, was the largest of these.

Her worship was a soldier's and a watchman's faith, practical rather than mystic. To stand a
post honestly was prayer. To hold a line against a stronger enemy was the highest rite.

## Edicts (as they were)

- Hold what is given into your keeping
- Stand between the weak and what comes for them
- Yield ground only to save lives, and retake it when you can
- Keep your watch honestly; a line held by a liar is already broken

## Anathema (as they were)

- Abandon a post while it can still be held
- Turn a defense into a slaughter
- Take by siege what you would not defend afterward

## The death

Aurenthal's army took Ilvaren, and Lanric came in person to claim it. Then the shrine went. A
shrine is a channel to its god, and something drove Ilva through it all at once: the goddess
herself was the blast, and the shrine only the point where she entered the world. It killed
the army, the king, the town, and Ilva, and it cannot happen twice.

Who triggered it is the war's last and worst dispute. Aurenthal holds that the elven command
detonated their own goddess rather than yield the crossing. The elves hold that human hands in
the captured shrine broke the channel open, through sabotage or desecration or plain ignorant
greed. No one close enough to know survived, and each side's story requires the other to be a
monster.

Her clerics felt her go mid-prayer, in every elven city at once. That silence is how the elven
nations learned of her death before any messenger arrived.

The theology of how a god can die is not settled, and this document does not settle it. What
the elves know is that no prayer to Ilva has been answered since.

## Legacy

Her shrines still stand at other crossings, and elves tend them the way a family tends a
grave: swept, repaired, and empty. Elves do not speak her name casually. The polite habit,
even among elves, is to say "the Ward" and move on.

The elves keep the cold peace in part because their shield is gone. Whatever else the war
cost, it cost them the goddess whose whole purpose was to keep them, and half the world says
they spent her themselves. Neither the grief nor the accusation fades on a mortal timescale,
and elves do not live on a mortal timescale.

## Design notes

- Her portfolio deliberately rhymes with the game's central theme: the outpost is a held line,
  and the bulwark is exactly the kind of thing Ilva existed to bless. No character should say
  this out loud.
- The trigger question has no canonical answer yet. If it ever gets one, it should be earned
  late and cost something; until then, every in-game source speaks from its own side's story.
- Elara was born in Ilvaren and is secular; see `../characters/elara.md`. She grew up under
  Ilva's shrine, and her secularism dates from the day she came home to the crater.
- The rest of the elven pantheon is unnamed (open question in `../world.md`).
