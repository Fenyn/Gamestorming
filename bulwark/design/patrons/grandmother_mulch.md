# Grandmother Mulch

Witch patron. An entity older than the forest it inhabits, though "inhabits" is the wrong word.
Grandmother Mulch is the process by which the forest digests itself. She is the rot that feeds
the root. She is the warm, wet darkness under the leaf litter where dead things come apart and
become the raw material for living things. She has been doing this longer than any tree currently
standing has been alive, and she will continue long after the last one falls.

## Core identity

- **Type:** Witch patron (not a deity)
- **Tradition granted:** Primal
- **Patron theme:** Decay, rebirth, the cycle of life through death
- **Familiar form:** Animated cauldron (vessel of transformation)

## Nature

Grandmother Mulch presents herself as warm and nurturing. She tends the forest the way a
gardener tends a compost heap: with genuine affection for the process of things coming apart.
She sees death not as an ending but as an ingredient. Every fallen tree is a gift. Every rotting
carcass is a beginning. The cauldron she grants her witch is the original vessel of
transformation, where the dead become the living.

She is kind. She is also the oldest thing in the forest by an order of magnitude, and her
kindness operates on a timescale that does not always feel kind to mortals. She does not mourn
individual deaths because she does not perceive them as losses. They are contributions. The
beetle that dies at the base of an oak feeds the fungus that feeds the soil that feeds the oak.
Grandmother Mulch finds this beautiful. She does not understand why anyone would find it sad.

## Philosophy

Nothing is wasted. Nothing is permanent. These are not rules. They are observations about the
way the world works, and Grandmother Mulch has been observing for a very long time.

She does not oppose civilization. She does not oppose fire or axes or farming. She opposes
waste. A tree felled for timber is fine. A tree felled and left to dry out on bare stone where
nothing can reach it is an obscenity. Use what you take. Return what you cannot use. Feed the
soil. This is all she asks, and she asks it with the quiet insistence of something that has
never once been wrong.

## Relationship to Spore

Grandmother Mulch chose Spore's vessel and called the spirit into it. She is patron,
creator, and the closest thing Spore has to a parent, though neither of them would use that
word. Spore serves Grandmother Mulch the way moss serves a stone: by covering it, by living
on it, by doing the small necessary work that the stone is too large and too old to do itself.

The cauldron familiar is the conduit between them. When Spore brews, Grandmother Mulch
watches through the steam. When the cauldron hops and clatters, it is sometimes excited and
sometimes receiving instructions. Spore does not always distinguish between the two, and
Grandmother Mulch does not always clarify.

## Notes

- Grandmother Mulch is not a deity. She does not have clerics, temples, edicts, or anathema
  in the formal PF2e sense. She is a patron: a powerful entity that grants power to a single
  witch in exchange for service that neither party has ever clearly defined.
- She is not evil. She is not good. She is a process with opinions. Her morality is ecological,
  not personal.
- Her "old god" quality comes from her age and her alienness, not from power. She is not going
  to smite anyone. She is going to watch everything eventually return to the soil, and she is
  going to be patient about it.
- If the party ever asks Spore what Grandmother Mulch wants, the answer is always some
  variation of: "She wants you to use what you take and give back what you do not need."
