# Fenwick

- **id:** `fenwick`
- **Class:** Wizard (School of Battle Magic)
- **Ancestry:** Halfling
- **Pronouns:** he/him
- **Kind:** starting-party PC
- **Associated building:** Tavern
- **Deity:** Lyndra (see `../deities/lyndra.md`)

## Vibe

Fenwick's friendliness makes other people friendly. He talks to everyone, remembers
what they like to eat, and has an opinion about the proper way to prepare almost anything. He is
chipper without being grating, largely because his enthusiasm is genuine rather than performed.
He calls himself the world's first gastronomancer, and while no one else uses that word, no one
has been able to argue with the results.

He is clever, the kind of clever that comes from loving to learn. He picks things up quickly,
asks good questions, and remembers details. He holds a diploma in evocation and transmutation,
but his heart was never in the arcane theory. It was in the kitchen. He uses the magic the way
a chef uses a good knife: as a tool in service of the real work.

## Role in the town

Fenwick runs the tavern and the kitchen. He is the outpost's chef, its social anchor, and the
person most likely to know how everyone is doing on any given day. People talk to Fenwick because
he is easy to talk to, and because there is usually something cooking.

Mechanically, he is the source of day-long meal buffs for the party. As the Tavern building
upgrades and his recipe collection grows, the range and quality of those buffs expand. He is
also the party's second spellcaster, bringing arcane firepower that complements Tharr's divine
support.

## Background

Fenwick's family are capital halflings who wanted their son to make something of himself, which
in their view meant the arcane academy. Wizard training is expensive; the whole kin-group pooled
for it. He went, worked hard, spent more time in the dormitory kitchen than the library, and
graduated with a solid grasp of evocation and transmutation and an even more solid grasp of how
to properly reduce a sauce. Then he told them he meant to cook.

They disowned him. Among Lyndra's people that is done at the table: when the family gathers, no
place is set for him. The shared meal is the rite of the faith, and a bowl set before you arrive
says you belong. To come home and find no bowl is as final a judgment as halfling culture can
pass. From the family's side, he took the kin-group's sacrifice and made a joke of it. From his,
the hearth was always the point of everything, including the diploma. Neither side has moved.

He does not talk about it. Most days he is the least burdened person at the outpost, and the
hurt surfaces only in what he never fails to do: nobody sits at Fenwick's table and finds
nothing waiting for them. Halflings have older words for a cook who feeds his kin. They are not
his to use now, so he coined his own.

At the academy he fed a homesick farm boy from outside the walls, and the friendship stuck.
When the frontier posting came through, the ministries' overdue answer to a border outpost's
requisitions, Fenwick volunteered to go along. His stated reason is true: a kitchen built from
scratch, a menu designed from the ground up, forage no capital chef has touched, and cooking
for people who genuinely need a good meal. The fact that the borderlands are dangerous only
makes the cooking more important, in his view. Morale starts at the table. The unstated reason
is that the capital holds a family table he is not welcome at, and distance makes that easier
to live with.
