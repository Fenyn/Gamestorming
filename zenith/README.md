# Eidolarch

A two-player dueling card game. Two duelists, mages or plain fighters, contest a place of power where a gate can be opened for an otherworldly Eidolon; the winner becomes its Eidolarch. The folder and code name are still `zenith`. Design: `../designs/zenith.md`.

- **Engine:** Godot 4.6 (Forward Plus), GDScript
- **Status:** playable hotseat, AI, online and adventure prototype. Rules engine with headless tests, 3D playspace, deck select, fourteen starter decks whose cards carry their full mechanics (themed titles, no flavor text yet). Screens and effects: `docs/presentation.md`. Who the characters are: `docs/cast.md`. How each deck is piloted: `docs/strategy.md`.
- **Run:** Open `zenith/project.godot` in Godot 4.6 and press F5. Title → Duel the AI (second option; Hotseat duel is the third) → pick duelists → matchup → play. The table swings to whoever has to decide, behind a hand-off screen.
- **Adventure:** Title → Adventure (first option) → pick a starter → a node map of three acts, each ending in a boss, with Halden Quarr at the end of act 3. The map takes most of the stage screen, with the run's own portrait beside the node it stands on; the side panel shows the deck, act, duels won and Motes over a preview of the node picked, and the footer button goes to a glowing node. Fighting nodes start a duel; the other node types do nothing yet. Each win offers one of three theme bundles of 2 to 4 cards to add to the Life Deck, which can be skipped or traded for cutting a card. A duel that grants an Aspect first asks which next-tier card of the Duelist to take, then offers the bundles. A loss ends the run, and so does conceding; the result is recorded the moment the duel ends, not when Continue is pressed. One run is saved at a time (`user://adventure/run.json`), rewritten after every command of a duel, so closing the game and coming back resumes at the stage screen, or inside an unfinished duel at the position it was left in (the referee replays the saved commands on the duel's own seed, and the lead-in is not shown again). The map's numbers live in `data/adventure/map.json`, the run state in `adventure/`, and the screens in `scenes/adventure/`.
- **Motes:** the one currency outside a run. A won duel pays Motes (more in later acts, most for an act boss), beating the final boss pays a bonus, and dissolving a spare copy pays a quarter of its price. Nothing banks for free: when a run ends, won or lost, its cards go on offer and each copy kept costs Motes, at a discount and across the whole run deck when the run was won. The card vendor sells cards outright off a short shelf that rolls over after every run, or for a fee on the spot. Prices are flat by a printed tell in three bands (`base`, `limited`, `restricted`), and every number lives in `data/adventure/economy.json`. The collection (`user://adventure/collection.json`) is a library, not a box: before a run its cards can be swapped into the starter, one out and one in, and a run only copies from it. Motes and the vendor's shelf sit in `user://adventure/wallet.json`.
- **Copy caps:** the collection holds at most 3 copies of a normal card and 4 of a card named for a character; personalities and Seals are 1 apiece, and a card printed at one or two keeps that lower number. Keep and Buy stop at the cap rather than selling a copy that will not fit. Anything that lands past the cap anyway, an old save above the new cap most of all, dissolves into Motes on the spot, and the screen says so once ("2 copies dissolved for 40 Motes").
- **Loadout limits:** a starter may take at most as many copies of a card as the collection holds. The library is shared, so the same three copies go into every starter at once and nothing is consumed; the starter's own printed cards never count against it.
- **Deck slots and Aspect tiers:** bought with Motes on the loadout screen, per starter, and kept for good (`user://adventure/upgrades.json`). A slot raises that starter's 50-card deck cap by one, filled with any legal pick from the collection, and an empty slot still starts. Slots run all the way to the 85-card deck maximum (90 for a Root deck), so a 53-card starter has 32 of them, and the Nth costs 100 x 1.1^(N-1) rounded to the nearest 10: 100, 110, 120, up to 1920 for the last. A full win pays 490, so the first three slots cost 330 together and the whole 32-slot track is 20110, about 41 wins. An Aspect tier unlock lets the loadout add the next Aspect card of the starter's own character, one tier at a time from Aspect 1, at 100 for tier 2, 200 for tier 3, 400 for tier 4 and 800 for tier 5; the run's own Aspect grant then carries on from wherever the stack ends.
- **Against the AI:** Title → Duel the AI → pick your duelist and the AI's, and Easy, Normal or Hard in the footer. You sit at seat 1 and the AI's hand stays face down.
- **Online:** every online duel is refereed on the duel server (`docs/duel_server.md`). One player presses Host a duel, which opens a room on the server and shows a five-letter share code (copied to the clipboard); the other pastes it and presses Join. Both connect out to the server's fixed address, so nobody forwards ports. The select screen becomes the lobby: each client picks and locks in its own duelist, and the server sends neither client the other's deck until both have locked. Both then land on the matchup screen, and the server deals once both clients have shown it for three seconds. From the moment both have locked, the server refuses any change of pick, and the matchup screen has no Back. Find a duel, first under Online, needs no code: the client joins the server's casual queue, the title's online slot reads "Looking for an opponent" with the time waited and Cancel (and "Nobody else is looking right now." after 10 seconds), and the two who have waited longest are paired. Both land on the select screen with "Lock in within 0:59." left of Details, over Lock in, no code banner, no name field and Back to title for Back; nothing is ever picked for a player, so the line turns to "Lock in within 0:09 or you leave the queue." for its last 10 seconds, a seat still unlocked at 60 seconds is dropped with "You did not lock in a deck in time." and a locked one goes back to the front of the queue. Once both lock, the matchup reads "Starting in 5 seconds." and the server deals. A player who leaves before the deal sends the other back to the front of the queue and to the title, still waiting, its count going on from the first join and no notice shown. Strangers see each other by duelist name only, never by typed name. A queue duel is one game: its result offers Rematch while the other player is still there (dealt when both ask), Find another duel, which goes back into the queue on the same connection, and Title. Queue duels are casual and unrated, and the server drops anyone still waiting after 30 minutes (`docs/duel_server.md`, Find a duel). Ranked play is a second queue on the same server, never a share code: it pairs players whose ratings are within 3 mu, a window that widens by 3 mu every 30 seconds and opens to anyone after 90, into a best-of-3 match on the two locked decks. Between games the server waits 20 seconds, or less once both players are ready, and deals the next game on the same decks with a fresh seed, the loser of the last game opening. A second win decides the match, and so does a game lost by running out of time or by leaving, or a player leaving the match; Concede loses only the current game, Leave match the whole match, and there is no rematch or lobby. The match is rated once, with openskill's Plackett-Luce update ported to `Rating`; players see 40 times mu less three sigma, never below 0, marked provisional while sigma is above 6. A player who leaves a queue duel or dodges a ranked matchup gets a warning, then 2, 5, 15, 30 and 60 minutes out of both queues, cleared after a day without a leave (`docs/duel_server.md`, Ranked); the title counts that down as "You can queue again in 4:59." with Find a duel and Ranked match disabled (Host and Join stay open, since the cooldown covers only the queues). Ranked match sits under Find a duel on the title; its screens are under Client below. Neither client runs the rules or holds the seed; each receives only what its seat may see and sits at its own side of the table. When a game ends the server writes a signed record of it (seed, every command, result) before telling either player, then sends each a copy for `user://matches/online.jsonl`; offline duels keep unsigned records in `user://matches/local.jsonl` (`docs/duel_server.md`, Match records). Every decision in a server room runs on a clock (`DuelClock`): 30 seconds, 45 for a look through a pile, a pick of several cards or naming a card, and 60 for the Reserve swap at setup. The last 10 seconds turn the countdown warning orange with a fuse along the decision panel. Behind the timer is a time bank of 60 seconds at the deal plus 10 at each of the player's own turn starts, at most 120, which drains only once the timer is gone. Nothing is ever played for a player: when a decision's timer and the bank are both gone, that player loses the game at once, every decision including the Discard step. The Reserve swap and the Discard step run both players' clocks at once; the earlier to run out loses, and an exact tie ends the game with no winner. A clock starts when the client reports its decision panel open, or 10 seconds after the update went out. The waiting player's panel reads "Opponent deciding", and X's plate shows nothing while X is on the timer and "Time bank 0:48" once X draws on the bank. Hotseat, vs AI, adventure and the LAN dev path have no clock. A dropped connection does not end a server duel: the server keeps the seat for 90 seconds with its clock running, the other player sees "Disconnected 1:12" on X's plate and "X has 1:12 to come back." in the waiting panel, and the client that dropped shows "Connection lost" and "You lose if you are not back in 1:05." with Concede and tries again every 3 seconds, using the seat's token kept in `user://online/rejoin.json`. It comes back to the same table with this turn's log. A seat that does not come back in 90 seconds, or whose clock runs out while it is away, loses with "X left the duel."; with both players away both clocks stop, and after 90 seconds the game ends with no winner. A relaunched game offers "Your duel against X is still running." in the title's online slot, in place of Find a duel and Ranked match, with Rejoin and Concede, which asks "Concede the duel?" inline first (`docs/duel_server.md`, Reconnects). A client and the server must be the same build: the first message carries the online protocol number (9 at present, so the deployed server has to be rebuilt with `tools/server/deploy.ps1`, and the clients exported after it) and a hash of the card, deck and strike table data, and the server refuses a mismatch with a reason. Each install has an identity, an RSA key pair made on first use in `user://identity.key`: the first message also carries its public key, the server has it sign a nonce before letting it in, keeps its id on the seat and in the match record, and takes a rejoin or a give-up only from the identity that holds the seat (`docs/duel_server.md`, Identity). Traffic to the duel server runs over DTLS, and the client accepts only the server certificate pinned in `zenith/net/duel_server_cert` (`data/net/duel_server.crt`, which `deploy.ps1` copies down from the box); another certificate is refused on the title within a second with "The duel server's certificate is not the one this copy of the game trusts…", and a missing one with "This copy of the game is missing the duel server's certificate…" (`docs/duel_server.md`, Transport). The LAN dev path stays on plain ENet with no identity check. Find a duel, Host and Join give up connecting after 10 seconds, and Cancel on the title stops them sooner. Every failure ends on the title with a sentence saying why: the server cannot be reached or does not answer, the code is wrong or its room is full or already playing, the builds differ ("This copy of the game is out of date. Update it to play online."), the room closed, or the server dropped the connection. The title's status line holds two lines and cuts a longer reason short. If the other player leaves the lobby, the room and its code stay open for a new one; a room left with one player closes after 10 minutes. After a duel either player of a server room can ask for a rematch, which the server deals once both have asked, or take both back to the lobby in the same room, and either can concede during a duel. Typing an address (`host:port`) instead of a code connects to a client hosting on a plain ENet port, the LAN dev path, in debug builds only. Transports live in `scripts/net/` behind `NetTransport`, so a Steam transport would slot in beside them.

## Engine

`DuelEngine` runs the duel as a state machine. It advances on its own until a player has to decide something, then exposes a `Prompt` whose `options` are the only `Command`s it accepts. `submit(command)` applies one and runs on to the next prompt or the end of the game. Everything that happens is appended to `events` as `GameEvent`s for the client to animate and log. Same seed plus same commands replays the same game.

```gdscript
var engine: DuelEngine = DuelEngine.new()
engine.setup([deck_a, deck_b], library, strike_table, seed_value)
engine.start()
while not engine.is_over():
	var choice: Command = engine.prompt.options[0]   # a driver picks here
	engine.submit(choice)
```

Prompt kinds: `reserve` (reserve_in / reserve_done), `non_combat` (place / relic / done), `declare` (declare / skip), `attack_action` (attack, with value `"empower"` for the Empowered version / use / power / copied_attack / final_strike / pass), `respond` (counter / decline), `defense` (defend / power_defend / no_defense), `control` (context `role` attacker or defender), `redirect` (target), `endurance` (endure / no_endure), `capture_instead` (capture / deal_damage, for an attacking Ally with the capture trait), `critical` (capture / discard_ally / lower_fervor / no_critical), `keep` (keep / discard_all), `recover` (recover / no_recover), `pay` (value = Energy paid), `discard_choice`, `pick_in_play` (+ pick_none), `name_card` (value = title), `pick_option`. A prompt raised by a card's effect carries `source` (the card's uid) and `card_title` in its context, and a `pick_option` also says what the pick is for (`purpose`: forbid_type, stop_kind, look_at, capture), so titles name the card asking and the client can show it.

Batches: a prompt that picks several cards (`reserve`, a discard of two or more, a search or pick-in-play for several) also sets `batch_type`, `batch_min`, `batch_max` on the `Prompt` and `PromptView`. One Command of that type with `value` = an Array of card uids taken from the options answers it in a single step; `Prompt.accept` normalises and checks it, and the referee refuses anything outside the options, repeats, or the wrong count. Clients build it with `PromptView.batch_option(uids)`. The one-card options stay legal, so drivers that pick one at a time still work.

Effects run through a queue that pauses on a choice prompt and resumes afterwards, so a card can ask its owner or the target to pick cards mid-resolution.

A hand Combat card is an attack-phase `use` only when playing it does something (it has secondary effects). A pure counter ("use when needed", `counter: "combat"`) is offered only in the `respond` window that opens when the other player uses or defends with a Combat card; in its owner's own attack phase it can only be thrown away for a Final Strike.

**Log.** Every state change is an event with a line from `CardText.event_line`, so the log tells the whole story and not only the players' commands: a card whose trigger fires gets "<card> triggers when entering Combat" (once, at the first effect that actually applies, never for a card the player just played or used), every Energy change from an effect names its source card and the before and after, forbids and other standing effects read as they land ("may not perform Arts for the rest of Combat"), flags such as a skipped attack phase or a forced Combat are announced when set, and wounds outside an attack's own damage, removals from the game, looks at the deck and bonds each have a line. Lines are worded per seat (`event_line(ev, engine, seat)`): a card is named only if that seat may see it now or is the player who acted, so the opponent of a draw check learns whether it matched and that the effect follows, never which card was drawn, and a Reserve swap or a search into hand reads "a card" on the other side.

**Seat views.** Clients never read the engine. `Referee` owns it and answers each seat with a `SeatUpdate`: the log lines for what happened, a `SeatView` (public standings, every zone as uids, and a card table where cards that seat may not see carry only uid and zone: Life Decks, the other seat's hand and Reserve), and a `PromptView` when the next decision is that seat's (kind, title, options with ready-made labels). The engine holds at most one prompt per player (`DuelEngine.prompts`, `prompt_of(seat)`; `prompt` is the first): the Reserve swap and the Discard step ask both players at once, so nobody waits on the other for a choice that only concerns their own cards, and everything else is one prompt at a time. `Referee.submit(seat, command)` refuses anything that is not that seat's own pending option. Everything has `to_dict` / `from_dict` so the same objects cross the wire. Card uids are dealt after the shuffle so a face-down uid says nothing about the card.

**Attack in the air.** `SeatView.attack` carries the public shape of the current attack plus `damage`, the breakdown from `DuelEngine.damage_breakdown`: the Strike Table lookup (both Mights and bands, or the Art base, or printed numbers), then `adds`, one entry per card that changes it (Drills, Mastery, Grounds, attachments, floating modifiers, Empower, paid Energy), and the totals. Before the defense it is the forecast the defender is answering; once `landed` is set the battle sequence has fixed the same numbers, and `stages_dealt`, `life_dealt` and `life_remaining` follow the flips. `damage_breakdown` also returns `overflow` (Energy past what the target is standing on) and `wounds` (`life` plus that overflow), because the deal path turns overflow into life cards and a forecast that ignored it read a costly Strike as costing nothing. The HUD prompt panel leads a damage decision with one large number, the wound total, shown only when there is one (an attack the target can absorb falls back to the tiles rather than a large zero), and `OptionView.outcome` (`DuelEngine.option_outcome`, filled for `endurance` and `defense`) says what each choice would leave: hovering a button or a legal card redraws the number at that value with the change beside it, so "what does this buy me" is answered by the number rather than a sentence. Prompts with no single number to lead with fall back to at most three tiles (`DuelHud._facts_for`). The step-by-step maths stays in the log rather than the panel, which gets a "Strike Table: Might 19 (C) vs 9 (B) gives 2 stages." line and a "Modifiers: +4 stages (Ashmark's Relentless Fury). Total 8 stages." line per attack. A Final Strike is a legal option on every hand card, so the client never fires it from a bare card click: it sits behind a "Final Strike…" button that opens the hand in the tray, and a card whose only action is a Final Strike opens the sub-choice tray instead of committing.

**Outcome.** `SeatView.battle_step` says where the battle sequence stands while an attack is in the air, and `SeatView.last_attack` keeps the outcome of the last attack of the Combat after the attack itself has left the view: who attacked, with what (`source_title`, `performer_title`), whether it was stopped and by what (`stopped_by` is `{card, how}` with `how` card / power / shield / floating, `stopped_by_title` the wording), `stages_dealt`, `life_dealt`, `target_title`, `endurance_prevented`, and the critical-damage choice. It clears when the next attack is declared or Combat ends. The log gets the same tally as one line at `attack_end` ("Bram's Overhand Cut lands for 3 stages, 2 wounds." / "… is stopped by Tide Parry."), a "Combat: Bram attacks first." line at `combat_begin`, and a line when a defense is skipped for a reason the player should know (no legal defense, or a Final Strike this Combat).

**Attack forecasts.** While a seat's attack action is pending, `SeatView.forecasts` (only that seat's view) carries a breakdown per offered attack, keyed by the option's card uid: hand and Remain cards, a Power attack under the personality's uid, and a bare Final Strike for a card with no attack of its own (`is_final`). `DuelEngine.attack_forecasts` builds each one the way declaring it would (`_build_attack`: the card's conditional lines, the attacker's standing effects) and runs the same `_damage_calc`, after paying the attack's Energy cost so the Strike Table is read at the right stage (`cost_stages` travels with it); an Empower option adds its totals under `empowered`. The hand shows each card's total as a chip ("6 Energy", "4 wounds"; "Final: 2 Energy" in grey for a card that can only be thrown away), and the quick view lists the steps under the face, so a "+3 Energy" card is compared by what it would land for, not its printed bonus. Faces carry the printed base in a badge over the art ("Strike +3 Energy", "Strike Table Energy", "Art 4 wounds", "Focused Strike 7 Energy"), from `CardText.attack_badge`. `CardText.breakdown_steps` is the one wording for a breakdown's steps (the log and the quick view alike).

**Event data for animation.** `SeatUpdate.lines` entries carry `type`, `player` and the seat's `line`; for the event types in `Referee.ANIMATED` they also carry `data` with only the public fields listed there (card uids that reached a pile, damage numbers, the attack's kind and source), the same for both seats. Anything not listed reaches clients as its line alone, so nothing hidden leaks through the animation channel.

The quiet beats are animated too, so the table does not stand still through a step that nobody had to answer: `turn_start` and `turn_end` (`turn`), `recover_step` (`eligible`), `combat_declared` (`forced`), `combat_skipped` (`forced`, `reason` grounds / forbidden), `entering_combat` (`role` active / opposing), `pass` (`forced`, `consecutive`), `attack_phase_skipped`, `no_defense` (`auto`, `reason` none / standing / final_strike / countered, and `auto` false when the defender chose it), `declined_counter`, `control` (`card`), `power_used` (`card`, `aspect`) and `relic_used` (`card`). A window that opened on nothing emits `window_skipped` (`window`: respond, entering_combat, after_damage, combat_end, late_stop) rather than closing in silence, the same rule a swallowed effect follows.

They also carry `state`: the table numbers as they stood *when that event fired* (`GameEvent.state` — `energy` per personality uid, `fervor` per player, `zones` as [life deck, hand, discard, removed] per player, and the turn position (`step`, `phase`, `active`, `attacker`)). A beat is stamped with the phase it belongs to, not the one the engine moves to next: `attack_declared` is emitted before the phase leaves ATTACK, and the hand-over between exchanges emits `fight_back` (`next`, the seat that attacks now) while the phase is still FIGHT_BACK, so a client's Combat tracker sees Attack, Defend and Battle in order instead of sitting on one chip. The stamp's `attacker` is `GameState.display_attacker()`, the active player through the prepare phases (the state's own `attacker` keeps the last Combat's value until the opposing draw). `combat_begin` carries `attacker`; `fight_back` is stamped before the engine flips the attacker, so the client takes the next attacker from its `next` for that beat. Turn numbers, attack counts and battle-step progress are not sent to clients: nothing on screen uses them. The phase track reads FIGHT_BACK as Attack. One update's view is the state after everything in it, so without this a client drew the end state under every beat: a duelist that charged to full Energy and was drained again in the same update never appeared charged. `DuelEngine.record_display_state` stamps the events; `Referee.setup` turns it on for its own engine and `clone()` leaves it off, so the AI's simulations never pay for it. `duel_view._play_update` hands each beat's `state` to the HUD, the panels, the markers and the phase banner, and clears it at the end, so nothing on screen runs ahead of the beat under it.

**Pending queue.** `SeatView.pending` is what resolves next, first element first, built by `DuelEngine.pending_items()` and masked per seat by `SeatView._mask_pending`. It **is** an ordering contract: the engine works through it top to bottom, so a client can draw it in order, which the duel HUD does as the pile of faces over the Focus slot. Each item is `{kind, uid, title, owner, target, note, current}` with `target` -1 when there is none and exactly one item carrying `current`. Kinds, in the order they appear: `attack` (the attack in the air, `uid` its source card, `target` the defender's controlling personality, `note` the damage total such as "5 Energy"), `pending_card` (`state.pending_play`, the announced card in its counter window), `trigger` (one per job in the effect queue, `note` the trigger's wording; a `then` list inserted at the front of the queue reads first, because that is where the engine runs it), `hidden` (a job whose source this seat may not see: `owner` and nothing else, so the shape of the queue is public and the card is not) and `wounds` (what the life-damage loop still has to flip, `note` "N wounds"). `SeatView` also carries `consecutive_passes`, `attack_phase_count` and `combat_count`, all public counts, so a client can say "one more pass ends Combat" without holding a rule of its own.

**Effective values.** A `SeatPlayer` carries every per-player number the rules currently apply, computed by `DuelEngine` getters (`fervor_needed`, `fervor_gain`, `recover_gain`, `fervor_shield`, `aspect_shield`, `restrictions`) from base state plus standing effects: a Mastery that demands 6 Fervor of its opponent, a forbid in force. Clients render those fields and never a rules constant, so a card that moves one moves the display in the same update. `PlayerState.fervor_needed` is the adjustable base behind the first of these (ops `fervor_needed` and `set_fervor_needed`); the opponent's Mastery demand is layered on top, the higher wins.

A `SeatPlayer` also carries the fighter's readout against its printed baseline: `energy` and `might` for the personality in control, `energy_printed` / `might_printed` for what the card prints at the Energy that personality takes the field on (`DuelEngine.STARTING_ENERGY` for a Duelist, `ALLY_STARTING_ENERGY` for an Ally), and `energy_delta` / `might_delta` as effective minus printed. A client colours the number green above the baseline and red below without doing rules maths. Nothing in the rules modifies a printed Might, so Might moves only with the Aspect and the Energy stage (`CardInstance.might_at`), which is why `GameEvent.state` stamps the effective `might` alone and carries no printed one beside it.

Covered: Reserve swap at setup, Double Power first-player rule, 7-step turn, 6-phase Combat, 15-step battle sequence, Strike Table, Arts with costs and cost discounts, overflow to wounds, Endurance (fixed and X), Defense Shields, Focused and unstoppable attacks, unpreventable damage, prevent-all, floating stop-all and stop-next, Fervor and Aspects (set, lose, advance, threshold changes, Fervor and aspect shields), Allies (placement, overlay, control at any stage, redirect, protection), Drills with school lock, modifiers, shields, once-per-Combat uses and Drill protection, Seals (placement, bypass, capture, capture effects, both win paths), Grounds with standing forbids, Masteries (modifiers, entering-Combat, on-success, use), Relics with per-game powers and passives, Final Strike, Remain, Empower, counter window for Combat cards, attack variants ("if X is in control, +3"), per-Ally and Surge-scaled damage, pay-any-Energy attacks, attachments (modifiers, entering-Combat draws, wound removal), constant powers (focused attacks, first styled attack unstoppable, on-attack, turn-start, shared with Allies), named-card locks, copied attacks, forbids by card type / attack type / powers / Mastery / Drills / end-Combat / stop-all / Seals / everything-but-attacking, searches by type, school, title, tag, signature, or Reserve into hand or play, start-in-play Drills, hand discards chosen by either player or shuffled back.

Also covered: "you may" prompts on any effect line with a `then` list that runs only on a yes, pay-any-Energy effects with a per-unit follow-up, look-at-top-or-bottom-N with a pick, searches that let the searcher choose among distinct hits, instead-of-damage choices after a successful attack, attacks that need two stops, Ally powers usable without control, Drills that promote "if successful" text or discard themselves, Remain cards that shuffle back when unused, returning removed cards, forbids with an Energy escape clause, forced Combat declarations.

And: Bonds (two named Allies fold under a Bond card that fights as one at full Energy, burning a life card each turn until it ends), wound triggers on life cards ("if this card is discarded from your Life Deck…", optionally delayed to the next fight-back phase), look-at with a play-instead option and reshuffle, searches by what a card does (`has_effect`), an end-turn effect, and a response window during the opponent's Declare step (`opponent_declare` trigger on a Non-Combat in play).

Also: damage multipliers (one at most, the biggest applies) and caps on modifiers (`multiply`, `cap_stages`, `cap_life`; caps bite at deal time), attacks that cannot be reduced (`no_reduce` on the attack, or a floating `no_reduce`), Seals immune to card effects that do not name them, cards that end Combat usable only as an attack action, "X only" attachments that go on X wherever X stands (`attach` with `to: "character"`, playable while X is in play; `to: "named"` with `character` attaches to a different named personality, needs X in control and the named one on the table, and `attachment.limit_attached` caps copies per host), an unplayable drawn school Drill shown and shuffled back (`shuffle_back` option in the Non-Combat step), and look-at effects that put the looked-at cards back in a chosen order (`rearrange: true`).

Timing windows, audited against the reference rulebook and rulings on 2026-09-16: a spent Duelist (Energy 0 or 1) may hand the attack phase to an Ally at the start of it (`control` prompt with `role: attacker`), and resumes control once above that; a Non-Combat in play used as a defense is spent; after a Final Strike the player cannot defend either (Shields and standing stops still apply); a skipped attack phase never happened, so the passes around it are not consecutive; a Relic's power is offered where its `relic_step` says (`non_combat`, the default, `combat` for in place of an attack, or `any`); an attacking Ally with the capture trait may capture a Seal instead of dealing damage (battle step 11); the Recover step is announced every turn (`recover_step` event) and the card returns only when Combat was skipped.

Not yet: Use-when-needed windows outside the Combat-card counter and the Declare window (no starter card needs one), choosing the order of Defense Shields, of "if successful" effects, or of one's own entering-Combat effects (they resolve in printed or source order), the free Ally takeover when the opponent uses a non-attack card, trying an any-type stop against a Focused attack for its secondary effects, the replacement effects the design lists (draw from the discard pile, keep cards in the Discard step, alternative costs, blank text boxes), and Restricted Drills. A card with both an attack and a utility mode can only attack; no starter card has one.

## AI

One AI plays every deck. Nothing in `ai/` names a card or a deck; it reads card data and table state.

The integrated sequence planner, tuning controls, diagnostics, and behavioral tests are documented in [AI planning](docs/ai_planning.md).

- **`AiProfile`** is the data: weights for the AI's own side (`own`), the opponent's (`foe`), effect ops (`effect`), play costs (`play`), and how hard it thinks (`think`: `search`, `top_k`, `samples`, `budget_ms`, `max_steps`, `turns`, `noise`, `prior`). Defaults live in `AiProfile.DEFAULTS`; a JSON file under `data/ai/profiles/` overrides only what it lists. A deck's playstyle is a profile: raise `own.seal` for a Seal deck, `own.ascension` for an Ascension deck, `own.ally` for an Ally deck. `play.declare_bias` decides whether to open Combat, added to the number of attacks in hand and weighed against a flat 1.0 for skipping; set it below any reachable attack count and the deck only ever fights as the defender, which is what the Root Seal deck does. `play.declare_use` (off by default) adds what the hand's Combat-only cards are worth to that same decision, for a deck that has to open Combat to spend them. The declare prompt is answered by the scorer at every difficulty (`think.scorer_kinds`, default `["declare"]`), at the table and inside the search's own plans, since a search that ends at this turn's discard step cannot see the Combats a stance is about.
- **Matchup pivots.** A profile's `vs` block changes how it plays by what the deck across the table declares itself to be, so one deck can turtle into a beatdown and press into a slow deck. Keys are an `Archetype` id (`strike_beatdown`, `seals`, `allies`, `drills`, `art_beatdown`) or `+<subtheme>` for one of its subthemes; each value is a partial profile laid over the base. The archetype applies first and subthemes after, in the order the deck declares them, so the finer statement wins; weights a pivot does not name are untouched. `AiProfile.for_matchup` returns a copy and never edits the base. `AiPlayer` resolves it once on its first decision, since a declared archetype is fixed at setup, and both seats do it, so it holds for AI against AI. Archetype and subthemes are shown to both players, so this reads nothing private; inside a playout the opponent is still modelled on the default profile, pivoted against **our** archetype, because how they weigh things is not on the table.
- **`AiEvaluator.evaluate(engine, seat, profile)`** scores a position. Each win route is its own term: Life Decks (survival), Fervor and aspects (Ascension), Seals of one set (Seal win), plus Energy, Might band, hand, Allies, Drills, Non-Combats, attachments and forbids. The opponent's side is scored with `foe` weights, so a beatdown AI still fears six Seals. A Drill that guards Seals turns capture off outright, so it scores above a plain Drill on its owner's side and as a target on the other (`own.seal_guard`, `foe.seal_guard`); `AiScorer.hold_value` gives it the same edge in hand. Energy the next Power Up would refill anyway counts half (`held_energy`), cards past the discard-step limit count half on their owner's turn (`kept_hand`), and standing modifiers a side has put out are priced per attack they are expected to touch (`standing_modifier_value`, weight `standing`).
- **Grounds** bind both players, so `AiEvaluator.grounds_value` scores them by whose cards they help and hinder: the AI's whole deck against what the opponent has shown in play, discard and removed piles. It reads the Grounds' data (forbids, doubled costs, modifiers, searches), never their id. A Fervor cap binds both players rather than any one card, so it is scored apart from the per-card fit: it is worth the difference between how much the opponent's profile wants to climb and how much ours does. The evaluator counts the Grounds in play, and the scorer rates placing new Grounds as new minus old, less the Combat that placing them gives up (`play.grounds_skip`). Weights: `own.grounds`, `foe.grounds`. A Grounds that taxes one kind of attack (`strike_cost_delta`, `art_cost_delta`) hurts exactly the decks built on that kind, so it is scored per card like a forbid.
- **`AiReserve`** decides the Reserve swap. It reads the opponent's duelist, Mastery and Relic, the cards both players show before the swap, for signs of an Ally, Drill, Non-Combat or Seal deck and of a deck that sits on its lowest aspect (`read_setup`: what those cards fetch or shield, never what they aim at the opponent). A Reserve card that removes a kind of card comes in when the opponent shows that kind and stays out otherwise; one that hits both sides is charged for my own cards of that kind; one that raises both players' Fervor is for aspect-sitters and costs an Ascension deck its win; a plain strong card comes in when it beats the average Life Deck card, since each swap pushes a random card out. Attacks stay in the Reserve when the main deck has a card that fetches from it, unless the opponent is what they answer. Cards that start in play from the Reserve, Bond cards and cards the duelist cannot use never come in. Profile group `reserve`: `tech`, `threshold`, `toolbox_keep`, `max_swaps` (Tide sets 0). `tests/ai_reserve_report.gd` prints every deck's read and every Reserve card's score against every opponent.
- **`AiScorer`** scores each option of the pending prompt with no simulation, from `attack_forecasts`, `damage_breakdown` and effect ops. It supplies move ordering, bounded-search fallback, and short simulated continuations. Scorer-only play is an explicit benchmark policy. An option type it does not know scores 0, the same as "do nothing". An effect is worth every branch it opens (`AiScorer.branches`): `then` in full, and a check's `effects` and `else_effects` as an even call. An effect either side may take is worth its better aim. An attack also counts the Mastery's `on_attack` and `on_success` lines its kind and school meet. A held attack is valued from `DuelEngine.printed_base`, a plain Strike at the equal-band table result. Paying life or a card for damage, and burning the discard pile against a hit, are priced as wounds gained or prevented against the card spent.
- **`AiSearch`** explores alternative sequences over shared sampled worlds. Candidate selection preserves different mechanical roles. Iterative deepening retains the last fully compared root round; hypothetical state pivots update the continuation policy while leaf evaluation keeps a common scale. Opponent replies create weighted successor worlds, regrouped by the AI's own observations before it chooses again. Legal semantic plan hints preserve intention across prompts and are re-evaluated after disruption. When the budget buys only one completed ply and it disagrees with the scorer by less than `think.trust_margin` (default 1.0), the scorer's pick stands. `last_report` records alternatives and representative continuations; `metrics` reports budget use, completed depth, intent reuse, cutoffs, and evaluation terms. `think.algorithm = "rollout"` retains the historical root-only search for arena comparisons.
- **`AiPlayer.choose(referee, seat)`** returns the wire form of one of the prompt's options, or `{}` when the decision is not that seat's.
- **Fair play.** Simulations originate at `Referee.sim_for`. Own known composition and all currently revealed cards are preserved; unknown placement and future randomness change. Undisclosed opponent composition is sampled from a broad library prior compatible with public declarations, rather than copied from the true hidden list. Opponent response models pass through the same boundary in the opposite perspective. Tests preserve exact real/public views, prompts, and authoritative state. The prior does not yet learn archetype frequencies or retain a complete history of earlier reveals.
- **Cost.** Easy allows 200 ms and 750 simulated submissions with one initial sample; Normal uses two samples, 1,600 ms and 6,000 submissions; Hard allows three samples, 4,000 ms and 16,000 submissions. Budgets are checked inside search, including response expansion and rollouts. An individual engine operation cannot be interrupted, so small time overruns remain possible. Fixed node limits with `budget_ms = 0` provide deterministic regression runs. Exact transposition caching is available but off by default because serialization cost can outweigh its benefit. The client continues to run AI on a worker task.

## Client

- `duel_view.gd` renders one seat's `SeatView` and nothing else. Hotseat and hosting keep a `Referee` in the scene; after every command it plays the seat's `SeatUpdate` into the log and into table tweens, then shows the next prompt. A `Card3D` exists per uid the view lists, with a face only when the view carries a definition. Any card decision can be made by clicking the card (in the 2D hand or on the table) or by the button list in the prompt panel, which always lists every legal option. `DuelHud.BUTTON_KINDS` forces a kind's options into the panel buttons even when they name a card, for a yes-or-no about a card that is already in a pile (Endurance); without it those options fall out of both the button list and the tray and the prompt has no visible answer.

- The focus view (`Root/Focus` in `hud.tscn`, `DuelHud.show_focus`) holds the card a decision is about at readable size in the middle of the screen, captioned by what it is ("Incoming", "The card you just lost", "Asking"): the attack in the air, a prompt's `context.card`, or the card whose effect raised the prompt. Both seats see it, the one deciding and the one waiting, it takes no mouse input so the table underneath stays clickable, and it steps aside for the tray and the inspect overlay. The same slot, at the same rect, is what a replay beat pins a card into (`DuelHud.show_replay_card`, caption moved on with `DuelHud.set_focus_caption`), so a declared attack pinned through its exchange and the defense prompt's own focus card are one card that never jumps. `Root/Focus/Stack` (`DuelHud.push_response(def, caption, owner, uid)` / `pop_response(uid_or_index)` / `clear_stack()`) is the stack of cards answering that attack, laid over it inside the same rect and clipped to it: each response is a face at `STACK_SCALE` 0.8 of the Focus face, stepped `STACK_STEP` (-26, -34) up and to the left per level with an alternating `STACK_TILT` 2.5° tilt, newest on top, each carrying a caption strip on its bottom edge and a border in its owner's role colour (attack red, defend blue). Because the stack costs the decision column no room, it stays up while a prompt is open, so the seat being asked to respond sees the state it is answering. `pop_response` is how the beat that resolves a card takes it off again, over `STACK_LEAVE` 0.25 s, and it does nothing for a card the stack never held. The slot and the stack are the only place anything pending is drawn: there is no second column anywhere else. `DuelHud.refresh_state` reconciles the stack against `SeatView.pending` every beat (`_reconcile_pending`). The anchor in the Focus slot is the `attack` item when there is one, otherwise whatever resolves first; every other job is a face stacked over it, pushed in reverse list order so the one resolving next is on top, with a `trigger` carrying the strip "Trigger" and its owner's tint, a `hidden` job carrying a card back reading "Opponent's trigger", and a `wounds` job carrying no face at all, because it is the attack's own loop and reads as "· 3 wounds to resolve" on the anchor's caption. Faces the queue dealt are keyed (`PENDING_KEY`) so a refresh can take one off again with the same leaving animation when its job is gone and no beat popped it, and a replay beat naming a card already on the pile only renames that face instead of dealing a second copy. Beyond `STACK_MAX` 4 faces the jobs furthest from resolving give up their faces for a "+N" badge on the top one. An emptied queue with no pinned attack clears the slot. `DuelHud.focus_uid()` still names the pinned card (`show_focus`'s uid, or the one `show_replay_card` was given). `Root/FocusFilament` then runs the line to the target from the pinned card itself: from the left edge of the Focus face, or of the topmost stacked response when that response is the job resolving now, to `DuelView.screen_anchor` of the current pending item's target, in the attack colour, with a transverse cap when `attack.stopped` and a double chevron once `landed`, redrawn every frame so it follows the camera. It hides when nothing is pinned or nothing is aimed anywhere; the 3D link between the two cards on the table is unchanged.
- Combat beats: `_play_update` replays each event line that carries `data` as its own beat (`_replay`) against the update's final layout before the closing sync. An attack raises its card to the Play slot, lunges the attacker's personality at the defender's with a streak and sparks (`duel_fx.gd`, the `Fx` node: floating text, bursts, slashes, rings, all primitive meshes freed when their tween ends), shows a banner (`DuelHud.toast`) naming it, and pins its public face in the focus slot for `ATTACK_READ` 2.2 s (`ATTACK_READ_OWN` 1.0 s for the viewer's own), which is held even when the defender has nothing and `no_defense` follows at once; the pinned card stays there for the whole exchange, its caption following it to "No defense · …", "Stopped", "Hits for …" and "Dealt …", and is released `FOCUS_RELEASE` 0.6 s after `attack_end`; a card that answers it is pushed onto the stack over it and holds on top for `OPPONENT_DEFENSE_READ` 2.2 s (`OPPONENT_STOP_READ` 3.2 s for a stop, `ANSWER_READ_OWN` 1.0 s for the viewer's own), then leaves at the beat that resolves it — a countered card at `countered` and the counter itself after its hold, a defense at `attack_stopped` or at `attack_successful` when it did not stop the attack, a Shield at its `shield` beat, an Endurance at `endurance_used`; a card used or a trigger fired outside an attack holds the slot for `CARD_USE_READ` 0.8 s when it carries rules text; a trigger whose card is already anchored in the slot or stacked over it (`DuelHud.pulse_pending`) is lit and read where it stands for that same hold and then leaves the pile, so only a card that is nowhere on the right still gets the table's spotlight hop; a defense rings the defender in blue; "Hits for 8 Energy, 1 wound" goes on the pinned card's caption; an Energy hit lands as a jab from the attacker that holds on the contact frame for the hit's weight (`hit_tier`: chip, solid, heavy), then shakes and flashes the target with "-5 Energy" over it, a heavy hit punches the camera once (`TableCamera.kick`, off under Reduced motion), and Energy that spills over slides "+N wounds" to the Life Deck; a stop knocks the attacker back; each flipped life card arcs from the Life Deck to the discard face up (`_fly`) with "Wound N" and a banner naming what it cost, held half a second so a run of wounds reads one at a time; Endurance used and Endurance declined both banner their outcome, so the attacker sees the answer to a choice it watched being offered; Seal capture, aspect changes, Energy and Fervor changes all get their number over the card. A card about to resolve holds the table first (`_spotlight`: it lifts, rings gold and names itself), and an effect that lands elsewhere pulses its source card, so a chain of effects reads as a sequence of cards acting rather than the table changing by itself. Discards and removals arc to their pile as their event plays instead of sliding in the sync. A card the exchange uses (the attack, a defense, a counter, a card used mid-Combat) is held face up in its owner's Play slot (`DuelView._held`, which `_targets` places and `_fly` leaves alone) until its part is over, even when the update's final view already has it in a pile or face down in the Life Deck, wearing the face its event's `id` named; it then flies to where the view put it, the attack at `attack_end` together with the Focus pin, a defense at `attack_stopped` / `attack_successful`, a counter or a used card after its read hold, and a hold outlives the update it started in and clears at `combat_end` or game over. While an attack is in the air the two personalities in control carry a role glow under the card (attacker red, defender blue, `Card3D.set_role`; `Body` under the card holds the quads so shakes and lunges never fight the table tween). Every window the rules opened on nothing gets a quiet beat of its own (a pass, a skipped attack phase, no defense, a declined counter, a response window nobody could answer, the turn and Recover boundaries), so nothing happens in silence; `control`, `power_used` and `relic_used` spotlight their card. Changes of hands get a hand-over banner (`DuelView._handover`, at least 0.4 s however busy the queue): Combat declared ("COMBAT · X attacks first" when the first attacker arrives in the same update), `combat_begin` otherwise, `fight_back` ("X attacks"), `combat_end` ("Combat over") and `turn_start`. Every banner is one ribbon across the ring (`DuelHud.show_banner`, tiers HANDOVER / OUTCOME / QUIET). The table dims part way for the whole Combat and fully while an attack is in the air, the role glows follow the beat's own stamp, and the wound that empties a Life Deck lands heavier and holds, as does `game_over` before the result covers the table. Pacing scales with the work queued: `SeatView.pending` at three items shortens every beat to 0.6, at six to 0.35, never below 0.05 s, except the holds that exist so a card can be read (`_read_beat`), which keep their authored length because a busy Combat is when a card most needs its time; four or more triggers in a row announce "Resolving N triggers" and run at a glance; three or more skipped response windows in one update drop their beats; and holding **Space** through a replay runs it at the floor (never while a prompt is addressed to the viewer, and never tied to any turn timer). `Engine.time_scale` (`--dev-fast`) speeds every beat.
- `table_layout.gd` turns zone names into table slots. Each zone owns its own rectangle on the board: Allies, Drills, Seals, Non-Combats and Remain as rows. The two Duelists meet nose to nose across the centre line, each with its Mastery beside it as a second, smaller hero, level with it; each seat's Ally wing runs left of its duelist and its Drill wing right of the Mastery, both against the centre line; the Non-Combat row lies under the Drills with the Relic beside it and Out under the Relic; the Life Deck stands over the Discard (three quarters of a pile card, tight outline) at the board's right edge; the Seals run along the far left with Remain under them; the stat plate lies under the Ally wing (`Plate` marker). The centre band holds the cards in play (small, the HUD rail shows them), the Grounds on the viewer's left (`GROUNDS_POS`) and the phase track on the right. A seat's status chips and Seal sets print along its Ally row, the row fewest decks use (`DuelView._flag_row`, `DuelistReadout.flag_home`), and fall back to the plate's outer side while an Ally is in play. Nothing off-field sits in a screen-edge panel. Discard, Out and the Relic are piles: a card-sized outline and a caption stay on the board when empty, the caption carries the count (the Relic's is its Reserve count), the Reserve lies face down under the Relic with its edges fanned, and a click on any pile card or on the bare felt (`TableLayout.pile_clicked`) opens the pile browser (`DuelHud.show_pile`, zones `discard`, `removed`, `relic`; the Relic pile lists the Relic first, then the Reserve, and hidden Reserve cards are counted, not drawn). A pile card the pending decision offers stays a choice. A Relic that can be used now keeps the legal-card frame, and a click on its pile opens a small choice, "Use it" or "Inspect Reserve" (`DuelHud.show_relic_choice`); an unusable Relic's pile opens the browser straight away. `--dev-pile=mine|theirs[:removed|:relic]` shows a browser for a screenshot. The outlines and labels are drawn from the same markers and slot counts, and `_assert_no_overlap` keeps every zone and the plate clear of the others and inside `TableLayout.BOARD`, which the home framing fills. Rows squeeze past their slot count instead of spilling into a neighbour. Player 1's slots mirror player 0's; every card and label turns to read upright for the current viewer.
- `card_face_cache.gd` draws each card face once into a `SubViewport` and caches the texture. Faces are procedural (`card_face.tscn`, 512 x 716) in two layouts. Duelists and Allies get a portrait: aspect box and name across the top, art down the left, the ten-stage Might ladder down the right with the Surge badge under it, and the aspect's power text along the bottom. Every other card gets title, type chip, an art box of a fixed height for its type (`CardFace.ART_HEIGHTS`: 320 px for Strikes, Arts and Seals, 300 for Combat, 240 for Non-Combats, Drills and Grounds, 220 for Masteries, 200 for Relics), an Energy cost badge over the art, the rules text in the fixed box that remains, and Endurance under it. Boxes never move between cards of one type; the text steps its font down from 24 to 12 px to fit. Art comes from `assets/card_art/<id>.png` when it exists, covered and cropped to the box.
- Rules text comes from `CardText` in the engine (`rules_text` for cards, `aspect_text` for a personality aspect with its Power, Constant powers and Defense Shield). Effects that share a trigger and condition fold into one sentence; a forbid or board clear that hits both players reads once. `CardText.KEYWORDS` lists every keyword with a regex, a colour role and a tooltip; `scripts/ui/keyword_text.gd` turns plain text into BBCode with those colours (ink on the cream face, light on the dark HUD) and `keyword_label.gd` is the `RichTextLabel` that shows the tooltip on hover. Shorthand on the face: `Hit:` is "if the attack is successful", `Remain N` is "stays in play for N more uses this Combat"; each is a keyword with its own tooltip. Hovering any card (hand, tray, or table) shows a quick view of its face at about half size beside the pointer; it follows the mouse and never takes clicks. Right-click any card or press Inspect in a card's action choice to bring it up in the centre at full size over a dimmed table; that face is live, so hovering a keyword on it explains it. Esc or a click outside closes it.
- The table camera (`table_camera.gd` on the duel scene's Camera) moves without changing its angle: the mouse wheel dollies toward the spot under the pointer, middle-drag or WASD / arrow keys pan along the table, and the look point is kept on the table by a margin that grows with height, so a zoomed-out view stays centred while a close view can roam to the edges. After four seconds without any input it glides back to the home framing; hand-offs and the hotseat swing send it home at once. Key panning is ignored while a text field has focus.
- Duelist numbers live on the duelist card, not in the HUD. `status_markers.gd` sits under each personality `Card3D`: a pulsing bar over the Might ladder rung for the current Energy stage (one step below the ladder, in the warning colour, at 0), and one Fervor pip per point the duelist currently needs along its top edge (the row is rebuilt when `fervor_needed` changes). Rung positions come from the face layout (`CardFace.ladder_rects`, cached by `CardFaceCache`). The player panels show only seat data: Life Deck, hand, discard, removed, Reserve, Seals, and standing flags, including any effective value that differs from the printed rules ("Needs 6 Fervor", "Fervor shielded", "No Art attacks"). - Online API for the duel scene: `Net.concede()`, `Net.rematch()` and `Net.back_to_lobby()` work for either seat of a server room and for a LAN host, which is what `Net.can_rematch()` answers; a LAN joiner leaves rematch and the lobby to its host. A concession arrives on both sides as `Net.duel_ended(winner_seat, "concede")`, a clock run out as `Net.duel_ended(winner_seat, "timeout")` or, for an exact tie, `Net.duel_ended(-1, "abandoned")`, a player who dropped and did not come back as `Net.duel_ended(winner_seat, "left")`, the other seat's rematch request as `Net.rematch_requested(seat)`, the other player leaving as `Net.peer_left` (this client stays connected and seated), the other player's connection dropping mid-duel as `Net.peer_away(seat, grace_ms)` and their return as `Net.peer_back(seat)` (`Net.away_left_ms(seat)` counts the grace down, -1 while they are here), and a lost or refused connection as `Net.connection_failed(reason)`, with the same sentence kept in `Net.last_error` for the title, which shows it once. A pairing loads the select screen and a re-queue the title from `Net` itself; a drop from the queue (lock-in missed, 30 minutes waited, a leaver cooldown) arrives as `Net.connection_failed(reason)`. A cooldown refusal first sets `Net.cooldown_until_msec` (ticks at which it ends, 0 for none; `leave()` keeps it and the server taking a queue join clears it), and `Net.cooldown_left_ms()` counts it down. `Net.queue_room()` says this seat's room came from the queue. The title's `_ready` leaves the connection open while `Net.queue_state` is `queued`. Ranked: `Net.find_ranked()` is `find_duel()` into the ranked queue (the same queue states and signals; `find_duel()` is always casual), and `Net.ranked_room()` says this seat plays a ranked match, where `Net.can_rematch()` is false. `Net.series_game`, `Net.series_wins` and `Net.next_game_at` hold the match as the server last told it (`_rpc_series(best_of, game, wins)` before each deal and each resume). Between games `Net.game_over(game, wins, next_in_s)` fires after the game's result, and `Net.next_game_ready()` asks for the next game at once. `Net.concede()` loses the current game, `Net.leave_match()` the whole match, during a game or between games, and `Net.give_up()` in a ranked match concedes the whole match too, connected or cut off; a match conceded as a whole ends with the match reason `concede_match`, while the game it ended on reads `concede`. `Net.match_over(payload)` fires once the match is decided, with `winner`, `wins`, `reason` (a result reason, or `concede_match`), `rated`, `shown_before`, `shown_after`, `provisional` (each per seat) and this seat's own `rating_before` and `rating_after` (`mu`, `sigma`); `Net.clean_match_payload` is its checker. For a scene built after a signal, `Net.last_match` keeps the last `match_over` payload, `Net.last_game_over` the last `game_over` as `{game, wins, next_in_s, at_msec}` (`at_msec` the ticks it arrived at) and `Net.last_duel_ended` the last `duel_ended` as `{winner, reason}`; only the next deal or resume and a new queue join clear them, and `Net.leave()` and a dropped connection keep them. `Net.rating_known(shown, provisional)` fires after the greeting and after each match, and `Net.rating` keeps `shown`, `provisional`, `mu` and `sigma`. In a ranked match the rejoin file stays until the match is decided, so `Net.forget_rejoin()` keeps it until then. Identity and transport: `Net.identity()` is this install's `Identity` (`scripts/net/identity.gd`: `public_pem()`, `id()`, `short_id()`, `sign(bytes)`, static `verify(public_pem, bytes, sig)`, `load_or_make()`), loaded or made on the first greeting; `Net.queue_ticket()` is what `_rpc_queue_join` carries; `Net.server_cert_path()`, `Net.pinned_certificate()` and `Net.server_tls(address)` read the pinned certificate; `Net.serve(port, tls)` takes the server's `TLSOptions`, and `LanTransport.tls` sets DTLS on either end. on the title, `--dev-rejoin` presses Rejoin when a good rejoin file is there, and a rejoined client counts `--dev-steps` from the catch-up update; without `--dev-autoplay` the select screen takes the screenshot instead, showing the players strip and the room code banner. Online, `--dev-seed` and `--dev-pick` on the duel scene are ignored because the lobby already agreed on both. In a ranked match `--dev-autoplay` carries on into the next game instead of stopping at a game's result, `--dev-concede=N` concedes each game once N updates of it have played, `--dev-leave-match=N` concedes the match instead, `--dev-next-game` presses Ready as soon as the between-games card shows, and `--dev-screenshot=<png>` saves `<png>_game1.png` (`_game2` after a second game) at the between-games card and `<png>` at the match result. An autoplay or screenshot client quits 4 seconds after the match result shows, so the other client's shot is taken first. A whole match runs unattended with `--dev-ranked --dev-next-game --dev-autoplay` on both clients and `--dev-concede=N` on one.

Presence check: add `--dev-presence-demo` to one instance and `--dev-screenshot=<png>` to the other. The demo instance sends a scripted pointer instead of the mouse: `=card` circles over its own Mastery (the other side sees the ring and a seat-coloured aura on that card), `=hand` reads hand slot 1 (the matching back rises in the other side's fan), `=look` has the other side's Discard open (the other side sees "X is viewing your Discard"); the bare flag cycles through the three. It stays up 4 seconds after its last step so the other instance can take its shot. With `--dev-steps=18` and `--dev-pick=3,5` the shot usually lands on an open table rather than a tray.

## Loadouts

Fourteen starter decks, hand-edited in `data/decks/`. Every card parallels one printed card of the reference game's era and carries its mechanics in the effect schema below; every name is original. Who each duelist is: `docs/cast.md`. How each deck is piloted: `docs/strategy.md`. What the pool is made of: `docs/archetypes.md`.

Each deck file carries a `tagline` (one line under the duelist's name on the select screen) and a `blurb` (two or three sentences on who they are and how the deck plays); the select screen's key card row is the Mastery, the Relic and every card whose title carries a word of the duelist's name.

Each deck file names its **archetype** (`strike_beatdown`, `art_beatdown`, `allies`, `drills`, `seals`, `ascension`, `control`), its `subthemes` (one word each for what the deck leans on: `fervor`, `energy`, `draw`, `might`, `disruption`, `bond`, `arts`, `strikes`, `swords`, `automatons`, `seals`, `allies`, `drills`) and a `difficulty`. The vocabulary, labels and one-line plans live in `engine/archetype.gd`, and `DeckValidator` rejects a word it does not know. The archetype is public, like the duelist and the Mastery: it rides on `PlayerState` and `SeatPlayer`, shows as a chip and a plan line on the select screen and after the style in the duel's player panel, and `AiReserve.read_setup` takes the signs it declares (an `allies` deck reads as an Ally deck, a `seals` subtheme as a likely Seal deck) on top of what the setup cards show.

Structural rules from the 2014 relaunch, adopted 2026-09-16: every deck carries a Mastery and that Mastery's school is the deck's Style (nothing is declared, the old +1 Surge stays as a flat bonus for every deck, Endurance always works); the Ascension win is full Fervor at the duelist's own top aspect, plus one house rule on top (2026-09-19): entering an Aspect above every Aspect the rival can reach is itself an Ascension win, with no second Fervor peak, and is only available when your announced ladder is taller than theirs (`DuelEngine.mppv_aspect` returns that Aspect, or 0 when there is none). The same gates apply as to the Fervor road: `no_ascension_win` blocks it and a `use_at: "ascension_win"` card answers it; an attack that deals 5 or more life cards is critical damage and the attacker may capture a Seal, discard an Ally, or lower the rival's Fervor by 1, ignoring Ally and Fervor protections since it is a game rule and not a card effect; Duelist Powers are once per turn and an aspect change mid-Combat does not refresh them. A second house rule (2026-09-21): every Ally rule is per player and nothing in play reads the other side of the table, so both players may field the same Ally at once, at the same Aspect, even the same card, and an Ally may share a name with the rival's Duelist; one player still gets only one Ally per character, and not sharing your own Duelist's character is a deck rule checked by `DeckValidator` alone. This one is ours, not adopted: the relaunch rulebook sets no in-play Ally uniqueness rule, only a one-copy-per-deck limit. Nothing else from the relaunch is adopted: deck sizes stay 50 to 85, Duelists keep 3 to 5 aspects, Surge and Might stay per duelist.

Remaining approximations in the starter set:

- Marrow's Appraisal and Watchful Eye show the owner the opponent's whole hand as the choice list. Seals 2, 6 and 7 of each set are simple fillers the sheets never use.
- Steel Standoff ends the turn for both players: nobody takes a Discard step.
- Bonds (the two-Allies-as-one card): the Bond burns one life card per turn of its owner and ends at five; the two Allies return at 3 Energy and the Bond card goes back to the Reserve. A Bond that leaves play takes both Allies with it.

`tests/matchlab.gd` is the deck and AI bench, and the one to reach for. The two sides of the table are configured independently, so the same runner answers "which deck wins", "does Hard beat Easy" and "does another 400 ms of search buy anything". Side `a` is the pilot and side `b` the field it is measured against. Win rates carry a 95% Wilson interval, which is the number to read when asking whether a balance change did anything; at 100 matches a 55% win rate still spans about 45-64%.

**Card integrity guard.** After every command the `Referee` runs `DuelEngine.integrity_problem()`: no card may sit in two zone lists, and a card in the hand, discard, Life Deck, removed pile or Reserve must carry that zone. The first fault is logged as a red `DUPLICATED OR MISPLACED CARD` error naming the card, the turn and the command, and kept in `Referee.integrity_fault`. `SimMatch` fails the match with "Card integrity: ..." as its error, so matchlab, adventure_lab and every other sim runner report it as a broken match instead of a result. `DuelEngine._use_card` also logs an error when a hand card's zone disagrees. This exists because of the 2026-09-22 Watchful Eye loop, where one card in the Life Deck twice let a spent Combat card be used forever.

```powershell
# the standing balance matrix, every deck against every deck from both seats
& $godot --headless --path zenith -s tests/matchlab.gd -- --scenario=res://tests/scenarios/balance.json

```

Fields are selectors, not just names: `*` every deck, `@archetype`, `+subtheme`, `#difficulty` (the pilot difficulty in the deck file), a deck name, `!name` to remove one, and `:N` after any of them to weight it. `--field` sets both sides; `--a-field` and `--b-field` override one. Weights only matter in `--mode=sample`, where opponents are drawn at random; `--mode=matrix` (the default) plays every pair `--repeats` times from both seats.

Side flags: `--policy` for both sides, `--a` and `--b` for one (`random`, `scorer`, `search`, `rollout`, or a level file under `data/ai/profiles` such as `easy` or `hard`); `--styles=off` to drop each deck's own playstyle profile; and the think knobs `--budget`, `--samples`, `--turns`, `--steps`, `--depth`, `--nodes`, `--branches`, `--responses`, `--top-k`, `--rollout-steps`, `--noise`, `--prior`, `--settle-plies`, `--settle-lead`, each of which also takes an `--a-` or `--b-` prefix. `--a-think=budget_ms=800;cache=1` reaches any knob without its own flag. Also `--seed`, `--limit` to cut a run short, `--progress=N` for a line every N matches, `--verbose`, `--tsv=` for one row per match and `--json=` for the machine-readable summary. For reviewing play, `--records=<file.jsonl>` writes every finished game as a match record that the duel scene replays (`--dev-replay=<file>:<record id> --dev-replay-seat=all`), and `--decisions=<file.jsonl>` writes one line per AI decision: the record id and the command's `entry` (open the replay there with `--dev-replay-to=<entry>`), the seat's Energy, Fervor, hand and life, every option with the scorer's score, the search's values when it searched, and the option taken.

An unknown flag stops the run and says so, so `--repeat=20` no longer quietly runs the default. Any set of flags can live in a scenario file under `tests/scenarios/`, loaded with `--scenario=`; anything on the command line overrides it, which is how you vary one axis against a fixed baseline. Four ship: `balance.json`, `difficulty.json`, `think_time.json`, `beatdown_bracket.json`.

`tests/adventure_lab.gd` is the same bench for a whole adventure run rather than one duel. It takes one starter and plays complete runs across the node map, picking a random path at every fork, carrying the deck forward and taking a reward bundle at random from each offer, until the run is won or dies. It reports a survival table by duel number (runs that reached each duel, the win rate there with a Wilson interval, mean turns, which route the losses came by and which opponent families killed them), a win rate per act and node type, a run summary (runs won, mean duels won, where runs ended, median duel of death, mean Motes), a rough per-bundle "did it help" win rate, and a per-opponent table. Duels run on the adventure lives rule by default: the player has two, an ordinary opponent one, a boss two.

```powershell
& $godot --headless --path zenith -s tests/adventure_lab.gd -- --starter=pyre_beatdown_start --runs=20
```

Flags: `--starter=<id>` (a deck under `data/adventure/starters`, required), `--runs=N` (default 10), `--seed=N` (default 1; each run derives its own seed from it, so a seed reproduces the whole set), `--policy=` for the player (default `scorer`, since the sequence planner is too slow for eight duels a run), `--opponent-policy=` to override the `ai_level` each ladder row names, `--lives=off` for a symmetric first-to-two instead, `--max-steps`, `--tsv=` for one row per stage played, `--json=` for the full report, `--verbose` for a line per stage as it happens, and `--progress=N`. Unknown flags stop the run, as in matchlab.

`tests/deck_report.gd` (random pairings, `--games=N`) and `tests/deck_outcomes.gd` (full matrix with endgame state: how far each loser still had to go on every route, `--repeats=N` per ordered pair per seat, `--tsv=` for one row per match) are the older, narrower tournament runners. `tests/deck_ai_comparison.gd -- --targets=a,b --seeds=N --budget=MS --samples=N --report=<json>` plays each target against the full field from both seats, first with the scorer and then with the sequence planner on identical seeds, and reports the paired gains and losses.

`tests/ally_probe.gd -- --deck=<name> --repeats=N` plays one deck against the whole field and counts what happens to its Allies: turns with one in play, turns with the Duelist spent enough for a takeover, turns with both at once, control prompts split by attacker and defender, attack phases an Ally held, and who actually performed the attacks. It separates an Ally deck losing on its cards from one losing because the takeover condition never comes up.

`tests/ai_trace.gd` plays one game (`--deck=`, `--foe=`, `--seed=`, `--budget=`) with the search AI in seat 0 and prints a line of table state per turn plus a tally of seat 0's commands and events, which shows whether a playstyle profile does what it says. `tests/print_text.gd -- <card ids>` prints generated rules text. `tools/prompt_census.gd` plays random duels between the shipped decks and counts prompts by kind and how many had a single option (`-- --detail` lists those), to find needless stops in the flow. `tools/validate_starters.gd` prints one line per deck under `data/adventure/starters` and `data/adventure/opponents` with its size and any DeckValidator problem, and exits non-zero when a deck fails, for a quick check while rebalancing; the suite covers the same data in `test_adventure_starters_and_opponents_are_legal`. `tools/adventure_offers.gd` plays an all-wins, always-take-the-first-bundle run of every playable starter and prints the three bundles offered at each stage, the Aspect card taken, and how many bundles are eligible per stage, to see what a run's reward pool looks like. It opens with the economy as it stands (payout per stage, price and card count per band, the vendor's shelf) and ends each run with the Motes it earned, what its added cards cost to keep and how many rows the run-end offer lists.

`tools/match_stats.gd` reads real games instead of simulated ones: the match records the duel server and the client write. It prints the same kind of tables as matchlab, with a Wilson range on every win rate. There is one block per catalog hash, with per-deck records, the matchup grid, the first player's win rate, how duels ended, and with `--cards` per-card counts from a replay of each duel. Flags go through `SimArgs`: `--in=<file or folder>`, `--since=`, `--until=`, `--mode=` (default `ranked,casual,code`), `--origin=server|client|all`, `--secret=<secret.key>`, `--catalog=`, `--cards`, `--tsv=`, `--json=`. `docs/duel_server.md` ("Reading the records") covers pulling the server's files and reading the numbers. `tests/fixtures/match_records.jsonl` holds six synthetic records to try it on: `& $godot --headless --path zenith -s tools/match_stats.gd -- --in=res://tests/fixtures/match_records.jsonl`.

Every runner defaults to the sequence planner (`search`), so a large default tournament is slow; pass `--policy=scorer` for a fast baseline. Policies are `random`, `scorer`, `search`, `rollout` (the historical root-only search) or a profile name (`easy`, `hard`).
