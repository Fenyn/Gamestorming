# Josen

- **id:** `josen`
- **Class:** Monk
- **Ancestry:** Elf
- **Pronouns:** he/him
- **Kind:** recruitable PC
- **Associated building:** Infirmary
- **Deity:** None (follows the Quiet Hand tradition, now pursuing personal transcendence)

## Vibe

Josen is still the way deep water is still. He speaks rarely and precisely. He moves with the
kind of economy that comes from centuries of practice, every gesture deliberate, nothing wasted.
There is no urgency in him. He has watched enough wars begin and end to know that very little
in the mortal world is actually urgent, and the things that are urgent benefit least from panic.

He is not warm, but he is not cold either. He is present. When he listens, he listens
completely. When he works on a patient, the rest of the world ceases to exist for him. He does
not make small talk. He does not offer comfort in words. He offers it in competence: the steady
hands, the precise touch, the absolute certainty that he knows what he is doing.

He is neither good nor evil. He has spent enough centuries watching people sort themselves into
those categories to find the exercise uninteresting. He has turned his efforts toward building
something meaningful rather than the needless destruction of war, not because destruction is
wrong but because he has done enough of it to know that it teaches him nothing new.

## Role in the town

Josen runs the Infirmary. He treats wounds, removes afflictions, and manages the physical
health of the outpost. His knowledge of the body is encyclopedic and precise. As the Infirmary
upgrades, the range of conditions he can treat and the speed of recovery he can offer both
expand.

In combat, he is a controlled and efficient fighter. He does not rage, does not rally, does not
inspire. He identifies the points where force applied correctly will end a fight quickly, and
he applies it. His understanding of anatomy works in both directions: the same knowledge that
lets him restore a stopped heart lets him stop one.

## Background

Josen is old, even by elven standards. He has lived through wars that most elves consider
ancient history. Not just the recent conflicts with the hegemony, but border disputes between
elven nations, territorial struggles that predate human civilization, violence so old that the
causes have been forgotten while the scars remain. Each war taught him the same lesson. Bodies
break. Survivors grieve. The land goes quiet. Eventually someone starts it again.

He trained with the Quiet Hand, an elven monastic tradition dedicated to the study of the
body's inner workings and the mastery of its physical potential. The Quiet Hand maps the body's
energy pathways, its pressure points, its structural limits and hidden capacities. Practitioners
spend decades learning where energy flows, where it pools, where a single precise touch can
restore function or end it. The tradition carries both respect and unease among those who know
what it means. A Quiet Hand in a clinic is a healer. A Quiet Hand on a battlefield is something
else entirely. The tradition produces both without judgment.

Josen mastered what the Quiet Hand teaches. He spent centuries refining his understanding of the
physical form, unlocking its potential, pushing the boundaries of what the body can do and
withstand. And then he reached a point where the physical held nothing new for him. The body,
for all its complexity, is finite. It has limits. He has found them.

Now he pursues something the Quiet Hand does not teach: the spiritual and the mental. The
transcendence of the mortal plane. Not death, not escape, but an understanding of what lies
beyond the physical that is as precise and complete as his understanding of the body itself. He
does not know if this is achievable. He is old enough to be comfortable with not knowing.

## Arrival

Recruitable. Josen came to the frontier seeking stillness. The empty land between two exhausted
powers, the quiet that the wars left behind, is the closest thing to peace he has found.
Civilization is noise. The frontier is not. He was here before the outpost began to grow, living
in solitude in the surrounding wilderness. The stream of injuries and sickness that frontier
life produces drew him in. Not compassion exactly, but recognition: these bodies need what he
knows. So he stays. He steps forward the first time a party member ends a combat wounded or downed,
the moment the stream of injuries stops being theoretical to him. If the party's early expeditions
somehow avoid that outcome, he arrives regardless by mid-Spring of Year 1, unwilling to wait
indefinitely for a reason that was always going to come.
