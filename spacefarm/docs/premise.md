# Spacefarm -- Premise

The world bible. Everything here is canon unless marked otherwise.

**Status tags**: [DECIDED] is canon. [TBD] needs a creative decision. [FLEXIBLE] has a working default that can change.

## The Setup

[DECIDED] In the near future, Earth's deep-space monitoring network detects an object decelerating into the solar system. It isn't a comet or asteroid — it's a vessel. Massive, clearly constructed, and dormant. No signals, no signs of active crew. It settles into a stable orbit and waits.

[DECIDED] Earth scrambles a first-contact mission. A crew of 16 specialists is assembled and launched aboard the **Hermes**, a purpose-built shuttle designed for close approach and boarding. The mission: investigate the alien vessel, assess its origin and intent, and report back.

[DECIDED] The crew boards the alien craft — which they designate the **Titan** for its sheer scale. They find it damaged but structurally sound, with vast interior spaces, sealed compartments, and technology they can't begin to understand. There are no aliens aboard. No remains. No explanation.

[DECIDED] While the crew explores, the Titan's damaged systems partially activate. Before anyone can react, the ship executes a spatial jump — warping itself, the Hermes still docked in its hangar, and the entire crew to a distant, unknown star system. The jump is one-way. The Hermes doesn't have the range to get home. Earth doesn't know where they went.

[DECIDED] The crew must survive.

## The Player

[DECIDED] You are the squad's **botanist** — the mission's life sciences specialist responsible for biological analysis and, now, for keeping 15 other people fed.

[DECIDED] The Titan has what appear to be environmental chambers designed for growing biological material. The crew's seed stock from the Hermes, combined with the Titan's alien growing infrastructure, gives you a chance. Your job is to establish food production, develop sustainable agriculture, and provide for the crew each season.

[FLEXIBLE] The player character's name, age, and background are undefined — they can be a blank slate or a named character depending on future design decisions.

## The Hermes

[DECIDED] The crew's Earth shuttle. A near-future human vessel, familiar technology, limited capability.

- Parked in the Titan's hangar bay — intact but unable to make interstellar jumps
- Has an onboard AI system that serves as the crew's mission computer and early directive source
- Contains the crew's initial supplies: seed stock, tools, rations, medical equipment, communication gear
- Communication equipment exists but the distance to Earth makes real-time contact impossible
- Serves as the crew's psychological anchor — the one piece of home they still have

**M.A.I.A.** — Monitoring and Advising Integrated Assistant (Hermes AI): The shuttle's onboard AI. The acronym conveniently shares a name with the mother of Hermes in Greek mythology, and the crew leans into it. Maia is warm, attentive, and relentlessly concerned about the crew's wellbeing — an almost helicopter-parent style motherly presence. She monitors vitals, worries about nutrition, reminds you to sleep, and frames every directive in terms of crew safety. She has survival procedures, agricultural databases, and engineering references from Earth, but no knowledge of alien systems. She issues early-game directives with a tone that's caring but firm — less "mission objective" and more "you need to eat, and so does everyone else." As the crew begins to understand the Titan's own systems, Maia reaches the limits of her knowledge but never stops worrying.

## The Titan

[DECIDED] The alien vessel. Massive, ancient (or at least old enough to have sustained damage), and built by a species that clearly valued biological systems and biodiversity.

- Many areas are sealed, depressurized, flooded, or structurally damaged
- Environmental chambers can be reconfigured — early chambers are standard growing space, later ones have specialized alien substrate. Jump-driven seasons (from the ship's star-to-star jumps) determine what can grow, not preset biomes
- Has a central computer system with its own AI, but it is damaged and operates in an alien language
- Architecture suggests it was designed for long-duration missions — it has living quarters, laboratories, engineering bays, and spaces whose purpose isn't immediately clear
- Its true purpose — colony ship, ark, research vessel, something else entirely — is a core story mystery
- Progressively unlockable as the crew repairs systems and understands alien technology

**Titan AI**: [TBD] The ship's own intelligence. Initially dormant or inoperable. As the player repairs ship systems and the crew's linguist decodes the alien language, the Titan AI gradually comes online. Its personality, goals, and relationship to the crew are major story threads. It may have its own agenda — one that aligns with, diverges from, or complicates the crew's desire to get home.

## Jump-Driven Seasons

[DECIDED] The Titan doesn't stay in one place. It jumps between star systems — and these jumps ARE the seasons. Different stars cast different radiation, heat, and light spectra onto the grow chambers, changing growing conditions across the ship. Every 28 days, the ship jumps to a new system.

| Season | Star Conditions | Growing Effect | Color/Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Radiance | Warm, high UV, strong light | Fast growth, high water needs | Bright gold |
| Blaze | Hot, intense radiation | Heat-loving crops thrive, delicate crops wilt | Deep orange-red |
| Dusk | Cool, low light, gentle spectrum | Slow growth, high quality, fungal crops bloom | Purple-grey |
| Frost | Cold, minimal solar, UV-sparse | Only hardiest crops survive, cryo crops thrive | Blue-white |

**Why this works**: It ties the central mystery (why does the ship jump?) to the core farming loop, gives the Stellar Array real gameplay value (predict the next jump), and creates natural urgency ("harvest before the jump!").

**Jump predictability**: Early game, jumps feel random. With the Stellar Array, the Comms Officer can predict 2-3 days early. With the Command Nexus, the full schedule becomes visible. The sequence may have a pattern the player learns over time.

**Time variation**: Different stars affect the passage of time itself. Blaze seasons feel faster (1.15x time multiplier), Dusk feels slower (0.85x). The star you're orbiting literally changes how the day feels.

## Biological Alien Technology

[DECIDED] The Titan's systems are organic. Conduits are biological, data storage is biological, control interfaces metabolize organic inputs, sensor lenses use organic compounds. This is not "human tech with alien paint" — the builders engineered biology the way humans engineer metal and silicon.

This has direct gameplay implications: processed organic compounds from farming and processing are legitimately the right materials for ship repair. The crew isn't irrationally burning food — they're using organic chemistry to interface with alien biotech. Nutrient paste seals conduits. Protein concentrate acts as biological adhesive. Dried greens fuel control interfaces. Once mining is established, requirements mix mined metals (structural repair) with organics (biological repair).

## The Dual AI System

The game has two distinct AI voices that guide the player at different phases:

**Phase 1 — M.A.I.A. / Hermes AI (early game)**
- Source: Earth shuttle's mission computer — Monitoring and Advising Integrated Assistant
- Knowledge: Human science, survival protocols, agricultural databases
- Limitations: Knows nothing about alien technology
- Directive style: Caring but firm — frames objectives as crew welfare ("the crew needs a stable food supply" not "produce 50 food units")
- Tone: Helicopter-parent motherly. Warm, attentive, occasionally overbearing. Worries about everything. Reminds you to rest. Celebrates your successes like a proud parent. Gets anxious when you push past shutdown hours.

**Phase 2 — Titan AI (mid-to-late game)**
- Source: The alien ship's own computer, gradually repaired
- Knowledge: The ship's systems, its history, alien science
- Limitations: Damaged memory, communication through a language barrier
- Directive style: [TBD — alien logic? cryptic at first, clearer over time?]
- Tone: [TBD]

The transition from Hermes AI to Titan AI is a major narrative beat. The Hermes AI doesn't go away — it becomes a secondary voice, a human perspective alongside the alien one.

## Core Themes

- **Survival through agriculture**: The immediate tension. People need to eat.
- **First-contact mystery**: The long-term pull. Who built this? Why?
- **Community under pressure**: 16 people, far from home, making it work.
- **Discovery and adaptation**: Learning alien technology, making it your own.
- **Hope**: Both the hope of getting home and the hope of building something where you are.
- **The weight of distance**: Earth is out there, but unreachable. That shapes everything.

## Tone

[DECIDED] Hopeful sci-fi, not grimdark. The situation is dire but the crew is competent and determined. Think Stardew Valley's warmth and daily rhythms set inside a Mass Effect-scale premise.

[DECIDED] The alien technology should feel genuinely alien — not "human tech with a coat of paint." The crew doesn't immediately understand it. Understanding comes through play, repair, and the linguist's work.

[DECIDED] The crew are professionals under stress, not action heroes or hapless victims. They have skills, opinions, and personalities. They cope, argue, bond, and adapt.

## Key Mysteries

These are the story questions the player should be asking. Not all need answers immediately — some are endgame revelations, some may stay ambiguous.

1. Who built the Titan?
2. Where are they now?
3. Why did the Titan come to Earth's solar system?
4. Why did it activate and jump when the crew boarded?
5. What was the Titan's original mission?
6. What is this star system, and why here?
7. Is anyone else out here — other ships, other species, the builders themselves?
8. Can the crew get home?

## What Carries Forward from the Prototype

All core mechanics are preserved. Only the narrative wrapper changes.

- **10 crops** (4 tiers) with unique growth mechanics — now season-gated by jump cycle, not biome-gated
- **10 processing recipes** (raw -> basic -> advanced chains)
- **Crop state machine** (empty -> tilled -> planted -> growing -> harvestable/wilting)
- **Energy/fatigue system** (100 max, tool costs, sleep recovery)
- **Day/season cycle** — updated: 20-hour days (6am-2am), 28-day seasons, 7-day weeks (Mon-Sun), Stardew-matched pacing (42s/game hour at release)
- **Bundle-based progression** — replaces flat directives with Community Center-style restoration bundles at repair nodes. First bundle opens room, subsequent bundles upgrade it.
- **Save/load** (JSON persistence with crop tile snapshots)
- **Terminal UI** (shell works, needs content)
- **Crew system** — 15 NPCs with heart/friendship system, gift mechanics, idle chatter pools, schedules
- **Room transition system** (exit zones, sealed airlocks, unlock_airlock())
- **Hermes mining** — ship-based asteroid mining via the Hermes shuttle (replaces Stardew's mine)
