# Thistle

- **id:** `thistle`
- **Class:** Ranger
- **Ancestry:** Gnome
- **Pronouns:** she/her
- **Kind:** recruitable PC
- **Associated building:** Watchtower
- **Deity:** None currently. Fragments of a forgotten gnomish faith surface occasionally — a
  gesture before eating, a word muttered at sunset — but she does not recognize them as belief
  anymore. As she recovers, these fragments may become conscious again.

## Vibe

When the party first meets Thistle, she is quiet in a way that feels heavy. She speaks in short
sentences when she speaks at all. She responds to questions but does not volunteer anything. She
follows instructions because following is easier than deciding. She is functional, competent,
and almost entirely absent from her own life.

This is The Stillness. Thistle has been alone on the frontier for a long time, long enough that
the novelty ran out, long enough that the days stopped being different from each other, long
enough that the thing inside her that needed constant change began to starve. She is not dead.
She is not dying in any way a healer could treat. She has simply stopped being present in her
own existence.

As her relationship with the outpost deepens, she comes back. It is not dramatic. It is
gradual. She starts asking questions again. She corrects someone's route without being asked.
She laughs at something Fenwick says and looks surprised that she did. She starts caring about
the answers to her own questions. She picks up habits, preferences, opinions. She becomes
someone again, not the someone she was before, but someone new built from the wreckage of the
old. The daily chaos of a frontier settlement, new faces, new problems, new arguments, meals
she has never tasted, is the opposite of The Stillness. The outpost is saving her life and it
takes her a while to realize it.

At her best, Thistle is quick and curious and sharp-tongued. The name used to suit her
differently: playful and prickly rather than just prickly. That version of her is the
destination, not the starting point.

## Role in the town

Thistle runs the Watchtower. She has been scouting the territory around the outpost for longer
than the party has been here. She knows every trail, every game path, every seasonal flood
pattern, every cave and hollow for miles. The Watchtower formalizes something she was already
doing. As the Watchtower upgrades, her range expands: territory reveals, encounter previews,
and eventually fast travel to previously scouted locations.

In combat, she is precise and patient. She fights the way she scouts: from a distance, picking
her moment, never committing to a position she cannot leave. She is not flashy. She is
effective.

## Background

Thistle came through a Fey connection a long time ago. She does not know exactly how long. Time
has become slippery for her, which is itself a symptom of The Stillness. She remembers the Fey
in fragments: a sky that changed color with her mood, paths that went where she wanted them to
go, the feeling of a world that responded to intention. She remembers the door closing. She
remembers looking for it again. She remembers stopping.

She drifted to the frontier because the edges of the world are where the connections happen.
She has spent years, possibly decades, circling places where the Fey has touched the mortal
world before, waiting for the door to open again. It has not. The waiting became routine.
Routine became numbness. Numbness became The Stillness.

By the time the outpost began to grow, Thistle had stopped actively looking. She had a camp.
She had her trails. She had a rhythm of survival that required no thought and produced no
change. She was well on her way to becoming another gnome who sat down and did not get up.

## Arrival

Recruitable. The party encounters Thistle during a territory expedition. She is at a campsite
that is clearly long-established but barely maintained. She is not in danger. She is not hiding.
She is just there. She follows them back to the outpost not out of enthusiasm but because
following is easier than deciding not to.

The first real sign that something has shifted is small: she corrects someone's route the next
morning, pointing out a better trail, without being asked. The knowledge is still in there. The
person is still in there. The outpost just needs to give her a reason to come back out.

She arrives like a feral cat. Wary, silent, present but not participating. Each increase in
her heart level represents another step back from The Stillness. Specific trigger TBD (zone
discovery or day threshold).

  grows, her dialogue becomes more animated and she begins to offer information and opinions
  unprompted. This is the gnomish parallel to someone breaking free of depression: not a cure
  but a slow, uneven return to engagement with life.
- She may eventually hint at a deity or belief system from before The Stillness, something
  gnomish or Fey-adjacent that she carried from her old life. This should surface late in her
  arc, when she is strong enough to look backward without being pulled back down.
- Her knowledge of the territory should be mechanically significant from day one despite her
  personal state. She is immediately the most useful scout the party has. The gap between her
  competence and her affect is part of what makes her sad.

  Spore's cheerful alienness might be exactly the kind of novelty that reaches Thistle.
