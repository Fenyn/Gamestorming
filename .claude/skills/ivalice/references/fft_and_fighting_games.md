## Sources and caveats

- Almost everything below is a **fan transcription** from a wiki, pulled as raw wikitext through the wiki's API or as raw page text. I copied the lines as the wiki gives them, typos included (for example "Damien", "taker her place").
- The house rule bans em dashes, so every em dash in a source line is written here as `--`. Nothing else in the quoted text is changed. `[...]` marks lines I cut from a longer scene.
- FFT names follow the script being quoted. War of the Lions (WotL) and The Ivalice Chronicles (IC) use Argath, Gaffgarion, Zalbaag, Milleuda, Folmarv, Barrington. PS1 uses Algus, Gafgarion, Zalbag, Miluda, Vormav, Barinten.

FFT battle dialogue is turn-triggered. A battle opens with a short exchange, then lines fire on a named unit's first turn, on its tenth turn, at critical HP, and on defeat. One fight is a running argument in instalments, and the speech the player hears depends on who they fielded.

What changed:
- **PS1** is plain modern English with exclamation marks and scare quotes ("'Justice'?", "a 'heretic'"). Some lines are blunter and land harder than WotL ("Animals have no God!"). Others read as a rough translation ("Blame yourself or God").
- **WotL** moved to archaic register on purpose. Meyerink argues the elevated diction carries the class distance of the Japanese, and renamed terms carry their meaning ("Hokuten" became "Order of the Northern Sky"). Cairistiona finds WotL closer to the formal Japanese register. The standing complaint is "faux-Shakespearean" and harder to read (NeoGAF thread title: "FFT War of the Lions translation sacrifices readability for pretentious prose", https://www.neogaf.com/threads/fft-war-of-the-lions-translation-sacrifices-readability-for-pretentious-prose.1345227/).
- **IC Enhanced** keeps WotL wording in most unchanged lines, so it keeps the WotL register. It adds lines for voice acting, smooths a few awkward ones ("get your end" became "get what you wanted"), and adds callbacks (Gaffgarion's whistle). It also spells out what WotL implied. Delita now announces his whole future arc at Ziekden, and a new Agrias/Gaffgarion exchange states its gender theme in two long speeches. That last point is my judgement. Steam players also disagree about how much of IC is new translation and how much is reused WotL text (https://steamcommunity.com/app/1004640/discussions/0/594032928485494750/).

### FFT analysis

**Register and diction.** The archaic flavour sits in a small set of high-frequency words: 'tis, naught, ere, fain, mayhap, aught, ser, milady, Lord Brother, "venge" for "avenge", and inverted questions ("Think you Dycedarg's hand more just?"). The grammar underneath stays modern and keeps contractions ("I'd", "You'll", "Don't"). One or two archaic markers per line is the norm, never a whole Jacobean sentence. That keeps it readable at speed. Address terms carry the hierarchy: Ramza says "Lord Brother", Gaffgarion says "lad" and "boy", Elmdore says "dear", Argath says "churl".

**Line length.** The openers are short ("It's you and I now, Ramza! Shall we be about it, then?", "Draw your sword, Ramza.", "Ramza, ever the gallant fool!"). The mid-battle turns carry the speeches, 20 to 80 words. Sampled battle lines average 17 words but the median is 11.5, because a few tirades sit on top of many short lines. The deaths and farewells are the shortest lines in the game ("I feel...cold." / "Good-bye, Gaffgarion.").

**Ideology and class enter as arguments, not insults.** Each antagonist has a worldview and states it as a maxim: Gaffgarion's "A man cannot sell his blade and think to keep it clean!", Wiegraf's "The hands that guide history's reins are ever black with blood", Argath's "Common blood, common man", Dycedarg's "I, who have dirtied my hands to keep yours clean". The hero answers with a plain moral line ("But...but this isn't right!", "I will not be made a puppet!"). The villain gets the better rhetoric and the hero gets the shorter, more stubborn reply. Class shows in who gets called by a first name and who gets called "churl" or "wench".

**Family and friendship show through one shared detail.** The grass whistle (Ramza, Delita, later Ovelia, and in IC Gaffgarion). "Lord Brother" used by the brother being rejected. "Do you not know your own blood?" Tietra's name used as a weapon by both sides. Old friends meet with a jab that assumes history ("So I do. And you, ever your lord brothers' faithful hound?").

**Where FFT agrees with the BlazBlue short-and-loud style (reference_prev.md).**
- Openers and deaths are short and loud: "Tietra!", "Begone!", "Ramza, ever the gallant fool!" 48% of FFT battle lines carry an exclamation.
- Feelings are said outright: "Why? Why must it end like this?", "What am I doing? What have I become?"
- Relationships live in terms of address: "Lord Brother", "lad", "milady", "churl".
- Lore arrives as bare nouns: auracite, Lucavi, Ziekden, the Scriptures of Germonique.
- The second speaker often refuses the first: "I have no words for you." and "Speak not to me!"

**Where it disagrees.**
- FFT lets people argue. A fight is a debate held over several turns. BlazBlue intros never debate.
- FFT uses maxims freely, but they belong to antagonists as worldviews the story then rejects, and they are never tidy closing quips. reference_prev.md item 8 ("no epigrams") holds for intros, but FFT shows that a villain's creed can be a good long line.
- Lines run three times longer on average. FFT earns this because the player chose the fight and watches it unfold across turns. A one-screen pre-duel lead-in cannot carry 60-word speeches.
- No running nicknames and little comedy in battle. Humour is rare and dry (Gaffgarion's "Why use so many words when you could just call me an old relic?").

For our lead-ins, FFT suggests three things. The opener and the parting line stay short. A villain's line can state a worldview, and the reply is a short refusal. Old relationships show through one shared object or one term of address.

## 12. Writer and critic commentary (thin; few sources say anything about craft)

- **Scale forces reuse.** Dominic Cianciolo (Injustice 2 cinematic director): "There are 13,000 individual lines of dialogue in-game, and because of the way we have the system developed to re-purpose that in some ways, we have 6,000+ intros in the game." (EventHubs, 2017-05-15, https://www.eventhubs.com/news/2017/may/15/13000-lines-dialogue-and-over-6000-intro-interactions-injustice-2-heres-look-intros-first-three-premier-skins/). This is why some closers repeat across pairs (Cassie's "Knew you'd say some shit like that"). A reusable closer has to make sense after several different openers.
- **Wrestling logic.** TV Tropes, "So You Want To Write a Fighting Game": "Fighting games are a bit like Professional Wrestling in that players want to see unique interactions when their favorite characters square off", and spend the extra voice budget "particularly for characters relevant to their backstory." https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/SoYouWantTo/WriteAFightingGame
- **Reputation vs reality.** William F. Edwards (The Warthog Report, 2023-06-15) on writing per-matchup win quotes for his own game. His example shows one character meeting another they only knew by name: "So this is the Avatar of Flames. You look… sadder than I expected." https://warthogreport.substack.com/p/win-quotes
- **Register as worldbuilding (FFT).** Meyerink (RPGFan 2017) argues the WotL archaic voice and translated proper nouns carry meaning the PS1 romanisations lost. Cairistiona (2025) says WotL restores the honorific and register differences that PS1 flattened.
- **Rewriting for voice (FFT IC).** Maehiro (2025): lines written for silent text sound unnatural when acted, so Matsuno revised them. Matsuno (GoNintendo summary of a 2025 interview, https://gonintendo.com/contents/50087-final-fantasy-tactics-tic-scriptwriter-says-there-are-minor-dialogue-changes-that): "I've grown older since I first wrote the script for this game, and my perceptions have changed accordingly."
- I found no NetherRealm interview on how the intros themselves are written. The Cianciolo interviews (CBR 2023, Gamereactor) cover story and roster only.

## 13. Analysis: what makes the good ones read as written by skilled humans

**Line length.** NetherRealm lines average 5.5 to 7 words and almost never pass 15. Strive and BlazBlue sit in the same range. Length grows only where the format gives time: KOF XIII, UNI arcade and FFT mid-battle turns. Even there the first and last lines stay short ("Draw your sword, Ramza.", "I feel...cold.").

**Who speaks first.** The one who wants something: the parent with a question (Jax), the rival with a grievance (Scorpion), the pest (Shingo), the jailer (Chun-Li). The second speaker usually holds the power in the scene and spends it by refusing, deflecting or topping the first line.

**History in one detail.** The best lines name one concrete thing only these two share: Fiji, the sparring record of five and five, chili, Lisa Snart, the grass whistle, "the fight you lost", the arms Ermac ripped off, Cristina, Johnny Pee-Pants. Nobody explains the detail. Weak lines name the relationship instead ("You are my brother", "I can only succeed you, Lord Liu Kang").

**The reply.** It answers sideways. Kitana answers "Afraid your new lover will prefer me?" with "That you'll eat him." Ky answers chili with an accusation of Sol. Kyo answers a sparring request with an errand. A reply that agrees ("I agree. She'll make an excellent Shaolin.") kills the exchange.

**Callbacks and running gags.** Sin eating everyone's food (Ramlethal, Elphelt). Captain Cold's "final showdown" ("You always say that"). Vira's Katalina obsession in every one of her pairs. Kyo failing school. Heihachi's motto, which Kazuya repeats over his father's body. A gag pays off best when the third character notices it.

**Change across meetings.** FFT and KOF show it best. Gaffgarion goes from "Betray you?" to "Ramza, ever the gallant fool!" to "It's you and I now", and dies on "I feel...cold." Rock goes from "Hey, rookie!" to "my rookie days are over." Chun-Li vs Juri moves from "Hold it right there!" to "I heard rumors you'd retired."

**Humour sources.** Family embarrassment (Pee-Pants, rehab, "match point"). Status reversal (Shingo, Krillin's "missed one"). A literal mind (Ramlethal's incident log). A villain's understatement (Geese's "But... it's the truth.").

**Failure modes.** Film quotes standing in for a voice (Terminator, Joker DLC). Allies agreeing with each other (much of MK1). Exposition in dialogue form ("What I saw in Outworld flipped my mind upside down"). Long insults with no shared history (Kukri). Stilted mutual refusal (SFV Vega). Rewrites that spell out what the earlier version implied (IC Delita at Ziekden, IC Agrias). That last judgement is mine.
