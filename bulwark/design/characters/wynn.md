# Wynn

- **id:** `wynn`
- **Class:** Bard
- **Ancestry:** Human
- **Pronouns:** he/him
- **Kind:** recruitable PC
- **Associated building:** Tavern (co-tenant with Fenwick)
- **Deity:** None. Wynn has a deep appreciation for the narrative structure of religion
  and zero interest in participating in it. He once described a chapel sermon as "good
  material, needs tighter pacing."

## Vibe

Wynn is a playwright without a stage, a dramatist without a company, and he does not
consider either of these things a problem. The world is the stage. The people in it are
the company. He has been watching, listening, and taking mental notes since he arrived at
the outpost, and he has not stopped since.

He is witty in the way that makes people laugh before they realize they have been
observed. He notices things. He notices the way Aldric stands when he thinks no one is
watching. He notices that Thistle speaks more on days when the weather is good. He notices
that Fenwick salts the stew differently when Elara is eating. He files all of it away,
not to gossip but because it is true and truth is what makes a story work.

He is a theater kid to his core. He speaks in heightened language when he is excited and
drops into plain speech when he is serious. He pauses before punchlines. He raises an
eyebrow like it is a dramatic gesture. He frames mundane events with the cadence of
something important, and the strange thing is that it works. When Wynn describes your
morning patrol as the opening scene of a frontier epic, you almost believe him. When he
recounts a supply run as a comedy of errors, it becomes one retroactively.

He is charming without being overwhelming. He gives people room. He asks questions and
listens to answers with the intensity of someone who is genuinely interested, because he
is genuinely interested. Every person is a character he has not finished understanding
yet, and he approaches them with the same curiosity and respect a good writer brings to
a subject they care about. People call him quirky because they cannot figure out why a
conversation with him feels different from a conversation with anyone else. The answer is
that he is paying closer attention than they are used to.

Underneath the comedy is someone who takes stories seriously. Not preciously, not
solemnly, but seriously. He believes that what happens to ordinary people matters, that
the stories of a frontier outpost are as worthy of telling as the stories of kings and
generals, and that someone should be paying attention before it all passes unrecorded. He
is that someone. He just happens to be funny about it.

## Role in the town

Wynn shares the Tavern with Fenwick. Fenwick runs the kitchen. Wynn runs the
room. He is the entertainment, the atmosphere, the reason people linger after the meal
is finished. His dramatized retellings of outpost events, expeditions, and battles are a
fixture of evening life. Hearing your own adventure turned into a three-act drama with
character arcs and a moral is the kind of thing that builds community without anyone
noticing it happening.

**Rumor system.** Wynn is a collector of stories from everywhere. Travelers, merchants,
passing adventurers, even the occasional letter from contacts he maintains in the
hegemony. His dialogue cycles include rumors that mix flavor text with real gameplay
hints: hidden locations, character secrets, territory dangers, bounty targets. The player
learns to pay attention to what Wynn says because sometimes the stories are true.

**Morale performances.** Wynn's storytelling sessions provide outpost-wide morale buffs
distinct from Fenwick's meal buffs. Food feeds the body. Stories feed the spirit. The
specific story he tells could rotate, providing different buff types.

**Combat inspiration.** In the field, Wynn is the premier support character. The bard's
Inspire Courage and Inspire Defense abilities buff the entire party. He provides them not
through song but through spoken word: a quip that steadies nerves, a dramatic declaration
that makes the enemy hesitate, a murmured observation that makes an ally fight harder
because someone is watching and their story deserves a good ending.

## Background

Wynn was a playwright in one of the hegemony's larger cities. He was good. He was good
enough that people with taste noticed him, and then good enough that people with power
noticed him, and that is where the trouble started.

Wynn writes what he sees. He cannot help it. He sees the truth of how people behave and
he puts it on a stage and makes it entertaining, and most of the time this is a gift that
audiences appreciate. The problem is that truth does not care about status, and the
behavior of powerful people is as available to observation as anyone else's.

He wrote a piece that dramatized something a prominent figure preferred to keep private.
He did not name them. He did not need to. The resemblance was clear enough that the
audience laughed in recognition, and clear enough that the subject did not find it funny.
Wynn does not regret the work. He maintains it was accurate, well-structured, and his
best writing to date. He regrets that the subject had the resources to make his
continued residence in the city uncomfortable.

He left before anything formal happened. No charges were filed. No threats were made
directly. But the invitations dried up, the venue owners stopped returning messages, and
he recognized the pattern of a life being quietly dismantled by someone with influence.
He packed light and headed for the frontier, where nobody cares what a playwright wrote
about a lord three hundred miles away.

He did not come to the outpost on purpose. He came to the frontier because it was far
away, and he found the outpost because the frontier is small in the ways that matter. He
stayed because the outpost gave him something the city never did: material that had not
been written about before. A ruined fort being rebuilt by a squad of mismatched people
on the edge of the known world. A dwarf who builds things that outlast him. An elf
merchant who smiles like a card player. A goblin who farms by instinct. A bounty hunter
who will not share her name.

He is collecting material for a future work. He has been collecting it since he arrived.
The work keeps growing because the material will not stop being interesting. He mutters
dialogue to himself. He stages conversations in his head between people who do not know
they are being studied. The piece is always in progress, never finished, and the process
is the actual point even if he would never admit that.

## Arrival

Recruitable. Wynn arrives at the outpost as a traveler passing through. He intended to
resupply and move on. He stayed for a meal at the tavern, and then he stayed for the
evening because Fenwick's cooking was good and the company was interesting, and then he
stayed for another day because someone told him a story worth hearing, and then he
stopped pretending he was leaving.

The trigger is the Tavern reaching tier 2, the point where the tavern common room exists. That
room is the reason he stays: a space where people linger after a meal is finished is exactly what
a playwright without a stage needs, and the outpost only has one once the Tavern grows into it.
His arrival should feel organic: he wandered in, and the outpost was interesting enough to hold him.

## Heart Level Progression

- **Hearts 0-2:** The entertainer. Funny, charming, full of stories from the road. He
  deflects personal questions with humor. The player enjoys his company without knowing
  much about him.
- **Hearts 3-4:** The observer. The player catches him watching people, muttering to
  himself, scribbling notes. He admits he is working on something but is vague about
  what. He starts offering more pointed observations about the outpost and its people.
- **Hearts 5-6:** The exile. He tells the player why he left the city. Not with self-pity
  but with the wry delivery of someone who has turned his own displacement into an
  anecdote. The player understands that the comedy is a frame, not a mask.
- **Hearts 7-8:** The playwright. He shares fragments of what he is writing. The player
  recognizes the outpost and its people in the work, dramatized and heightened but true.
  Wynn is genuinely nervous about sharing it, which is the first time the player has
  seen him nervous about anything.
- **Hearts 9-10:** The friend. The performance has not dropped because it was never a
  performance. Wynn is exactly who he appeared to be, just more of it. He tells the
  player that the outpost is the first place he has felt at home, and he says it simply,
  without dramatizing it, which is how the player knows he means it.
