## Analysis

**Shape.** In the BlazBlue set, 217 of 226 exchanges are two lines, 8 are three, 1 is four. Mean line length is 5.8 words, median 5, longest 17. Half of all lines are five words or fewer. Story-mode scenes (SC, SF) run three or four lines, and each line is still short. Who speaks first is fixed per pair, usually the one who wants something: the challenger, the pursuer, the one with the grievance.

**Punctuation as volume.** 60% of BlazBlue lines have an exclamation mark, 27% a question mark, 11% an ellipsis. 56% of exchanges end on an exclamation. Capitals and doubled marks show shouting ("POINT YOUR BLADE AT ME!", "ANY day", "Shut up!!"). Stammers appear 11 times ("M-Mister Bang", "Di- Huh?").

**Relationships show through address.** 65% of exchanges contain a name or a term of address. Relationships live in what people call each other: "brother", "Old Man", "Dad", "Professor", "my dumbest student", "disciple number one", "kid", "Miss Litchi". Nicknames are a running joke and a relationship at once (Bunny Leech, Good Guy, Jinny, Lacking Lady), and the target's standard reply is "Stop calling me that!" Shared history is one tag, never a story: "You never change, do you?", "How long has it been since we last crossed blades?", "A cheeky mouth as usual".

**Register.** Contractions in about a third of lines, plenty of fragments ("Tch.", "You again, Jin?", "Master!"), slang and swearing in some voices only. Insults turn up in about 6% of lines, concentrated in two or three characters. Each character keeps one register across every matchup: Ragna swears and complains, Rachel is arch and polite, Bang is heroic bluster, Jin is cold until his brother appears, Lambda speaks like a machine. You can tell who is talking without the name.

**Emotion and humour.** Emotion is stated outright: "Why?", "Give me my sister back!", "I'm going to bring you back!", "What's happened to you?!" Earnest and melodramatic is the norm. Humour comes from character, not wit: Makoto forgetting the motto, Taokaka hearing "disciple" as food, Litchi not noticing Bang's confession. Dry wit exists (Rachel, Sol, Relius) but it belongs to one or two characters, and it plays against someone who is shouting.

**Endings.** Common closers: an exasperated shout ("You eat way too much!"), a flat threat ("Shut up and die!"), a flustered non-answer ("Can I... help you with something?"), a battle cry ("We fight!!!"). The second speaker often loses the exchange or ignores the first line entirely (Carl demands his sister, Relius says "Do entertain me!"). Tidy last-word epigrams are rare.

**Lore.** Lore appears as bare nouns the player is expected not to understand yet: "Where the hell's Saya?", "the azure", "Six heroes", "Nirvana", "Soul Edge must be near". Nobody explains.

**What literary or AI-sounding dialogue avoids.** Characters shout, stammer, repeat a catchphrase, call each other rude names, misunderstand, and say the feeling out loud. No line is written to be quoted.

## Our draft against the references (`zenith/docs/lead_ins_review.md`)

Counted over the 184 spoken lines in the draft: mean 8.1 words (median 8), 0 exclamation marks, 18 question marks (10%), 0 ellipses, a name or title in about 25% of lines.

1. **Nobody raises their voice.** 0 exclamation marks against 60% in BlazBlue. Every line reads at the same level.
2. **Lines are longer.** 8.1 words on average against 5.8; half the reference lines are five words or fewer.
3. **Three lines is our default.** BlazBlue uses two lines 96% of the time. Our third line is usually where the quip goes.
4. **Our scenes end on a dry winner's line.** The reference ends on shouts, threats, flustered replies or non sequiturs, and the second speaker often loses the exchange.
5. **Everyone shares one voice.** Alder, Caedan, Mourne, Sable, Quarr and Edric all speak in understated irony. Only Ashmark and Siphon sound different. In the reference each character has a register you can hear without the name tag.
6. **Feelings are hidden, never said.** Emrys comes closest ("Then let me carry some of it"). The reference says "Why?" and "Give me my sister back!" and means it.
7. **No nicknames.** Relationships use titles (Lord Mourne, Sir Edric). The reference uses nicknames and pet names as the main way to show a relationship.
8. **Epigrams.** Lines such as "A vault only hides who", "It's the one thing I've never had to pay for" and "I got up. You'd be surprised how often that's the same thing" are written to be quoted. The reference has almost none outside one arch character.
9. **Replies always engage.** Every answer in our draft lands exactly on the call. The reference often talks past the other person, ignores them, or misreads them.
10. **Lore is explained.** Ours gives the backstory in a full sentence ("He wrote to you last winter. I burned it unread."). The reference drops one unexplained noun. Our camera narration also carries mood and judgement ("has decided not to notice"), which fighting games leave to the portraits.

Some of these differences come from our own house style: rule 2 (up to three lines), rule 5 (only Ashmark and Siphon speak in one or two words), rule 7 (plain period register). Those rules would need loosening to match the references.
