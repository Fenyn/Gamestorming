# Elara

- **id:** `elara`
- **Class:** Rogue (Thief racket)
- **Ancestry:** Elf
- **Age:** 100–120 (young for an elf)
- **Pronouns:** she/her
- **Kind:** starting-party PC
- **Associated building:** Trading Post
- **Deity:** None (secular)

## Vibe

Elara is a silver tongue. She is the kind of person who makes a deal feel like a favor, who can
talk her way past a locked gate faster than she could pick it. There is genuine warmth in the
charm, but there is also calculation. She reads a room the way a jeweler reads a gem, quickly
and with an eye for value. She is well-traveled and worldly, the sort who has been to enough
places to know what things are worth and what people will pay for them.

She is not dishonest, exactly. She understands that the truth has many angles, and that
some of those angles are more profitable than others.

The composure is practiced. Nothing about her invites questions about her past, and that is
by design.

## Role in the town

Elara runs the Trading Post. She handles buying, selling, and the flow of goods in and out of
the outpost. As the settlement grows and the Smithy produces higher-quality equipment, her stock
expands to match. She is the economic engine of the outpost, the one who turns surplus into
opportunity.

Beyond trade, she is the party's social fulcrum. Where the player builds and Tharr repairs,
Elara connects. She knows what travelers want, what merchants need, and what information is
worth keeping close.

## Background

Elara was born in Ilvaren, the shrine-town at the ford, to a trading family. By the last years
of the war she was young for a merchant but trusted with routes, running caravans that supplied
the elven armies. She was away on a run when the war ended. She came home to the crater.

Family, neighbors, creditors, the shrine she was raised under: one blast took all of it, and
no one can tell her whom to blame. Aurenthal says the elven command spent their own goddess in
spite; the elves say human hands broke the shrine open. There was no enemy to hate cleanly and
no grave to tend.

For close to thirty years she stayed anyway, trading the roads of the elven heartland, and the
condolences never stopped and neither did the reminders. Elves do not let a grief that size
finish. Ilvaren was in every mouth she traded with, and the word means the war and her home in
the same breath. A couple of years ago she stopped being able to carry it, and left.

Crossing into Aurenthal was her first step into human lands. She had spent a war supplying
armies that fought humans and had never once lived among them; she came in through the
borderlands because that is where the door was — the crown's frontier concessions are open to
any merchant willing, and so few are willing that nobody looked twice at elven paper. Aurenthal
suits her better than she expected: nobody here knew her, and the war is called the Ashfall
here. The human name is easier on her than the elven one. Elves say Ilvaren and mean the war,
and she cannot hear the word without hearing home. Humans who say the Ashfall do not know what
they are saying, and she prefers it that way. She never corrects anyone.

The human world she knows is therefore only its edge: a couple of years of widening circuits
through the borderlands, learning what the frontier wants and what it can pay. She has never
seen the heartland cities; her whole map of humanity is its emptying frontier. A couple of
years is nothing to an elf and she does not pretend otherwise; what she knows of these roads is
recent, practical, and honestly bounded.

The concession paper is her stated reason for being here, and it is true as far as it goes. It
is also cover: she is adrift, thirty years out from the worst day of her life, and the empty
roads ask no questions. She had passed
the outpost's turn for two years without a reason to enter — nobody inside to sell to — until
she fell in with the player and Fenwick at the dead bridge (intro Scene 0) and walked them in.

By then a lifetime of moving was already wearing thin. The forests have grown more dangerous
even in her short time on the frontier, and a life lived on the road has begun to cost more
than it pays. Her first sight of the outpost's interior (intro Scene 2) turns that arithmetic
into a decision: a ruined trading post with no trader, at the edge of the map, among people too
busy surviving to ask about her past. She would not call it settling down. She stays anyway.

## Knowledge boundaries (for dialogue)

She is an elf on her first foray into human lands. Write everything human she knows at a
foreigner's remove: two years deep, learned road-side, none of it from the inside.

- **Knows:** the frontier roads she has walked these two years and their condition; goods,
  prices, and what an escort costs; the trade concessions (her own paper); the frontier's
  emptying, read from customers rather than politics; the war from the elven side, which she
  never volunteers.
- **Does not know:** the human heartland — she has never seen it; the academy (never heard of
  it); house patronage, the ministries' workings, garrison history, or what a posting means to
  a human soldier's career. Crown politics reach her as rumor with a price attached, not as
  civics.
- She reads the player and Fenwick as soldiers by kit and bearing, not by institution — she
  could not name the institution if asked.

## Arrival

Starting party. She came up behind the player and Fenwick at the dead bridge on the frontier
road (intro Scene 0); the outpost sat on her circuit, but she had never had reason to enter it
before walking them in.

  armor. Dex 18. Thief racket uses Dex for damage with finesse weapons.
- A Scoundrel racket unlock is planned as a future option but is not needed for the initial
  build. The existing engine has the Thief racket wired and tested.
- Sprite: uses the "rogue" Mana Seed sheet for now.
- Her secular outlook means no divine mechanics to worry about. Her power comes from skill,
  speed, and knowing exactly where to put the blade.
- Dialogue guideline: to the player the war is history; to her it is memory, and recent. She
  speaks of it factually or not at all, ledger voice throughout, never as tragedy.
- Heart-event ladder: trade banter early; a factual mention of supplying the armies at mid
  hearts; at high hearts, what she came home to. A well-meaning human saying "Ilvaren" stings
  her worse than "the Ashfall" ever could.
