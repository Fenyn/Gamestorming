# Ungilded

Story-driven PF2e tactics game, driven by the Pf2e.Core Remaster rules engine. This project hosts the opening (Prologue): see `designs/ungilded_opening.md` in the repo root. The combat layer (board, HD-2D terrain and looks, HUD, dice, log, reactions, knowledge journal) was copied from Delve.

The Pf2e.Core data path is the `ungilded/pf2e_pack_path` project setting in `project.godot`. Point it at this machine's `Pf2e.Core/Data/pf2e-source/packs/pf2e` folder.

## Run

```
dotnet build Ungilded.sln -c Debug
G:\Godot\Godot_v4.6.2-stable_mono_win64\Godot_v4.6.2-stable_mono_win64_console.exe --path G:/Godot/Gamestorming/ungilded
```

The main scene is the title, `scenes/story/title.tscn` (F5). Prologue plays the opening from `scenes/story/prologue.tscn` and returns to the title after the last beat.

The dev harness is `scenes/dev/combat_test.tscn` (F6 on the open scene): two preset heroes against two Giant Rats on a generated sewer map. `Biome` on the scene's root picks another biome ("forest"); `UseGeneratedMap` off gives the flat board. `UNGILDED_MAP_SEED` overrides the map seed. The heroes are Delve's Veteran and Elara presets. They are temporary, until the Prologue pregens exist. `scripts/presets` still holds Delve's whole roster for the same reason.

`scenes/dev/roster_preview.tscn` (F6) is the roster fight: the Player and the Friend against one of every creature with its own sprite (`RosterEncounter`: the twelve pack creatures in `EncounterTables` plus the Dire Sewer Rat and the Sewer Tyrant), stood in rows three tiles apart on a forest clearing (the forest biome with its scatter thinned). Initiative is fixed with the heroes first, so every creature holds its rest pose until a hero turn ends. `MapSeed` and `Spacing` are on the scene's root; `UNGILDED_MAP_SEED` overrides the seed.

In a debug build, F9 (`dev_jump_menu`) opens the jump menu (`scenes/dev/dev_jump_menu.tscn`, autoloaded) from any screen: pick the title or any Prologue beat, and the Prologue reloads at that beat with the wardrobe, creature journal and HP the skipped beats would have left. `StartBeat` on the `prologue.tscn` root does the same for F6.

## Story

Scripts are in `scripts/story/`, scenes in `scenes/story/`, data in `assets/story/` and `assets/portraits/`.

- `assets/story/*.tres` hold one `DialogueSequence` each: the narrative intro, scenes 1 to 7, the Encounter 1 Friend line and the Encounter 4 lines (half HP, quarter HP, victory, closing). Text is verbatim from the spec. `Backdrop` says whether the lines play over black, over the board or on a cutscene set. Scenes 2 and 3b play over the live combat board. Scenes 1, 3a and 4 to 7 play on cutscene sets: Scene 1 on the Encounter 1 tunnel (`cutscene_set.tscn`), Scene 3a further west along the same tunnel (`cutscene_set_e1_west.tscn`), Scenes 4 to 6 on the Encounter 3 cistern and its east passage, where the skeleton lies (`cutscene_set_e3.tscn`; Scenes 4 and 5 share one instance), and Scene 7 in the Encounter 4 hall (`cutscene_set_e4.tscn`).
- `assets/portraits/player_portraits.tres` and `friend_portraits.tres` map each `Emotion` (`Resting` and `Talking` dictionaries) to animations in `player_frames.tres` / `friend_frames.tres` (SpriteFrames generated from the 12-row Heroes99 sheets, 48x48 frames at 4x): every emotion rests with a blink and has a talk loop that plays while its text types out. Neutral uses idle/talk; Angry, Worried, Amused, Afraid, Determined, Smirk and Thinking use angry, worried, happy, sad, determined, smirk and thinking. `PortraitView` plays them in the box.
- Accessories: `SpeakerPortraits.Accessories` maps an accessory id to overlay frames with the face's animation names (`spectacles_player_frames.tres`, `spectacles_friend_frames.tres`). A `DialogueLine` can carry `AccessoryCues` (speaker, accessory, state: hidden, carried or worn), applied as the line starts and before its staging, narration lines included. The `Wardrobe` that records who carries or wears what belongs to `PrologueDirector` for the whole run, and the dialogue box shows the worn overlay on `PortraitView.Overlay` at the face's frame. A carried or worn accessory also lights the violet glow (`cutscene_glow.tscn`) on the speaker's cutscene actor (the director finds it through `CutsceneSet.Speakers`) and on the speaker's combat token (`UnitVisual3D.Glow.cs`, a dimmer `%Glow` child set by `PrologueEncounterRunner` at each Prepare and on each cue). A carried glow sits at satchel height (0.5 m) and a worn one at eye height (0.85 of the sprite's body height), moved 0.13 of the body height toward the side the sprite faces on screen; on a token the move between them takes 0.6 s. The glow's light skips the sprite layer, so it lights the floor and walls but not the carrier. Scene 3a's last line gives the spectacles to the Friend, carried. Scene 3b puts them on the Friend, back in his hand, into the Player's hand, then on the Player for the rest of the Prologue.
- `assets/story/story_theme.tres` adds the story type variations (narration page in Alagard 48, dialogue name, speech and narration text in Pixeloid 27, the opaque `StoryPanel`, 27 px story buttons) and the `StoryPalette` ember colour. Every size is 3x its font's pixel grid. Everything else falls through to `assets/ui/ui_theme.tres`.
- `advance_mark.tscn` (`AdvanceMark`): the bobbing caret that shows a page or line waits for input. The dialogue box and the narration screen each hold one.
- `title.tscn`: title art, Prologue and Quit.
- `fade_overlay.tscn` (layer 20): the black backdrop. `FadeIn` brings the black up, `FadeOut` takes it away.
- `narration.tscn` (layer 21): one line per page centred on screen, with the caret under the text. `BalancedWrap` breaks each paragraph into centred lines of near-equal width (`WrapWidth`). The Alagard page text has no panel behind it. A heavy dark outline and a drop shadow keep it readable. Behind the text is the line's `Scenery`, or black.
- `scenes/story/crawl/`: the crawl backdrops. Each is a `CrawlScenery` of `DriftLayer`s (`crawl_layer.tscn`, 320x180 art at 7x), and each layer drifts by its own `Drift` while the page shows. A new scenery crossfades over the old one. Pages 1 to 4 use the 2D Medieval Backgrounds Pack (`assets/story/crawl/mbg*`). Page 5 is the slum, drawn by `tools/art/crawl_slum.py` (sky and stars from the same pack's MBG5) into `assets/story/crawl/slum/`. Page 6 has `RevealsSet`: the slum fades out over the Scene 1 cutscene set while the camera moves slowly along the tunnel.
- `dialogue_box.tscn` (layer 22): portrait, name and typewriter text along the bottom. Both portrait slots keep their space, so the text column is the same for every line. Narration lines use `DialogueNarration` (muted, no portrait or name). A line with a blank-line break shows one paragraph per page. During a fight the HUD hides while the box shows.
- `tutorial_popup.tscn` (layer 23): the tutorial tips. A rule tip (`ShowRule`, `ShowMessage`) is modal with Continue and completes on dismiss; rule tips queue. A step tip (`OpenStep`) has no button, holds no modal and stays until its handle is disposed; the newest step tip shows when no rule tip is queued. A tip with a focus dims the screen (`SpotlightDim`, `assets/shaders/tutorial_spotlight.gdshader`: dim, feather, outline width and pulse rate are exports on `Dim`), cuts out the focus with a pulsing ember outline, and sits beside the cutout at the nearest spot that covers no HUD panel (unit cards, the timeline with its heading, the open command menu, the mode pill), or the least of them, `TipWidth` wide or narrower for a short step tip, with a caret in the panel border colour (`StoryPalette/panel_border`) aimed at it. Clicks inside a step tip's cutout reach the HUD and the board; every other click is swallowed. A unit in the focus gets no cutout: the tip draws a bobbing ember arrow over it (`TipArrows` on `Arrows`, art `assets/ui/tip_arrow.png`) above the dim and under the box, pointing at the top of its HP plate, at its head when the plate layout has lifted the plate away, and beside the turn crystal on the active unit. An arrow that would cover a HUD panel (unit cards, timeline, command menu, mode pill) is left out until the panel moves off. The box keeps clear of the unit and its arrow. A focus with only units shows no dim, the box sits beside them, and its step tip lets every click through. An undimmed focus (`TutorialFocus.Undimmed`: every Stride tip, the wrong-tile tip and the first Strike tip) keeps the outline and the caret with no dim, and its step tip lets every click through, the box included. A tip with no focus shows centred with no dim. `Records` keeps what each tip did on screen for the spikes.
- `scenes/story/cutscene/` and `scripts/story/cutscene/`: cutscene sets. `cutscene_set.tscn` (`CutsceneSet`) builds one Prologue board (`Encounter` 1 to 4) through its own `TerrainStage`, with no combat session, and has its own `CutsceneCamera` whose camera attributes switch off the look's depth of field. `LitArea` (board tiles) moves the look's halo darkening out past the tunnels the actors use. `cutscene_actor.tscn` (`CutsceneActor`) is a hero billboard sprite with the token's ground shadow; the set spawns one per hero id ("player", "friend") on first use, hidden until a `PlaceCue`. `Creatures` maps more actor ids to `PrologueBestiary` creature ids, and those actors draw the creature's board sprite (the rats in Scenes 3a and 5, the Tyrant and the Dire Sewer Rats in Scene 7). A creature wider than one tile (the Large Tyrant) stands centred on its square space, and its cue tiles name the space's low corner. The other set scenes inherit `cutscene_set.tscn` and change only `Encounter`, `LitArea` and `Creatures`. A sequence with `Backdrop` Set plays on its `CutsceneSet` scene. Each `DialogueLine` can carry `StageCues`, started as the line starts (narration pages included): `PlaceCue`, `WalkCue` (tile path, pace, end facing, and `KeepFacing` to hold the start facing, as in a step back), `FaceCue` (a grid direction or another actor) and `ShotCue` (look at a tile or the mean of some actors, then yaw, pitch, distance, fov, a camera shift and blend seconds; yaw 0 puts the camera south of the point, 90 east). Tiles are board tiles, and halo tiles are allowed. A cue with `Wait` keeps the dialogue box closed until it ends. A cue with `Delay` starts that many seconds after its line; the next line plays any that are still waiting at once. A line with no cues keeps the shot. Actors slide while they walk, because the heroes have no move clip yet; a sprite definition with a `MoveAnimation` plays it.
- Cutscene flavour cues: `EmoteCue` pops a bubble over an actor's head (`emote_bubble.tscn`, LimeZu frames copied by `tools/art/import_emotes.py`: the pop-in, then the emote's two frames, then a fade). `GlowCue` lights or puts out the violet glow an actor carries (`cutscene_glow.tscn`: light, halo and sparkles, #9a5cff) at a height above its feet. `PropCue` puts a sprite on the floor under an id or takes it away (the journal and dagger flat on the floor by the Scene 4 skeleton's hand, crops from Winlu's Fantasy Interior). On a set the skeleton takes the set's `LitArea` shade, like the props beside it. `SigilCue` brightens the Encounter 4 sigil (`SewerSigil.Awaken`). `QuakeCue` shakes the camera for a moment and drops dust (`cutscene_dust.tscn`) over a tile. Without a cue: the speaker of each line hops 2 sprite pixels when it stands on the set (`CutsceneSet.Speakers` maps speaker names to actors); a walking actor splashes (`cutscene_splash.tscn`) when it steps onto a tile under a puddle or slime decal (`DecorSprite.Wet`, collected by `SewerDressing.WetTiles`); creature actors darken like the halo terrain while they stand past `LitArea`. `letterbox.tscn` (layer 19, under the black, the narration and the dialogue box) eases in 30 px bars at the top and bottom while a set plays; the bottom bar sits in the 36 px gap under the dialogue box.
- Cutscene air (`CutsceneSet.Atmosphere.cs`): the set adds `torch_motes.tscn` (12 slow warm specks, #FFD08A) to each torch in `SewerDressing.Torches`. `GradeCue` blends the set's `Grade` overlay (a multiply rect on canvas layer 0, under the letterbox) to a tint over `Seconds`; white is neutral and hides it, and the set frees it. Scene 5 tints #FFDBD1 as the rats pour in; Scene 7 tints #C7EBDB as the sigil wakes and back to white on the Friend's line about the beasts.
- Cutscene camera rules: each scene opens with one lead-in shot, then keeps one side of the action. Every later shot blends 1.5 s or more, and all of a scene's yaws sit within an 80 degree arc.
- `prologue.tscn`: `PrologueDirector` runs its `Beats` (Narration, Dialogue, Encounter). A set-backdrop beat builds its set behind the black (a parked combat board is released first, since both use the same shader globals, sprite tint and environment), plays, and frees the set unless the next beat uses the same set scene. A narration beat with a `RevealsSet` page builds the next beat's set first. Encounters go through `IEncounterRunner` (`Prepare`, `Run`, `Release`). The scene's `Runner` node is `PrologueEncounterRunner`. With `EncounterRunnerNode` empty the director uses `StubEncounterRunner`, which wins every fight at once.

## Prologue encounters

To play the Prologue, run the project (the title is the main scene) and press Prologue. It plays the narrative intro, scenes 1 to 7 and the four encounters, then returns to the title.

Scripts are in `scripts/tutorial/encounters/`:

- `PrologueEncounterRunner` holds one combat scene (`scenes/combat/combat.tscn`, no intro, no result banner), the two heroes and the creature journal for all four fights. `Prepare` builds the board with the HUD hidden and holds the first turn; `Run` shows the HUD and plays the script; `Release` tears the fight down. HP carries over; the Friend's Acid Flasks reset to 2 each fight; after Encounter 3 the Friend's Treat Wounds heals each hurt hero to full minus 1, and only a fallen hero is restored before Encounter 4. While the dialogue box or a rule tip shows, the HUD takes no input; a step tip leaves it live.
- Tips name what they point at with `TutorialFocus` (scripts/tutorial): the action squares, reaction diamond or HP bar of a hero's card, a command menu entry with its open sub-menu, a knowledge row on a creature's card, the roll banner, the timeline, a unit's condition icons, the Journal button, the move bands, the gold tiles, a tile set or units. `CombatScene.ResolveFocus` turns it into screen quads every frame (a block of tiles is one quad; a unit adds only a rect the box keeps clear of and, unless it is context, the point its arrow aims at), and `CombatScene.HoldFocus` pins the hero card or the creature card and holds the roll banner while the tip shows. `EncounterStage.Rule` and `Step` open the tips; `StepUntilSpent` and `StepUntilTurnEnd` close a step tip when its step is done. Tip text uses PF2e rule terms (Stride, Strike, multiple attack penalty, off-guard, weakness, dying) and reads its numbers from the engine. A move to a tile off the gold set opens a step tip on the gold tiles. While any tip shows, the forecast slot drops the tutorial's move hint. Board cutouts cover only tiles on screen and outside the HUD panels. Treat Wounds and the Encounter 4 post-mortem stay centred.
- `Encounter1Script` to `Encounter4Script` follow spec section 3 (the PDF version for Encounters 2 and 3, per section 8). `EncounterStage` holds each turn at its start so a script reads the fight one turn at a time. `TreatWoundsBeat` plays the Friend's Treat Wounds after Encounter 2.
- Forced player rolls are queued when the player commits the order (`CombatScene.BeforePlayerCommand`), so the d20 is read from the state the order runs in.
- Recall Knowledge has no range: any foe the actor perceives is a target.
- The combat log shows persistent damage as it is gained, each tick with the HP pair, and the recovery that ends it (`CombatLogBridge.Persistent.cs`).
- When Vicious Bite fires, the "Abilities" row on the biting creature's card pulses (`CombatScene.HiddenAbility.cs`). The row text and the log do not name the ability.
- A wipe in Encounter 4 shows the post-mortem popup and restarts the fight live, without the opening steps.

Combat additions used by the Prologue: a Throw entry under Abilities for every carried bomb (splash preview on hover, the flask lobbed to the target before the damage, direct, splash and weakness in the log, "Splash" floaters); `CombatSetup.FixedInitiative` and `RecallAfterSuccess`; `CombatScene.TargetFilter`, `AutoEndTurn`, `SetHudVisible`, `SetStoryModal` and `ResultBannerEnabled`.

A bomb throw lobs the flask before its damage (`scenes/fx/flask_throw.tscn`, `scripts/fx/FlaskThrow.cs`, built by `tools/vfx/flask_throw/`). The flask leaves the thrower's hand at 0.6 of the body height at the end of the lunge and turns end over end on an arc to the target's impact point, the height the splash lands at, dropping a few specks of acid. Its shadow lies 15 mm above the board surface under it, sampled at its own spot every frame: the terrain, or the top of the masonry where the wall cut takes a wall or pillar down. The shadow shrinks and lightens with the flask's height above that spot, moves onto a new surface at once where the floor steps, eases its strength over a few frames there, and fades over the last metre before the target. The flight is 0.4 s for 3 tiles plus 0.03 s a tile, at most 0.65 s, and the beat waits for it whatever the combat speed. The splash or the miss plays as the flask lands, and a missed throw flies too. The presenter knows the throw by `BombEventKeys.Thrown` on its AttackRolled.

Acid damage plays the HD-2D acid splash (`scenes/fx/acid_splash.tscn`, `scripts/fx/AcidSplash.cs`) in place of the hit spark. A flask that strikes a creature gets the whole effect at its feet: the flask breaking on its body, a green light pulse, and spray and puddles over the squares around it. A creature that only the splash reaches, and any other acid damage, gets the minor splash: the acid splat, drops and bubbles on its body, with no glass, flash, light or floor layers. A persistent acid tick is other acid damage, so it gets the minor splash too.

Every HP change and death reaches the board through the session's `BattleRunner`. The runner watches each combatant's HP. An action's own DamageDealt or Healed claims the change it shows, and the runner plays the rest (a persistent tick, a fall, Drained) after the action, or before the next turn or round boundary. The runner is the only source of CreatureDied: it plays right after the damage that killed the creature, or at the next flush for a death with no damage (a failed recovery check). A turn ends in two steps (`TurnManager.FinishTurn`, then `AdvanceTurn`), and the session checks for victory between them, so a creature killed by its own end-of-turn tick dies on screen before the result and the next creature's turn never starts.

Every heal plays the HD-2D Treat Wounds wrap (`scenes/fx/treat_wounds.tscn`, `scripts/fx/TreatWounds.cs`) at the healed unit's feet: a bandage wound twice round the chest and knotted, drawn on the sprite's own pixel grid (the body scales by the sprite's pixel size over 0.028 m), then a few sparkles on the band. A unit whose HP bar is under 0.85 m gets the rat-sized set. A faint lantern light shows while the medic works, moved 0.6 m from above the head toward the healer.

A slain creature disintegrates (`scenes/fx/death_dust.tscn`, `scripts/fx/DeathDust.cs`, built by `tools/vfx/death_dust/`). Its sprite breaks up on its own texel grid from the back down, and the texels at the front glow pale orange (`death_progress` in `lit_sprite.gdshader`; the look values are on the Lit material of `billboard_sprite.tscn`). Each texel leaves as a pixel-square particle in its own colour at the moment the shader drops it, then drifts up and to the right on screen and cools toward ash (`SpriteErosion` holds the same threshold as the shader). The body is gone after 0.6 s, and the death beat waits that long whatever the combat speed. The dust is gone by 1.3 s. The torch shadow erodes with the body, because the eroding sprite moves 0.1 mm on alternate frames and so makes the torches redraw it. A hero at 0 HP goes Dying; a hero that dies keeps the old corpse tint.

`CombatScene.StartEncounter` loads the HD-2D effect scenes (flask throw, acid splash, Treat Wounds, death dust) as the board builds (`FxLibrary.PreloadEncounterEffects`), so the first throw, hit, heal or kill of a fight does not load one.

## Spikes

Headless, each prints `SPIKE RESULT: PASS`:


for `combat_smoke`, `crawl`, `cutscene`, `dressing`, `prologue_content`, `prologue_run`, `reaction_kill`, `roster_shot`, `story_flow` and `tutorial_hooks`.

`reaction_kill_spike` runs the harness fight on the flat board in a fixed order (hero, friend, three rats, every rat beside the hero). In each round one rat steps away and the hero's Reactive Strike kills it: in round 1 the last rat in the order, so the round wraps; in round 2 the first rat, with another rat next; in round 3 the last rat alive, which ends the fight. It checks that every turn starts once and in order, that the dead rat stays the current actor for `RemovedTurnBeatSeconds` (on `combat.tscn`), that its TurnEnded, its end-of-turn effects and an "until the end of its turn" countdown come before the next turn starts, and that the command menu shows only during a started player turn (never during a reaction prompt or the dead rat's beat). A second fight puts one rat at 1 HP with persistent acid: at its turn end the tick's DamageDealt and one CreatureDied play before EncounterEnded, with the rat still the current actor and no turn started after it. Rendered, it saves the round 1 kill to `dev_shots/reaction_kill_prompt.png`, `_kill.png`, `_beat.png` and `_next.png`.

`crawl_spike` checks the narration crawl sceneries (`scenes/story/crawl/`) at the window sizes on the spike scene (16:9, 21:9, 4:3, 720p). Each `DriftLayer` is drawn at the integer scale that covers the viewport height. Headless, it checks that every horizontal tiling layer covers the viewport at rest and at full drift. Rendered (off-screen, as below), it also captures each scenery at rest and at full drift over a magenta sentinel, fails on any sentinel pixel, and saves `dev_shots/crawl_<scenery>_<w>x<h>_rest.png` and `_drift.png`.

Per line it checks the flavour: the speaker hops once and nobody else does, every emote shows above its actor, each glow, prop and sigil cue left its state, a quake shook the camera, a grade cue set its tint, the letterbox is up with its bottom bar under the dialogue box, and creatures are shaded as the floor under them. Per case it checks that every splash was on a wet tile, that a creature placed past the lit area was drawn darker, and that the letterbox is down afterwards. Walls that hide a listener are printed, not failed. `UNGILDED_CUTSCENE` (a comma list of `s1`, `s3a`, `s4`, `s6`) runs only those cases. Rendered (off-screen, as below), it saves `dev_shots/cutscene_page6_crossfade.png`, `cutscene_page6.png`, the first line and every line with a `ShotCue` of each scene (`cutscene_s1_01.png`, `cutscene_s3a_01.png`, `cutscene_s4_01.png` and so on), and `cutscene_e1_after.png` to `cutscene_e4_after.png`. ## Sewer biome

The sewer theme rows are `MapThemes.Sewer` (surfaces) and `BackdropThemes.Sewer` (halo). A Prologue board brings its own halo (`HaloPlan`, painted by `SewerBoard` and `SewerHalo`, applied by `SkirtPlan`): every halo tile is wall unless the board opens it as floor, channel, sludge, a step or a footbridge, and a run carries a board edge out to the rim. A generated sewer board gets the synthesized halo instead: a walkway two tiles wide, then one uniform wall mass, with a culvert where a channel of one or two tiles leaves. Channels on the Prologue boards sit 2 corner units (0.25 m) below the floor, a step a creature takes in its stride.

The camera cuts walls (`CutawayMap`, `TerrainCutaway`, `assets/shaders/cutaway.gdshaderinc`). The cut follows wall faces: an outline wall comes down while the board floor it bounds, or the passage floor behind a solid run, lies on its far side from the camera, and the masonry in front of it comes down with it. A pier with floor on both sides keeps its height unless it hides board floor. The ring of masonry touching open floor stays as a 0.375 m stub with a stone cap and a thin, slightly lighter edge where the cut ends. Neighbouring stubs join into one top, even a step apart. Masonry further out drops to a dark floor, so the cut reads as a wall in section with darkness beyond. The props on cut walls hide, except that a torch keeps its light on while its sprite is away (`TerrainCutaway.Track` with `keepLights`), so the floor stays lit from the camera side; the halo mist keeps to the far side. The cut changes with the camera's quadrant as it turns, and holds its side for about 5 degrees past each axis so it does not flicker. On a cutscene set the cut is chosen as each shot starts, from the pose the shot ends on, and holds through the blend. Where a cut wall meets a standing one, the standing wall ends in a brick face drawn with the wall material. Clicks hit the stubs, not the hidden walls. Free-standing walls are never cut. The jambs of a culvert arch (the walls on both sides of its mouth, across the arch's depth) stay at full height while the camera is on the board side of the mouth, and the arch hides while any jamb is cut (`CulvertArches`, `TerrainCutaway.KeepStanding`). The Prologue pillars are 0.625 m tall, and a pillar drops to its stub while a unit stands right behind it from the camera (`CombatScene` passes the unit tiles every frame).

The look is `scenes/looks/sewer.tscn`. Its `Dressing` child (`SewerDressing`) places the props after each terrain build. `TorchesEnabled` on that node is off since 2026-10-22: no wall torches hang, and the torch checks in `dressing_spike`, `sewer_look_shot_spike` (hung torches, the E4 cut, the flicker and the pool shot) and `cutscene_spike` (motes) skip with a printed note. Turn it on to bring the torch rules back. Each prop has a rule, and `SewerDressing.Placed` lists every torch, grate and floor decal with the rule that placed it. Torches with flickering lights hang on one jamb of each passage mouth and each culvert (the lower end along the wall), on one jamb of each recess at least two tiles deep (the Encounter 4 apse) once the walls have theirs, and along each chamber wall of at least `TorchMinRun` tiles (`SewerWalls`; a passage or culvert mouth ends a wall) one per `TorchSpacing` tiles of its length along the board edge, rounded. A wall torch goes on a pier front of a ribbed wall, or at the back of a bay when no pier front can take it. A torch never hangs closer than `TorchMinGap` to another torch, never on the sigil's wall, and only on a face the cutaway leaves standing from some camera that sees it; among equal faces it takes one the opening view shows (`OpeningQuadrant`). `TorchNotes` says where each mouth's torch went or why each jamb was refused. Wall grates sit where a board channel runs into a wall. Floor grates lie two tiles down each passage (further out, up to `PassageGrateMaxRing`, while the tile is reserved) and beside a channel's end, no two within `GrateMinGap` tiles and none within `WallPropGap` of a torch. Puddles lie under outfall pipes, at the floor side of an arched culvert, around floor grates and beside channels; slime lies beside the sludge; bones lie in clusters of two or three pieces near the skeleton, in dead ends, niches and inside corners (`BoneClusters` per board), at least `BonePropGap` tiles from torches, grates, rubble and outfall puddles; rubble lies against a wall on the halo floor. No two floor decals of one kind touch, and the board and the halo each have a density cap. The rest: pipes that pour into the sludge, a water pipe on each long far wall, the sigil on the far wall behind the `tyrant` anchor, the marks a halo asks for (outfall pipes, the skeleton leaned back against its wall at `SkeletonLean` with no clutter on the tiles around it, `SewerMarks`), a vaulted arch over each culvert mouth (`CulvertArches`), streaks at every run that leaves the board, drifting along the board's flow (`CulvertFlows`, `scenes/props/culvert_flow.tscn`): out of the downstream mouth, and in from the halo at the upstream one, a stone kerb along the channel edges (`ChannelKerbs`), a slime stain behind every pipe, a faint mist rising off the board's channels and sludge (`ChannelMist`, `scenes/props/channel_mist.tscn`; cool over water, a fainter olive over sludge, `WaterMistColor` and `SludgeMistColor`), and drips falling from the ceiling over each puddle. Floor decals stay off the anchors in `PrologueMaps`, off difficult terrain and off water. The board gets only flat decals.

Unit and actor sprites are lit (`assets/shaders/lit_sprite.gdshader`, the material override in `scenes/combat/billboard_sprite.tscn`) with no light of their own. Its normal leans from world up halfway to the camera (`face_camera`), is bent left and right across the body (`bend`) and is tipped by the slope of the frame's normal page (`normal_tex`, `normal_share`). Each pixel takes its light and shadow at the point of the body's upright axis at its own height, no lower than just above the feet (`light_on_axis`, `feet_y`, kept by `BillboardSpriteAnimator`), so a light's direction and falloff change from feet to head: the Friend's lantern lights his legs more than his head. The diffuse wraps a little past the side facing the light (`wrap`). The animator loads `<frame>_n.png` beside each frame once (`SpriteNormalPages`); a frame without one draws with `bend` only. Sprites cast shadows from every shadowed light (the wall torches; the sewer look's key light is a faint cool fill with no shadow, since a sewer has no sky). In the shadow pass the quad stands upright on its feet, turns to face the light and stands `shadow_push` behind the axis along the ground, so the shadow is the full silhouette and the sprite never shadows itself. The sewer look's ambient comes from a gradient sky (cool above, dark and warm below), so the ambient also follows the normals. The blob decal under each token and actor stays as a contact shadow. The look's `SpriteTint` and the halo shade multiply on top.

The Friend carries a hand lantern on his combat token and his cutscene actor (`scenes/combat/hand_lantern.tscn`, `HandLantern`; `Carriers` names who carries it). The 9x14 lantern sprite hangs from his empty off hand (`HookPixel`, the master's `pivot_grip_far_relaxed`) on his billboard and draws in front of the hand. His frames (`assets/sprites/heroes/friend/base.png`, `rest_1..5.png`) are the master flattened with the off-hand flask layers hidden; the master itself still has the flask on. Its light is warm, short (4 m) and unshadowed, sits a little above and in front of the lantern so his own sprite takes it, and breathes like the torches. It stays unshadowed because it sits in front of his body: a shadow from it would throw his whole silhouette onto the wall behind. The lantern sways while he moves and hangs still at rest. In combat its light and glow fade out over a second while his HP bar shows 0 (dying or dead) and light again when he is healed; the lantern sprite stays.

The ground shader reads wetness from the baked atlas alpha (`MapSurfaceStyle.Wetness`): a wet surface is glossy, and sludge also ripples. The sewer stone floor has no wetness, so it stays matte under the torches; only the sludge carries a sheen. The water shader scrolls `FlowTexture` across the channels. Each map in `PrologueMaps` names the side its water and sludge flow toward (`SewerBoard.Flow`: E1 and E2 west, E3 and E4 south). The water scroll moves that way at the look's `WaterFlowSpeed`, and the sludge ripple drifts that way at `SludgeFlowSpeed`.
