# Tharr

- **id:** `tharr`
- **Class:** Cleric (Warpriest)
- **Ancestry:** Dwarf
- **Age:** middle-aged for a dwarf (~160)
- **Pronouns:** he/him
- **Kind:** starting-party PC
- **Associated building:** Command Post (construction / planning table)
- **Deity:** Valdûr, the Stonefather (see `../deities/valdur.md`)

## Vibe

Tharr is gruff and dependable in equal measure. He takes genuine pride in solid craftsmanship,
and if a wall is going up under his watch, it is going up right. He is quietly relieved to
finally have help at the outpost, though he would never say so directly. There is a stubbornness
underneath the stoicism, the kind that comes from holding something together alone for too long.
His faith lives in his hands and in his hammer.

Waste offends him more than ruin does. Ruin is honest; waste is a choice somebody made.

Solitude and eight years of upkeep have worn him down to a withdrawn, solemn floor. That is
where he starts — not his whole shape, only what is left when there has been no one to be
anything else for. He keeps his feeling below the surface; the one thing that still breaks it
is waste, and it breaks as anger, brief and clamped fast rather than vented. Underneath is a
cheerful, fulfilled dwarf who took genuine joy in good work and good company, and the game is
the slow return of him: as the outpost comes back under the player's hands and the two come to
know each other, he opens by degrees. That warmth is earned across the whole game, not given at
the gate.

## Role in the town

Tharr serves as the outpost's construction-master. He leads building repair, restoration, and
the construction of new structures. He operates the planning table at the Command Post and
carries in his head a running inventory of everything the outpost needs and in what order it
ought to be done. When he is not working stone, he is swinging his warhammer at whatever
threatens the walls he built.

He is also, by a paperwork fiction eight years old, the outpost's warden of record: the legal
authority the player and Fenwick report to on arrival. He never wanted command and does not
much perform it. He assigns work.

## Background

Tharr has laid stone for the crown for the better part of a century. Under Lanric he worked
the golden-age projects: roads into the frontier, bridges, chapel foundations, garrison walls.
The kingdom the songs describe, the one the player grew up believing in, has Tharr's tool
marks on it.

The Ashfall ended that kingdom, and Tharr spent the thirty years since watching the crown
squander what he had helped build. Repair orders that never came. Projects abandoned half
built, which is anathema to Valdûr. Crews dissolved to save coin, good works surrendered to
weather because a clerk somewhere weighed them against nearer needs. To a Valdûrite, stone and
metal and toil are gifts, and the caretaker ministries have spent his life's portion of them
badly. His disillusionment is not a mood. By his faith's lights, the kingdom is sinning, and
he has been made to watch.

The garrison posting at this outpost came late in his service, and eight years ago the crown
recalled it: a budget decision, nothing more. Nobody argued the border did not matter; they
declined to pay for it. Tharr would not board the wagon. This outpost was not going to become
one more wasted effort, not while he could still lift a hammer. His commander, weighing a
desertion report and everything it would cost against a single line of ink, wrote him down as
voluntarily detached, caretaker of record, and the ministry stamped it. On paper Tharr is the
outpost's warden and its garrison of one. Among the soldiers who were there, the story is that
the old dwarf deserted to his post. It is mostly told with respect.

Eight years alone followed. He could not hold everything, so he chose: which roofs to brace,
which walls to patch, which buildings to let the forest have. He can name the year he gave
each one up. Every season he filed a requisition for men and materials, because doing the
paperwork right is part of doing the work right, and every season nothing came.

A few nights before the relief arrived, the forest pressed him for the first time: a wolf pack
out of the deep wood, led by something too big to be natural, came at the gate in the dark. In
eight years the walls had never been tested — the forest had only ever crept. He held the gate
alone, and he is repairing it when the party walks up (intro Scene 2). The brace took the last
sound timber he had — which is why the party's first task is a gathering trip (the Day-1 loop,
`../tutorial.md`). He reads the attack plainly: the pack will come again.

The answer to his requisitions, when it finally arrived, was two academy graduates on foot with
a trader they had met on the road. He asked for a garrison and got children. He put them to
work the same afternoon.

## Arrival

Starting party. Tharr is already at the outpost when the player arrives. He is the lone holdout.

## Notes (deferred / later)

- Build: Cleric (Warpriest doctrine) + Marshal FA line. Warhammer (Valdûr's favored weapon),
  steel shield, breastplate. Deity: Valdûr, the Stonefather (heal font, Crafting divine skill,
  Earth/Creation/Fire/Toil domains).
- The warhammer's Shove trait gives Tharr a defensive and controlling combat style that fits
  his personality well.
- Heart events reveal his war years in small pieces: he served through the war keeping roads
  and fortifications standing under fire, like most dwarves in crown service. Service material
  only; no lost-crew tragedy.
- Kin: he left the hold young for crown work and never looked back. No feud, no letters, no
  care. His biography is his work.
- Restoration flavor: every ruined building the player restores is one Tharr chose to let go.
  Repair dialogue can draw on this; he remembers what each building cost him to abandon.
- Arc: his motive starts as refusal (prevent one more waste) and turns positive through play.
  Emotionally he begins withdrawn and solemn — worn flat by the solitude — and opens by degrees
  toward the cheerful, fulfilled dwarf underneath. Two levers move him: the keep coming back up
  (every repair is one waste undone, which is his whole creed) and his heart level with the
  player. His warmth is a payoff the intro only floors, never delivers. A late heart event
  should have him design and raise something new, his first new work in thirty years, rather
  than another repair.
- Intro Scene 2 shows the floor of him and one flash off it. The emotional order across the
  scene is weariness (the approach, his first turn from the gate), unvoiced surprise that anyone
  came at all (it shows only in the fact that he keeps talking), a single flash of anger as it
  lands that the crown's answer to eight years of requisitions is two green recruits, then the
  clamp — the anger swallowed, the requisitions arithmetic delivered cold, work assigned. He
  ends the scene as withdrawn as he began, only pointed at a job now. He does not warm up in the
  intro.
- Sprite: uses the "cleric" Mana Seed sheet for now.
- Tharr should feel like the outpost's backbone. He was here before anyone else and will be here
  long after.
