# Eidolarch

A two-player dueling card game. Two duelists, mages or plain fighters, contest a place of power where a gate can be opened for an otherworldly Eidolon; the winner becomes its Eidolarch. The folder and code name are still `zenith`. Design: `../designs/zenith.md`.

- **Engine:** Godot 4.6 (Forward Plus), GDScript
- **Status:** playable hotseat and online prototype. Rules engine with headless tests, 3D playspace, deck select, eight starter decks whose cards carry their full mechanics (themed titles, no flavor text yet).
- **Run:** Open `zenith/project.godot` in Godot 4.6 and press F5. Title → Duel the AI (first option; Hotseat duel sits under Local) → pick duelists → matchup → play. The table swings to whoever has to decide, behind a hand-off screen.
- **Against the AI:** Title → Duel the AI → pick your duelist and the AI's, and Easy, Normal or Hard in the footer. You sit at seat 1 and the AI's hand stays face down.
- **Online:** every online duel is refereed on the duel server (`docs/duel_server.md`). One player presses Host a duel, which opens a room on the server and shows a five-letter share code (copied to the clipboard); the other pastes it and presses Join. Both connect out to the server's fixed address, so nobody forwards ports. The select screen becomes the lobby: each client picks and locks in its own duelist without seeing the other's, both land on the matchup screen once both have locked, and the server deals. Neither client runs the rules or holds the seed; each receives only what its seat may see and sits at its own side of the table. Typing an address (`host:port`) instead of a code connects to a client hosting on a plain ENet port, the LAN dev path. Transports live in `scripts/net/` behind `NetTransport`, so a Steam transport would slot in beside them.

## Engine

`DuelEngine` runs the duel as a state machine. It advances on its own until a player has to decide something, then exposes a `Prompt` whose `options` are the only `Command`s it accepts. `submit(command)` applies one and runs on to the next prompt or the end of the game. Everything that happens is appended to `events` as `GameEvent`s for the client to animate and log. Same seed plus same commands replays the same game.

```gdscript
var engine: DuelEngine = DuelEngine.new()
engine.setup([deck_a, deck_b], library, strike_table, seed_value)
engine.start()
while not engine.is_over():
	var choice: Command = engine.prompt.options[0]   # a driver picks here
	engine.submit(choice)
```

Prompt kinds: `reserve` (reserve_in / reserve_done), `non_combat` (place / relic / done), `declare` (declare / skip), `attack_action` (attack, with value `"empower"` for the Empowered version / use / power / copied_attack / final_strike / pass), `respond` (counter / decline), `defense` (defend / power_defend / no_defense), `control` (context `role` attacker or defender), `redirect` (target), `endurance` (endure / no_endure), `capture_instead` (capture / deal_damage, for an attacking Ally with the capture trait), `critical` (capture / discard_ally / lower_fervor / no_critical), `keep` (keep / discard_all), `recover` (recover / no_recover), `pay` (value = Energy paid), `discard_choice`, `pick_in_play` (+ pick_none), `name_card` (value = title), `pick_option`. A prompt raised by a card's effect carries `source` (the card's uid) and `card_title` in its context, and a `pick_option` also says what the pick is for (`purpose`: forbid_type, stop_kind, look_at, capture), so titles name the card asking and the client can show it.

Batches: a prompt that picks several cards (`reserve`, a discard of two or more, a search or pick-in-play for several) also sets `batch_type`, `batch_min`, `batch_max` on the `Prompt` and `PromptView`. One Command of that type with `value` = an Array of card uids taken from the options answers it in a single step; `Prompt.accept` normalises and checks it, and the referee refuses anything outside the options, repeats, or the wrong count. Clients build it with `PromptView.batch_option(uids)`. The one-card options stay legal, so drivers that pick one at a time still work.

Effects run through a queue that pauses on a choice prompt and resumes afterwards, so a card can ask its owner or the target to pick cards mid-resolution.

A hand Combat card is an attack-phase `use` only when playing it does something (it has secondary effects). A pure counter ("use when needed", `counter: "combat"`) is offered only in the `respond` window that opens when the other player uses or defends with a Combat card; in its owner's own attack phase it can only be thrown away for a Final Strike.

**Log.** Every state change is an event with a line from `CardText.event_line`, so the log tells the whole story and not only the players' commands: a card whose trigger fires gets "<card> triggers when entering Combat" (once, at the first effect that actually applies, never for a card the player just played or used), every Energy change from an effect names its source card and the before and after, forbids and other standing effects read as they land ("may not perform Arts for the rest of Combat"), flags such as a skipped attack phase or a forced Combat are announced when set, and wounds outside an attack's own damage, removals from the game, looks at the deck and bonds each have a line. Lines are worded per seat (`event_line(ev, engine, seat)`): a card is named only if that seat may see it now or is the player who acted, so the opponent of a draw check learns whether it matched and that the effect follows, never which card was drawn, and a Reserve swap or a search into hand reads "a card" on the other side.

**Seat views.** Clients never read the engine. `Referee` owns it and answers each seat with a `SeatUpdate`: the log lines for what happened, a `SeatView` (public standings, every zone as uids, and a card table where cards that seat may not see carry only uid and zone: Life Decks, the other seat's hand and Reserve), and a `PromptView` when the next decision is that seat's (kind, title, options with ready-made labels). The engine holds at most one prompt per player (`DuelEngine.prompts`, `prompt_of(seat)`; `prompt` is the first): the Reserve swap and the Discard step ask both players at once, so nobody waits on the other for a choice that only concerns their own cards, and everything else is one prompt at a time. `Referee.submit(seat, command)` refuses anything that is not that seat's own pending option. Everything has `to_dict` / `from_dict` so the same objects cross the wire. Card uids are dealt after the shuffle so a face-down uid says nothing about the card.

**Attack in the air.** `SeatView.attack` carries the public shape of the current attack plus `damage`, the breakdown from `DuelEngine.damage_breakdown`: the Strike Table lookup (both Mights and bands, or the Art base, or printed numbers), then `adds`, one entry per card that changes it (Drills, Mastery, Grounds, attachments, floating modifiers, Empower, paid Energy), and the totals. Before the defense it is the forecast the defender is answering; once `landed` is set the battle sequence has fixed the same numbers, and `stages_dealt`, `life_dealt` and `life_remaining` follow the flips. `damage_breakdown` also returns `overflow` (Energy past what the target is standing on) and `wounds` (`life` plus that overflow), because the deal path turns overflow into life cards and a forecast that ignored it read a costly Strike as costing nothing. The HUD prompt panel leads a damage decision with one large number, the wound total, shown only when there is one (an attack the target can absorb falls back to the tiles rather than a large zero), and `OptionView.outcome` (`DuelEngine.option_outcome`, filled for `endurance` and `defense`) says what each choice would leave: hovering a button or a legal card redraws the number at that value with the change beside it, so "what does this buy me" is answered by the number rather than a sentence. Prompts with no single number to lead with fall back to at most three tiles (`DuelHud._facts_for`). The step-by-step maths stays in the log rather than the panel, which gets a "Strike Table: Might 19 (C) vs 9 (B) gives 2 stages." line and a "Modifiers: +4 stages (Relentless Fury). Total 8 stages." line per attack. A Final Strike is a legal option on every hand card, so the client never fires it from a bare card click: it sits behind a "Final Strike…" button that opens the hand in the tray, and a card whose only action is a Final Strike opens the sub-choice tray instead of committing.

**Outcome.** `SeatView.battle_step` says where the battle sequence stands while an attack is in the air, and `SeatView.last_attack` keeps the outcome of the last attack of the Combat after the attack itself has left the view: who attacked, with what (`source_title`, `performer_title`), whether it was stopped and by what (`stopped_by` is `{card, how}` with `how` card / power / shield / floating, `stopped_by_title` the wording), `stages_dealt`, `life_dealt`, `target_title`, `endurance_prevented`, and the critical-damage choice. It clears when the next attack is declared or Combat ends. The log gets the same tally as one line at `attack_end` ("Bram's Overhand Cut lands for 3 stages, 2 wounds." / "… is stopped by Tide Parry."), a "Combat: Bram attacks first." line at `combat_begin`, and a line when a defense is skipped for a reason the player should know (no legal defense, or a Final Strike this Combat).

**Attack forecasts.** While a seat's attack action is pending, `SeatView.forecasts` (only that seat's view) carries a breakdown per offered attack, keyed by the option's card uid: hand and Remain cards, a Power attack under the personality's uid, and a bare Final Strike for a card with no attack of its own (`is_final`). `DuelEngine.attack_forecasts` builds each one the way declaring it would (`_build_attack`: the card's conditional lines, the attacker's standing effects) and runs the same `_damage_calc`, after paying the attack's Energy cost so the Strike Table is read at the right stage (`cost_stages` travels with it); an Empower option adds its totals under `empowered`. The hand shows each card's total as a chip ("6 Energy", "4 wounds"; "Final: 2 Energy" in grey for a card that can only be thrown away), and the quick view lists the steps under the face, so a "+3 Energy" card is compared by what it would land for, not its printed bonus. Faces carry the printed base in a badge over the art ("Strike +3 Energy", "Strike Table Energy", "Art 4 wounds", "Focused Strike 7 Energy"), from `CardText.attack_badge`. `CardText.breakdown_steps` is the one wording for a breakdown's steps (the log and the quick view alike).

**Event data for animation.** `SeatUpdate.lines` entries carry `type`, `player` and the seat's `line`; for the event types in `Referee.ANIMATED` they also carry `data` with only the public fields listed there (card uids that reached a pile, damage numbers, the attack's kind and source), the same for both seats. Anything not listed reaches clients as its line alone, so nothing hidden leaks through the animation channel.

They also carry `state`: the table numbers as they stood *when that event fired* (`GameEvent.state` — `energy` per personality uid, `fervor` per player, `zones` as [life deck, hand, discard, removed] per player, and the turn position (`turn`, `step`, `phase`, `active`, `attacker`)). One update's view is the state after everything in it, so without this a client drew the end state under every beat: a duelist that charged to full Energy and was drained again in the same update never appeared charged. `DuelEngine.record_display_state` stamps the events; `Referee.setup` turns it on for its own engine and `clone()` leaves it off, so the AI's simulations never pay for it. `duel_view._play_update` hands each beat's `state` to the HUD, the panels, the markers and the phase banner, and clears it at the end, so nothing on screen runs ahead of the beat under it.

**Effective values.** A `SeatPlayer` carries every per-player number the rules currently apply, computed by `DuelEngine` getters (`fervor_needed`, `fervor_gain`, `recover_gain`, `fervor_shield`, `aspect_shield`, `restrictions`) from base state plus standing effects: a Mastery that demands 6 Fervor of its opponent, a forbid in force. Clients render those fields and never a rules constant, so a card that moves one moves the display in the same update. `PlayerState.fervor_needed` is the adjustable base behind the first of these (ops `fervor_needed` and `set_fervor_needed`); the opponent's Mastery demand is layered on top, the higher wins.

Covered: Reserve swap at setup, bracket first-player rule, 7-step turn, 6-phase Combat, 15-step battle sequence, Strike Table, Arts with costs and cost discounts, overflow to wounds, Endurance (fixed and X), Defense Shields, Focused and unstoppable attacks, unpreventable damage, prevent-all, floating stop-all and stop-next, Fervor and Aspects (set, lose, advance, threshold changes, Fervor and aspect shields), Allies (placement, overlay, control at any stage, redirect, protection), Drills with school lock, modifiers, shields, once-per-Combat uses and Drill protection, Seals (placement, bypass, capture, capture effects, both win paths), Grounds with standing forbids, Masteries (modifiers, entering-Combat, on-success, use), Relics with per-game powers and passives, Final Strike, Remain, Empower, counter window for Combat cards, attack variants ("if X is in control, +3"), per-Ally and Surge-scaled damage, pay-any-Energy attacks, attachments (modifiers, entering-Combat draws, wound removal), constant powers (focused attacks, first styled attack unstoppable, on-attack, turn-start, shared with Allies), named-card locks, copied attacks, forbids by card type / attack type / powers / Mastery / Drills / end-Combat / stop-all / Seals / everything-but-attacking, searches by type, school, title, tag, signature, or Reserve into hand or play, start-in-play Drills, hand discards chosen by either player or shuffled back.

Also covered: "you may" prompts on any effect line with a `then` list that runs only on a yes, pay-any-Energy effects with a per-unit follow-up, look-at-top-or-bottom-N with a pick, searches that let the searcher choose among distinct hits, instead-of-damage choices after a successful attack, attacks that need two stops, Ally powers usable without control, Drills that promote "if successful" text or discard themselves, Remain cards that shuffle back when unused, returning removed cards, forbids with an Energy escape clause, forced Combat declarations.

And: Bonds (two named Allies fold under a Bond card that fights as one at full Energy, burning a life card each turn until it ends), wound triggers on life cards ("if this card is discarded from your Life Deck…", optionally delayed to the next fight-back phase), look-at with a play-instead option and reshuffle, searches by what a card does (`has_effect`), an end-turn effect, and a response window during the opponent's Declare step (`opponent_declare` trigger on a Non-Combat in play).

Also: damage multipliers (one at most, the biggest applies) and caps on modifiers (`multiply`, `cap_stages`, `cap_life`; caps bite at deal time), attacks that cannot be reduced (`no_reduce` on the attack, or a floating `no_reduce`), Seals immune to card effects that do not name them, cards that end Combat usable only as an attack action, "X only" attachments that go on X wherever X stands (`attach` with `to: "character"`, playable while X is in play; `to: "named"` with `character` attaches to a different named personality, needs X in control and the named one on the table, and `attachment.limit_attached` caps copies per host), an unplayable drawn school Drill shown and shuffled back (`shuffle_back` option in the Non-Combat step), and look-at effects that put the looked-at cards back in a chosen order (`rearrange: true`).

Timing windows, audited against the reference rulebook and rulings on 2026-09-16: a spent Duelist (Energy 0 or 1) may hand the attack phase to an Ally at the start of it (`control` prompt with `role: attacker`), and resumes control once above that; a Non-Combat in play used as a defense is spent; after a Final Strike the player cannot defend either (Shields and standing stops still apply); a skipped attack phase never happened, so the passes around it are not consecutive; a Relic's power is offered where its `relic_step` says (`non_combat`, the default, `combat` for in place of an attack, or `any`); an attacking Ally with the capture trait may capture a Seal instead of dealing damage (battle step 11); the Recover step is announced every turn (`recover_step` event) and the card returns only when Combat was skipped.

Not yet: Use-when-needed windows outside the Combat-card counter and the Declare window (no starter card needs one), choosing the order of Defense Shields, of "if successful" effects, or of one's own entering-Combat effects (they resolve in printed or source order), the free Ally takeover when the opponent uses a non-attack card, trying an any-type stop against a Focused attack for its secondary effects, the replacement effects the design lists (draw from the discard pile, keep cards in the Discard step, alternative costs, blank text boxes), and Restricted Drills. A card with both an attack and a utility mode can only attack; no starter card has one.

## AI

One AI plays every deck. Nothing in `ai/` names a card or a deck; it reads card data and table state.

- **`AiProfile`** is the data: weights for the AI's own side (`own`), the opponent's (`foe`), effect ops (`effect`), play costs (`play`), and how hard it thinks (`think`: `search`, `top_k`, `samples`, `budget_ms`, `max_steps`, `turns`, `noise`, `prior`). Defaults live in `AiProfile.DEFAULTS`; a JSON file under `data/ai/profiles/` overrides only what it lists. A deck's playstyle is a profile: raise `own.seal` for a Seal deck, `own.ascension` for an Ascension deck, `own.ally` for an Ally deck.
- **`AiEvaluator.evaluate(engine, seat, profile)`** scores a position. Each win route is its own term: Life Decks (survival), Fervor and aspects (Ascension), Seals of one set (Seal win), plus Energy, Might band, hand, Allies, Drills, Non-Combats, attachments and forbids. The opponent's side is scored with `foe` weights, so a beatdown AI still fears six Seals. A Drill that guards Seals turns capture off outright, so it scores above a plain Drill on its owner's side and as a target on the other (`own.seal_guard`, `foe.seal_guard`); `AiScorer.hold_value` gives it the same edge in hand.
- **Grounds** bind both players, so `AiEvaluator.grounds_value` scores them by whose cards they help and hinder: the AI's whole deck against what the opponent has shown in play, discard and removed piles. It reads the Grounds' data (forbids, doubled costs, modifiers, searches), never their id. A Fervor cap binds both players rather than any one card, so it is scored apart from the per-card fit: it is worth the difference between how much the opponent's profile wants to climb and how much ours does. The evaluator counts the Grounds in play, and the scorer rates placing new Grounds as new minus old, less the Combat that placing them gives up (`play.grounds_skip`). Weights: `own.grounds`, `foe.grounds`.
- **`AiReserve`** decides the Reserve swap. It reads the opponent's duelist, Mastery and Relic, the cards both players show before the swap, for signs of an Ally, Drill, Non-Combat or Seal deck and of a deck that sits on its lowest aspect (`read_setup`: what those cards fetch or shield, never what they aim at the opponent). A Reserve card that removes a kind of card comes in when the opponent shows that kind and stays out otherwise; one that hits both sides is charged for my own cards of that kind; one that raises both players' Fervor is for aspect-sitters and costs an Ascension deck its win; a plain strong card comes in when it beats the average Life Deck card, since each swap pushes a random card out. Attacks stay in the Reserve when the main deck has a card that fetches from it, unless the opponent is what they answer. Cards that start in play from the Reserve, Bond cards and cards the duelist cannot use never come in. Profile group `reserve`: `tech`, `threshold`, `toolbox_keep`, `max_swaps` (Tide sets 0). `tests/ai_reserve_report.gd` prints every deck's read and every Reserve card's score against every opponent.
- **`AiScorer`** scores each option of the pending prompt with no simulation, from `attack_forecasts`, `damage_breakdown` and effect ops. It is the Easy AI, the move ordering for the search, and the policy both seats follow inside a playout. An option type it does not know scores 0, the same as "do nothing".
- **`AiSearch`** is the lookahead. For each of the scorer's top `top_k` options (plus one "do nothing" option) it asks for a simulated engine, plays the option, lets the scorer play both seats until the turn ends, and evaluates. `think.turns` above 1 plays on through that many further turns, for a plan that only pays off after the opponent answers; it costs roughly one extra turn's worth of playout each time, so under a fixed `budget_ms` it buys depth with `samples`. It averages over `samples` deals and stops at `budget_ms`. Every option meets the same deals. `last_report` lists what it weighed.
- **`AiPlayer.choose(referee, seat)`** returns the wire form of one of the prompt's options, or `{}` when the decision is not that seat's.
- **Fair play.** The AI never holds the real engine. `Referee.sim_for(seat, sample_seed)` returns `DuelEngine.clone()` after `determinize(seat, seed)`: every card the seat cannot see trades identity at random with the owner's other hidden cards, and the random stream is replaced. The seat's `SeatView` of the simulation equals its view of the real duel; a test checks that. The AI does not yet remember cards it saw earlier (a searched card, a revealed hand).
- **Cost.** A clone plus determinize is about 1.5 ms, a submit about 0.25 ms. Normal averages about 100 ms per decision with a 400 ms cap, and about 290 ms for the four decks that set `think.turns` to 3. The cap is checked between sample sweeps, not inside one, so a decision can overrun it by a sweep; 671 ms is the worst seen. In the client the search runs on a `WorkerThreadPool` task so tweens keep playing.

## Client

- `duel_view.gd` renders one seat's `SeatView` and nothing else. Hotseat and hosting keep a `Referee` in the scene; after every command it plays the seat's `SeatUpdate` into the log and into table tweens, then shows the next prompt. A `Card3D` exists per uid the view lists, with a face only when the view carries a definition. Any card decision can be made by clicking the card (in the 2D hand or on the table) or by the button list in the prompt panel, which always lists every legal option. `DuelHud.BUTTON_KINDS` forces a kind's options into the panel buttons even when they name a card, for a yes-or-no about a card that is already in a pile (Endurance); without it those options fall out of both the button list and the tray and the prompt has no visible answer.

- The focus view (`Root/Focus` in `hud.tscn`, `DuelHud.show_focus`) holds the card a decision is about at readable size in the middle of the screen, captioned by what it is ("Incoming", "The card you just lost", "Asking"): the attack in the air, a prompt's `context.card`, or the card whose effect raised the prompt. Both seats see it, the one deciding and the one waiting, it takes no mouse input so the table underneath stays clickable, and it steps aside for the tray and the inspect overlay.
- Combat beats: `_play_update` replays each event line that carries `data` as its own beat (`_replay`) against the update's final layout before the closing sync. An attack raises its card to the Play slot, lunges the attacker's personality at the defender's with a streak and sparks (`duel_fx.gd`, the `Fx` node: floating text, bursts, slashes, rings, all primitive meshes freed when their tween ends), and shows a banner (`DuelHud.toast`) naming it; a defense rings the defender in blue; "Hits for 8 Energy, 1 wound" banners the total; an Energy hit shakes and flashes the target with "-5 Energy" over it; each flipped life card arcs from the Life Deck to the discard face up (`_fly`) with "Wound N" and a banner naming what it cost, held half a second so a run of wounds reads one at a time; Endurance used and Endurance declined both banner their outcome, so the attacker sees the answer to a choice it watched being offered; Seal capture, aspect changes, Energy and Fervor changes all get their number over the card. A card about to resolve holds the table first (`_spotlight`: it lifts, rings gold and names itself), and an effect that lands elsewhere pulses its source card, so a chain of effects reads as a sequence of cards acting rather than the table changing by itself. Discards and removals arc to their pile as their event plays instead of sliding in the sync. While an attack is in the air the two personalities in control carry a role glow under the card (attacker red, defender blue, `Card3D.set_role`; `Body` under the card holds the quads so shakes and lunges never fight the table tween). `Engine.time_scale` (`--dev-fast`) speeds every beat.
- `table_layout.gd` turns zone names into table slots. Each zone owns its own rectangle on the felt (two rows per side: Mastery, Allies, Duelist, Seals, Life Deck, Discard; then Relic with its Reserve, Drills, the card in play, Non-Combats, Removed; Grounds across the centre). The outlines and labels on the table are drawn from the same markers and slot counts. Rows squeeze past their slot count instead of spilling into a neighbour. Player 1's slots mirror player 0's; every card and label turns to read upright for the current viewer.
- `card_face_cache.gd` draws each card face once into a `SubViewport` and caches the texture. Faces are procedural (`card_face.tscn`, 512 x 716) in two layouts. Duelists and Allies get a portrait: aspect box and name across the top, art down the left, the ten-stage Might ladder down the right with the Surge badge under it, and the aspect's power text along the bottom. Every other card gets title, type chip, an art box of a fixed height for its type (`CardFace.ART_HEIGHTS`: 320 px for Strikes, Arts and Seals, 300 for Combat, 240 for Non-Combats, Drills and Grounds, 220 for Masteries, 200 for Relics), an Energy cost badge over the art, the rules text in the fixed box that remains, and Endurance under it. Boxes never move between cards of one type; the text steps its font down from 24 to 12 px to fit. Art comes from `assets/card_art/<id>.png` when it exists, covered and cropped to the box.
- Rules text comes from `CardText` in the engine (`rules_text` for cards, `aspect_text` for a personality aspect with its Power, Constant powers and Defense Shield). Effects that share a trigger and condition fold into one sentence; a forbid or board clear that hits both players reads once. `CardText.KEYWORDS` lists every keyword with a regex, a colour role and a tooltip; `scripts/ui/keyword_text.gd` turns plain text into BBCode with those colours (ink on the cream face, light on the dark HUD) and `keyword_label.gd` is the `RichTextLabel` that shows the tooltip on hover. Shorthand on the face: `Hit:` is "if the attack is successful", `Remain N` is "stays in play for N more uses this Combat"; each is a keyword with its own tooltip. Hovering any card (hand, tray, or table) shows a quick view of its face at about half size beside the pointer; it follows the mouse and never takes clicks. Right-click any card or press Inspect in a card's action choice to bring it up in the centre at full size over a dimmed table; that face is live, so hovering a keyword on it explains it. Esc or a click outside closes it.
- The table camera (`table_camera.gd` on the duel scene's Camera) moves without changing its angle: the mouse wheel dollies toward the spot under the pointer, middle-drag or WASD / arrow keys pan along the table, and the look point is kept on the table by a margin that grows with height, so a zoomed-out view stays centred while a close view can roam to the edges. After four seconds without any input it glides back to the home framing; hand-offs and the hotseat swing send it home at once. Key panning is ignored while a text field has focus.
- Duelist numbers live on the duelist card, not in the HUD. `status_markers.gd` sits under each personality `Card3D`: a pulsing bar over the Might ladder rung for the current Energy stage (one step below the ladder, in the warning colour, at 0), and one Fervor pip per point the duelist currently needs along its top edge (the row is rebuilt when `fervor_needed` changes). Rung positions come from the face layout (`CardFace.ladder_rects`, cached by `CardFaceCache`). The player panels show only seat data: Life Deck, hand, discard, removed, Reserve, Seals, and standing flags, including any effective value that differs from the printed rules ("Needs 6 Fervor", "Fervor shielded", "No Art attacks"). The quick view and the inspect view are live faces fed the same state through `SeatView.live_energy(uid)` and `SeatView.duelist_owner(uid)`, so a personality in play shows its lit rung, its Fervor pips and its live Recover gain in the Surge badge there too (`CardFace.show_def(def, aspect, energy, standing)`); cached table textures carry none.
- Dev panel (debug builds, hotseat and host only): the Dev button beside the phase strip opens a panel of effect buttons (Fervor, Energy, aspect, draw, wounds, recover, discard, end Combat or turn, and a search by title into hand or play) for the viewer or the rival. Each runs as an engine effect through `Referee.dev` and `DuelEngine.dev_effect`, so triggers fire as they would from a card; the engine then re-issues the pending decision. Refused while a card effect is mid-resolution (choice prompts). Every use is logged as a Dev line.
- The log strip at the top right has a More/Less button that drops it down to most of the screen height; `--dev-log` opens it for screenshots.
- Hotseat: when the prompt's player changes, a hand-off overlay hides the hand, and on confirm the camera swings to that player's side.
- Online: one process is the authority and runs the only `Referee`, behind a `DuelHost`. That is the duel server (one room per share code, both seats remote) or, on the LAN dev path, a hosting client (seat 0 local, seat 1 remote). A client holds no engine and no seed: it sends its choices up as `Command` dictionaries and gets back its seat's `SeatUpdate`, so hidden cards never reach its process. Clients learn their seat from the authority, so the same client code plays seat 0 or 1. Updates travel zstd-packed (about 70 KB raw, 3.5 to 4.7 KB on the wire). Traffic that arrives while the table is still animating waits in a queue. When the other player is deciding, the prompt panel says so without listing their options. Disconnects end the duel with a notice; only the host can call a rematch or return both clients to the lobby.
- What the host can still do: read everything, since the rules run in its process. That is the friends-only limit of P2P; a headless server running the same `Referee` for both seats is the next step, and the client already speaks only views.

Dev flags after `--` when launching `res://scenes/duel/duel.tscn` directly: `--dev-autoplay` (random legal choices), `--dev-steps=N`, `--dev-seed=N`, `--dev-stop-at=<prompt kind>` (stop autoplay at the first prompt of that kind, e.g. `defense`, or `kind:flag` such as `pick_option:may` for prompts whose context sets that flag; add `--dev-click` to open the first hand card's action sub-choice before the shot, the way a real click would, or `--dev-click=final` for the first card whose only action is a Final Strike, or `--dev-zoom` to open the inspect view on the first hand card, `--dev-zoom=duelist` for the viewer's duelist, `--dev-zoom=rival` for the other duelist, `--dev-peek` or `--dev-peek=duelist` for the quick view, `--dev-log` for the dropped-down log), `--dev-pick=A,B` (deck indexes, in file order, for the two players), `--dev-ai` or `--dev-ai=hard` (seat 2 is played by the AI with that profile; autoplay then answers only for seat 1; the select screen takes `--dev-ai` too), `--dev-policy=attack` (autoplay declares Combat, attacks whenever it can, and never defends or endures, so a short run shows damage and a fight back; `--dev-stop-at=attack_action:fight_back` then lands on the defender's answer), `--dev-hover=N` (act as if the pointer were over the Nth prompt-panel button, for a screenshot of the outcome preview), `--dev-freeze=<event type>` (take the screenshot in the middle of that event's combat beat, with its effects still in the air: `attack_declared` for the swing and banner, `damage_stages` for the hit, `life_card_flipped` for a wound), `--dev-fast` (tweens run at eight times speed, so a long autoplay takes seconds), `--dev-camera=dx,dz,notches` (pan the table camera by dx right and dz ahead in table units, then zoom that many wheel notches toward the screen centre, before the shot), `--dev-hide-hud`, `--dev-screenshot=<path.png>`. The select screen (`scenes/select/duelist_select.tscn`) takes `--dev-pick=A,B`, `--dev-lock` (lock each seat as it is picked, so one pick lands on player 2 choosing and two on both locked) and `--dev-screenshot=<path.png>`; left and right arrows move through the roster and Enter locks in. The matchup screen (`scenes/select/versus.tscn`) takes `--dev-pick=A,B`, `--dev-ai`, `--dev-aspect=N` (show player 1's duelist at aspect N, as card clicks would) and `--dev-screenshot=<path.png>`; clicking a duelist's card there cycles through the aspects the deck plays with, and hovering a key card shows it at readable size.

Online without clicks, launching the project (title scene) twice: the first instance with `--dev-host-code` (a room on the duel server, its code printed to stdout as `join code: X`) or `--dev-host` (a plain LAN port 7777 with the rules in that process), the second with `--dev-join=<code>` or `--dev-join=127.0.0.1`; `--dev-net-log` prints each connection stage and update size, `--dev-server=host:port` points at another duel server; both with `--dev-pick=A,B` (each uses only its own seat), `--dev-autoplay` (the host starts as soon as both seats are set), `--dev-steps=N` (counts every command applied, so both stop on the same state) and `--dev-screenshot=<png>`; without `--dev-autoplay` the select screen takes the screenshot instead, showing the players strip and the room code banner. Online, `--dev-seed` and `--dev-pick` on the duel scene are ignored because the lobby already agreed on both.

## Loadouts

Seven starter decks, six of them modelled card-for-card on a community set of sample decks for the reference game, ordered easy to advanced. Counts and roles are kept and each card carries the mechanics of the card it stands in for, written in the effect schema below; every name is original. `data/cards/starter/starter_set.json` and the deck files are generated together by `tools/gen_starters.py` (run it from `zenith/`), so edit the generator rather than the JSON by hand.

Each deck file also carries a `tagline` (one line under the duelist's name on the select screen) and a `blurb` (two or three sentences on who they are and how the deck plays), set in the generator's `DECK_IDENTITY`; the select screen's key card row is the Mastery, the Relic and every card whose title carries a word of the duelist's name.

Each deck file names its **archetype** (`strike_beatdown`, `art_beatdown`, `allies`, `drills`, `seals`, `ascension`, `control`), its `subthemes` (one word each for what the deck leans on: `fervor`, `energy`, `draw`, `might`, `disruption`, `bond`, `arts`, `strikes`, `swords`, `automatons`, `seals`, `allies`, `drills`) and a `difficulty`, the way the sample-deck sheet labels its decks. The vocabulary, labels and one-line plans live in `engine/archetype.gd`; the generator's `DECK_KINDS` sets them per deck and `DeckValidator` rejects a word it does not know. The archetype is public, like the duelist and the Mastery: it rides on `PlayerState` and `SeatPlayer`, shows as a chip and a plan line on the select screen and after the style in the duel's player panel, and `AiReserve.read_setup` takes the signs it declares (an `allies` deck reads as an Ally deck, a `seals` subtheme as a likely Seal deck) on top of what the setup cards show.

## Card JSON

```json
{"id": "pyre_overhand_cut", "title": "Pyre Overhand Cut", "type": "strike", "school": "pyre",
 "endurance": 1,
 "attack": {"kind": "strike", "stages": 2},
 "effects": [{"trigger": "if_successful", "op": "fervor", "amount": 1}]}
```

- `type`: duelist, ally, strike, art, combat, non_combat, drill, seal, grounds, mastery, relic
- `school`: pyre, tide, storm, shade, steel, root, or omitted for Freestyle
- `attack`: `kind` strike | art; `stages`, `life` add to base; `printed_stages`, `printed_life` replace it; `cost_stages` (default 2 for arts), `cost_life`; `focused`, `unstoppable`, `no_prevent`, `no_stop_by` (a card type), `stages_from_table`, `life_per_ally`, `life_per_tag` (a keyword; counts personalities in play on both sides), `life_from_surge`, `only_first_attack`, `pay_stages` `{per, life, stages}`, `variants[]` of `{when, ...overrides, effects}` merged when the condition holds
- `defense`: `{"stops": "strike" | "art" | "any"}` plus optional `when` (condition), `stop_all` (kind), `stop_focused` (true or `"discard_hand"`), `cost_stages`, `cost_life`, `copy_attack`. A card with both `attack` and `defense` attacks in the attack phase and defends in the defense phase; `use_in_attack` lets a pure defense also be used in place of an attack
- `effects[]`: `trigger` secondary (default) | if_successful | if_stopped | before_damage (after a successful attack, before damage; with `skip_damage` the attack then deals none) | turn_start (`each_turn: true` to fire on both players' turns rather than its owner's only) | on_place (a Seal's trigger-less lines count as on_place: they resolve as it enters play, and a captor may run them on capture) | use | relic_use | on_success (Mastery) | entering_combat (optional `role`) | on_wound (this card left the Life Deck as a wound or cost; `at: "fight_back"` delays it to the next fight-back phase of the Combat) | opponent_declare (a Non-Combat in play, offered to its owner during the opponent's Declare step); `who` self | opponent; `when` (condition); `may` asks the owner first; `then` is a list of effects that run right after this one (only on a yes for `may`, once per unit paid for pay_energy); `after_empower` drops the line when Empowered. Ops: fervor, set_fervor, energy (amount or `"max"`, `target` duelist, `no_overflow`), set_energy (`target` duelist | in_control | all), draw, draw_until, draw_discard (`from`), draw_check (school, effects), discard_life, discard_hand (`random`, `chooser` owner, `to` deck, `filter` signature | non_seal), remove_hand, pay_energy (`per`, with `then`), look_at (`from` top | bottom, `amount`, `pick` filter, `to`, `play_if` filter for a card that may go into play instead, `shuffle_after`), search (`card_type`, `school`, `title_contains`, `exclude_title`, `tag`, `signature_of` duelist, `source` deck | discard | either | reserve, `to` hand | play | deck_top | deck_bottom | deck_shuffle | attack, `stages` (a number, or `"max"` for full Energy), `amount`, `has_effect` `{op, who}` to match by what a card does; the searcher picks when several distinct cards match, `choose: false` takes the first), choose_forbid_type (`unless_energy_min`), return_removed (`card_type`), focus_attack (the current attack becomes Focused), end_turn, force_declare, bond (`card`: the Bond Ally id, found anywhere in the owner's zones), discard_in_play (`card_type` non_combat | non_combat_only | drill | freestyle_drill | ally | seal | non_combat_or_ally | drill_or_ally, `amount`, `all`, `remove`, `choose`, `up_to`, `chooser`), remove_discard (`all`), shuffle_discard (`per_personality`), recover, end_combat, skip_next_attack_phase, cannot_declare_combat, stop_all, choose_stop_all_kind, float (`what`, `duration` combat | turn | next_turn_end, `params`), forbid (`what`, `duration`), lose_aspect, advance_aspect, set_aspect (`aspect` or `"fervor"`), no_ascension_win, attach (`to`), capture_seal, name_card, next_attack_tax, mark_used (spends a `once_per_combat` when taken rather than when offered; put it in a `then`)
- Conditions (`when`): character, duelist_character, alignment, performed_by, aspect_min, energy_min, opponent_fervor, allies_min, ally_present, opponent_allies_min, opponent_non_combats_min, discard_top_school, discard_top_school_not, discard_top2_school, discard_min, higher_might, opponent_used_combat_card, first_attack, role, seals_min, hand_min, stopped_last_phase, source_school, attack_focused, performer_tag, in_control_tag, duelist_tag, card_in_play (by title, either side). A Non-Combat in play may carry `use_at: "ascension_win"`, which takes it out of the normal Combat use list and offers it instead when the opponent would win by Ascension. A Mastery's `on_attack` effects are offered whenever its owner declares an attack, and its `blocks_to_bottom: <school>` key sends a used block of that school under the Life Deck when the block stopped the attack and does not remove itself
- `energy` accepts `target` duelist | last_searched (the card the last search put into play); A `search` whose `source` is the deck (or `either`) is a look through the Life Deck: the searcher is always prompted, even with one match or none, the prompt's `library` context lists the whole deck by title and `SeatView` reveals those cards to the searcher alone, `pick_none` takes nothing, and the deck is shuffled once when the search ends (event `deck_shuffled`) unless the effect sets `no_shuffle`. A `look_at` shuffles only with `shuffle_after`. `draw_check` takes `check` school | named | signature | title_contains, `discard: true` to check a life card thrown away instead of one drawn, and `else_effects` for a miss
- Floating `what`: no_prevent, prevent_all, make_focused (school), after_use_bottom (school), damage_removes, no_gain, no_ally_control, keep_hand, endurance_boost, no_endurance, stop_next, prevent_art_life, modifier (a modifier dict, `once` for a single attack)
- Forbid `what`: strike_attacks, art_attacks, strike_cards, art_cards, combat_cards, non_combats, drills, mastery, powers, end_combat, stop_all, seals, non_attack_actions, skip_combat, allies. Printed `forbid[]` lists work on Grounds, Drills, and Non-Combats
- `modifiers[]` on drills, masteries, grounds and constants: `scope` own | against, `kind` strike | art | any, `stages`, `life`, optional `school`, `title_contains`, `when`, `per_ally`
- `forbid[]` on grounds and drills: `{"who": "all" | "owner" | "opponent", "what": ...}`; `shield` on drills: strike | art | any; `attachment`: `{target, modifiers, effects, damage_removes, title_contains}`
- Keywords: `endurance`, `endurance_when` `{value_if, then, else}`, `empower`, `remain`, `remain_when` `{when, remain}`, `unused_return` `"shuffle"`, `counter` `"combat"`, `start_in_play` (from the Life Deck or the Reserve), `only` `{character | duelist_character}`, `bottom_after_use`, `remove_after_use`, `once_per_combat`, `tags[]`; on Drills also `hand_keep` (raises the end-of-turn hand limit), `promote_if_successful` (title fragment) and `discard_if_other_non_combats`
- Attack extras: `stops_needed` (the defender must stop it that many times), `life_per_opponent_seal`. Constants also take `no_modifiers_against` strike | art | any, which blanks the attacker's own modifiers on attacks of that kind aimed at the personality whose card carries it
- Personalities: `character`, `aspects[]` with `aspect`, `surge`, `might` (11 ints for stages 0 to 10), optional `wild`, `shield`, `power` (`attack`, `defense`, `effects`, `uses`, `no_control_needed` on Allies), `constant`; a Bond Ally adds `bond_of` (two Ally characters) and `bond_timer_max`, and can never be placed directly (`attacks_focused`, `first_styled_unstoppable`, `damage_removes`, `protect_allies`, `ally_control_any_stage`, `allies_share`, `fervor_multiplier`, `forbid_opponent[]`, `modifiers[]`, `on_attack[]`, `turn_start[]`, `entering_combat[]`)
- Masteries: `art_cost_delta`, `opponent_aspect_threshold`, `protect_drills`; Relics: `reserve_size`, `uses_per_game`, `relic_step` (`non_combat` by default, `combat`, `any`), `relic_flags` (`no_ascension_win`, `fervor_shield`, `aspect_shield`); Grounds: `double_costs`
- `text` overrides the generated rules text; `alignment_only`, `limit_per_deck`, `seal_set`, `seal_number`, `capture_trait`
- Discard pile and Seal extras (The Thornwald Grove): `draw_discard` takes `if_school` plus `effects` (run when the last card drawn is of that school); a `may` effect takes `otherwise[]` (run on a no, for "do X or do Y"); `search` takes `source` hand, `to` deck_bottom | deck_shuffle, `card_type` non_combat_any (Non-Combat, Drill or Seal) and `amount_per_set_seal` (one card per Seal of that set in play); `shuffle_discard` takes `from` top_and_bottom; attacks take `life_per_set_seal` (a set name); conditions `attack_kind` and `opponent_seals_min`; a Non-Combat in play may carry an `on_success` effect and spends itself with a `spend_source` line; Drills take `protect_seals`; Grounds take `fervor_gain_cap`; `discard_if_seal` (a Seal id) turns `remove_after_use` into a discard while that Seal is in play

Deck JSON: `name`, `duelist`, `aspects`, `style`, `alignment`, `mastery`, `relic`, `reserve[]`, `cards[]` of `{"id", "count"}`.
