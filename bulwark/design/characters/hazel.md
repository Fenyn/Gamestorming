# Hazel

- **id:** `hazel`
- **Class:** Thaumaturge
- **Ancestry:** Halfling
- **Pronouns:** she/her
- **Kind:** recruitable PC
- **Associated building:** Reliquary
- **Deity:** None. Hazel has spent her career studying the supernatural with the detachment
  of a scientist. She respects what the gods can do the way she respects what a venomous snake
  can do: from a measured distance, with good notes.

## Vibe

Hazel is the kind of person who picks up a cursed amulet with a pair of tongs, holds it up to
the light, and says "oh, that's a good one" with genuine warmth. She is meticulous about
handling, precise about cataloguing, reverential about preservation, and absolutely delighted
by things that would make most people leave the room.

She is older, and the age sits on her comfortably. She has been doing this for a long time and
she is very good at it and she knows it without needing to say so. Her enthusiasm is not the
breathless kind that young people have. It is the deep, steady kind that comes from decades of
sustained fascination. She has seen a thousand strange things and every new one still makes her
eyes light up. That kind of wonder does not survive without being genuine.

She is warm and patient with people who bring her things. She does not condescend. If someone
hands her a rock and asks if it is magical, she examines it thoroughly and tells them what it
is, even if it is just a rock. If someone brings her a monster tooth, she wants to know where
they found it, what the creature looked like, how it moved, what it smelled like. Every detail
matters. Every detail goes in the record.

Underneath the warmth is a quiet anger she does not talk about often. She has learned what
happens when a collection is not protected. She has learned that the work of preservation is
invisible to the people who benefit from it, and that invisible work is the first thing cut
when someone with money decides the building is worth more than what is inside it. She is
meticulous about cataloguing because she knows that sometimes the records are the only thing
that survives.

## Role in the town

Hazel runs the Reliquary. The building serves two functions:

**Museum.** The player donates artifacts, relics, and curiosities found during exploration and
combat. Hazel catalogues and displays them. Filling display cases and reaching collection
milestones unlocks rewards. The Reliquary grows visually as the collection expands, shelves
filling, cases lighting up, the space transforming from an empty room into something that
looks like it matters.

**Bestiary.** As the player encounters and defeats enemies, Hazel adds entries to a bestiary
that tracks creature types, weaknesses, resistances, and behavioral notes. The bestiary is a
living document that grows with the player's experience. Completing entries for a creature
type provides combat bonuses against that type on future encounters. Hazel's thaumaturge
expertise is what turns raw field observations into actionable intelligence.

As the Reliquary upgrades, the depth of identification Hazel can provide improves, and the
museum and bestiary systems expand in scope.

In combat, Hazel exploits weaknesses. The thaumaturge class revolves around identifying what
a creature is and using that knowledge to hit harder. She is not physically imposing. She does
not need to be. She knows where every creature is vulnerable, and she carries the right
implement for every occasion.

## Background

Hazel spent most of her career at a museum of supernatural history in one of the hegemony's
larger cities. She was not the director. She was the curator, the person who actually handled
the collection, catalogued new acquisitions, maintained preservation standards, and could tell
you the provenance of any artifact in the building from memory. The museum was small, poorly
funded, and largely ignored by the public. Hazel did not mind. The work was the point.

The building was purchased by investors who saw a property opportunity. The collection was
assessed for resale value. Items with aesthetic appeal were sold to private buyers who wanted
conversation pieces for their studies. Items without obvious value were boxed, warehoused,
and eventually discarded. Decades of careful curation, scattered or destroyed because someone
decided the building was worth more than what was inside it.

Hazel could not stop it. She tried. She argued, petitioned, appealed to institutions that
declined to intervene. In the end she took what she could carry, which was not enough, and
she left. She does not describe what happened as an injustice. She describes it as a tragedy,
the kind that has no villain, just a system that does not value what it cannot price.

She came to the frontier because the frontier produces things that no one has catalogued yet.
Every ruin explored, every creature encountered, every strange phenomenon the outpost stumbles
into is material for a new collection. The Reliquary is Hazel rebuilding what was taken from
her, one artifact at a time. Every display case filled is proof that the work was worth doing.
Every bestiary entry completed is a record that cannot be bought, sold, or thrown away.

## Arrival

Recruitable. Hazel arrives at the outpost after hearing about the growing settlement and the
things its people have been encountering in the surrounding territory. She shows up with a
careful inventory of what she saved from the old museum, a set of professional tools, and a
proposal: give her a space, bring her what you find, and she will build something that lasts.
The trigger is a trophy-count threshold: once the party has accumulated a collection's worth of
monster trophies and rare drops, scattered and uncatalogued, there is enough material sitting
around to be worth a curator showing up for (see `design/economy/characters.md` for the proposed
number).
