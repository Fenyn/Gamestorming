# Goblin revision research

Sources consulted:

- [Derek Yu: Pixel Art Basics](https://www.derekyu.com/makegames/pixelart.html): build solid forms, use coherent lighting, clean contour steps, and selective outlines.
- [Derek Yu: Common Mistakes](https://www.derekyu.com/makegames/pixelart2.html): reduce redundant colors, avoid pillow shading, and give limbs enough thickness to describe volume.
- [SLYNYRD: Human Anatomy](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2019/5/21/pixelblog-17-human-anatomy): establish wireframe and proportions before surface detail.
- [SLYNYRD: Developing Style](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/7/14/pixelblog-7-developing-style): resolution, perspective, palette, and cluster choices jointly define style.

Application to this asset: one right-facing side view throughout the head, shoulder, ribcage, pelvis, and feet. Far limbs overlap and stay darker. Use compact goblin proportions with bent knees, a short neck, a large pointed ear, and a readable bare hand for future equipment. Check the silhouette and the rat at the same native pixel scale.

The built-in image generation tool supplied `construction-reference.png` as a visual construction reference. Final game-resolution pixel work and layered exports are made in Aseprite.

Following the user's style correction, keep that pose but discard its textured shading. Repaint into broad flat skin and shadow regions, a tiny crown highlight, and sparse dark contact details. Keep face details small and high contrast. The rat is the style reference; the generated image supplies construction only.

Edge refinement: the rat uses a short light contour across its back and dark overlap shapes around the legs and underside. Apply this selectively to the goblin's upper head and shoulder contours, jaw/neck overlap, near upper arm, and far limbs. Keep the silhouette and palette fixed and avoid outlining the entire figure.
